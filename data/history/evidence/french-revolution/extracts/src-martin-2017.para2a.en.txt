His partisan positions discredited these figures, put forward without proof. But his successors bring no more argument to their evaluations. The nineteenth-century jurist Berriat-Saint-Prix records 14 807 judicially condemned to death, before the American historian Donald Greer estimates, in a small work published in 1935, The Incidence of the Terror, at 16 594 the individuals tried and executed; at between 10 000 and 12 000 the deaths in prison; and at a similar figure those killed without trial, in particular in the fighting in the West.
