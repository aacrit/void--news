Paris, 22 brumaire year X (13 November 1801). That I cannot delay the Saint-Domingue expedition by a single day, because after the month of germinal the climate is impracticable for Europeans; That, if I were obliged to put the expedition off to another year, I would be obliged to recognise Toussaint, to renounce Saint-Domingue, and to constitute French blacks there, which would doubtless not be a money-making venture but would be very advantageous to the Republic from the point of view of military power; That, in the course I have taken of annihilating the government of the blacks at Saint-Domingue, I have been guided less by considerations of commerce and finance than by the necessity of stifling, in every part of the world, every kind of seed of unrest and trouble; but that it could not escape me that Saint-Domingue, reconquered by the whites, would for many years be a weak point needing the support of peace and of the mother country; That the liberty of the blacks, recognised at Saint-Domingue and legitimised by the Government, would at all times be a point of support for the Republic in the new world;
