In historical scholarship it is disputed to this day how high the war losses of the Herero actually turned out. This is above all because no exact figures on the size of the Herero people survive either for the time before the war or for the time after it. For this reason all attempts at a quantitative determination of the war's victims are to be taken with great caution. Thus the estimates for the pre-war period rest on unverified statements by missionaries that are very doubtful in their reliability; the post-war figures are mostly based on counts of prisoners by the German military administration. Given the deficient transmission of the relevant data it is little wonder that the range of the data presented in each case is very great. Thus the figures for the whole Herero population before the war fluctuate between 35,000 and 100,000 persons, the post-war population is put at 14,000 to 25,000 persons, which, taking the respective extreme values into account, amounts to loss rates of between 29 and 86 per cent. Further uncertainties about the quantitative relations arise from the fact that since the end of the 19th century several larger epidemics have raged among the Herero with many victims, without it being possible to establish with certainty whether this state of affairs has been taken into account in the figures for the total population.
