Gentlemen, we must speak louder and truer! We must say openly that indeed the superior races have a right with regard to the inferior races... (Murmurs on several benches of the far left.)
M. Jules Maigne. Oh! you dare to say that in the country where the rights of man were proclaimed!
M. de Guilloutet. It is the justification of slavery and of the trade in negroes!
M. Jules Ferry. If the honourable M. Maigne is right, if the declaration of the rights of man was written for the blacks of equatorial Africa, then by what right are you going to impose exchanges and trade on them? They do not call for you! (Interruptions on the far left and on the right. Very good! very good! on various benches of the left.)
M. Raoul Duval. We do not want to impose them on them! It is you who impose them!
M. Jules Maigne. To propose and to impose are very different things!
M. Georges Périn. You cannot, however, make forced exchanges!
M. Jules Ferry. I repeat that there is for the superior races a right, because there is a duty for them. They have the duty to civilise the inferior races... (Marks of approval on the same benches of the left. New interruptions on the far left and on the right.)
