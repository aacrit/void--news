## OPEN
N: Monuments are built by winners and they all say the same thing. I won. Here is the enemy under my feet. Here is the number of them.
N: There is one that gives the number and then says the wrong thing afterwards.
N: It was cut into rock faces across a subcontinent, in the reigning emperor's own voice, and as far as anybody has found, it is the first time in recorded history that a ruler used a public monument to say that a war he had won should not have been fought.

## TITLE
N: Ashoka and the Maurya empire. The third century before the common era.

## SCENE 1 | Kalinga
N: Around two hundred and sixty one before the common era, the Mauryan army moves into Kalinga, a kingdom on the eastern coast of India, roughly where Odisha is now.
N: It is the largest campaign of the dynasty and it succeeds completely.
N: A hundred thousand people are killed. A hundred and fifty thousand are deported.
N: Those figures are not a later historian's estimate, and they are not an enemy's accusation. We have them because the emperor had them carved.

## DOCUMENT | Ashoka | Rock Edict XIII | c. 256 BCE
N: This is Ashoka, on stone, in the thirteenth of the major rock edicts.
M: A hundred and fifty thousand people were deported, a hundred thousand were killed, and many times that number perished. After the Kalingas had been conquered, His Sacred Majesty felt remorse for having conquered the Kalingas.

## ASIDE
N: Read who is being counted. The edict lists brahmins, ascetics and householders among the dead and the deported.
N: In his own telling, this is not two armies meeting. It is what happened to a population, and he put the arithmetic of it in public, in the middle of his own empire, where any traveller could stop and read it.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The machine he inherited
N: The empire was two generations old. His grandfather Chandragupta had assembled it, and at its height it ran from Afghanistan to Bengal and from the Himalayas down into the Deccan, governing something like fifty million people.
N: It was not assembled by persuasion. The manual for it survives.
N: The Arthashastra, attributed to the minister Kautilya, is a treatise on running a state, and it is entirely unsentimental. Networks of informers. Ministers tested with deliberate provocations to see who is loyal. Assassination treated as an instrument of policy, costed and compared against war.

## DOCUMENT | Kautilya | Arthashastra | c. 300 BCE
N: Kautilya also wrote this, which is the same coldness pointed at the king himself.
M: A king who has gained a victory should not be intoxicated. Intoxication at victory is the worst of defeats.

## SCENE 3 | What he did with it
N: After Kalinga, Ashoka announces dhamma. A moral law, turning on non violence, on welfare, and on tolerance between the religions in his territory.
N: And he does not just announce it. He builds administration for it. Officers whose job is the moral conduct of the state. Wells dug, trees planted along roads, medical provision extended, by his own account, beyond his borders.
N: He sends Buddhist missions south to Sri Lanka, north into Central Asia, and west to the Greek kingdoms of the Mediterranean.
N: And he publishes. Rock faces and polished stone pillars across the empire, in Prakrit for most of it, in Greek and Aramaic in the northwest, because in the northwest that is what people read.

## DOCUMENT | Ashoka | Rock Edict XII | c. 256 BCE
N: This is Ashoka again, on religion, in the twelfth edict.
M: Whoever praises his own religion, due to excessive devotion, and condemns others with the thought, let me glorify my own religion, only damages his own religion. Therefore contact between religions is good.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of a man who left an unusual amount of evidence about himself.

## PERSPECTIVE | Ashoka's own account | victor
N: The first is the edicts, which is to say the version he wanted.
N: It has a clean shape. Violence, recognition, renunciation. The king wins the war, understands what he has done, and changes the basis of his rule. One of the pillar edicts says that the sound of the war drum has become the sound of dhamma.
N: And the administrative record backs a good deal of it. The morality officers existed. The missions went. The infrastructure is described in specific, checkable terms rather than in praise.
N: It is also the only ancient statement of its kind, which is why it has held for two thousand years.

## PERSPECTIVE | Kalinga | vanquished
N: The second account does not exist, and that absence is the account.
N: No Kalingan text survives from the conquest. Nothing tells us what the people there thought when the army arrived, what the hundred and fifty thousand deportees walked into, or how the dead were mourned.
N: The conqueror had stone and the conquered did not, or theirs was not kept.
N: So the only testimony about what happened to Kalinga is the testimony of the man who did it, and the reason we take it seriously is that it is a confession rather than a boast.
N: And there is a thing the edict never says. He does not give Kalinga back.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | The Buddhist tradition | bystander
N: The third account comes from the religion he sponsored, and it makes him a second founder.
N: The chronicle written in Sri Lanka centuries later has him convening a council, purifying the order, and sending his own son as the missionary who converts the Sri Lankan king. His daughter follows, carrying a cutting from the tree the Buddha sat under.
N: That cutting was planted at Anuradhapura. A tree is still growing on the spot, which makes it one of the oldest continuously tended trees anybody can point to.
N: What this account gives is the scale of the consequence. A regional Indian teaching became a religion across Asia, and the decisive push came from a state.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Ambedkarite reading | revisionist
N: The fourth account is modern, political, and completely open about it.
N: On the fourteenth of October nineteen fifty six, in Nagpur, Bhimrao Ambedkar, the chief architect of the Indian constitution and the leader of the Dalit movement, converted to Buddhism, and about six hundred thousand people converted with him that day.
N: He had announced the intention twenty one years earlier, saying that he had been born a Hindu but would not die one.
N: And when he chose where to go, his reference was Ashoka. His argument was that the Buddha's teaching had been a social revolution against caste, that the Mauryan state had made it the framework of a subcontinent, and that both had then been pushed under by a brahmanical order that wrote the history afterwards.
N: On that reading Ashoka is not a curiosity from the third century before Christ. He is the precedent for an escape route out of the caste system, and six hundred thousand people took it in an afternoon.

## PERSPECTIVE | The southern kingdoms | bystander
N: The fifth account is about where the empire stopped, and you can read it in what the edicts decline to claim.
N: Rock Edict Two names the southern kingdoms precisely. The Cholas, the Pandyas, and two others. Ashoka says he has provided medical treatment there, for people and for animals.
N: He does not say he has conquered them, and he is not shy elsewhere.
N: The same edict that counts the Kalinga dead says his dhamma has reached as far as the Greek king six hundred leagues away, which is a claim about influence, not about territory.
N: So the southern kingdoms appear in the imperial record as neighbours who were sent doctors. The empire that governed fifty million people had a southern edge, and they were on the other side of it.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: And what each of them will not say.
N: The emperor's account is still a monument. It is remorse that was carved by a state, at state expense, in the winner's language, and it does not return anything. He remained the ruler of the territory the war had taken.
N: The Kalingan account is an argument from silence, and it says so. The only evidence it can use is the deposition of the man responsible.
N: The Buddhist account was written down centuries later in a different country by people with a stake in the outcome, and it needs the conversion to be complete. A king running an empire on espionage does not fit the chronicle, so the chronicle leaves it out.
N: The Ambedkarite account is using him. It says so plainly, which is more than most readings of the past manage, and it is still a use. It wants a precedent, and it selects for one.
N: And the southern account is entirely inference. It is built from what an imperial text chose not to boast about, which is a real method and a thin one.

## REST

## CLOSE
N: The treatise on informers and assassination was lost for most of two thousand years. A manuscript surfaced in southern India in nineteen oh five, which means that for centuries the edicts were the only surviving voice of that state, and the other half of the machinery was missing from the picture.
N: When India became a republic it took its national emblem from the lions carved on top of one of Ashoka's pillars, and put the wheel from the same capital in the middle of the flag.
N: The missions he sent south outlasted his own dynasty by more than two thousand years. The form of Buddhism they carried is still the majority religion of Sri Lanka.
N: The rock is still on the cliff face. You can walk up to it and read the number.
