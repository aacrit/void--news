## OPEN
N: Ask most people what happened at Bandung in nineteen fifty five, and they will tell you this is where the Non-Aligned Movement was founded.
N: It was not. The Non-Aligned Movement did not exist yet. It would not be founded for another six years, at a different conference, in a different city, by a different roster of governments.
N: What happened at Bandung was smaller and stranger. Twenty nine governments who agreed on almost nothing else, meeting in a room no Western power had organized and no Western power had been invited into.

## TITLE
N: The Bandung Conference. April eighteenth to the twenty fourth, nineteen fifty five.

## SCENE 1 | Gedung Merdeka, the morning of April 18
N: The morning of April eighteenth, nineteen fifty five. Sukarno walks to the podium inside Gedung Merdeka, the Freedom Building, in Bandung, Indonesia, wearing a white suit.
N: Behind him sit delegates from twenty nine countries. Outside, the streets of Bandung are lined with banners in dozens of languages.
N: India sent Jawaharlal Nehru. Egypt sent Gamal Abdel Nasser. China sent Zhou Enlai. Pakistan sent Muhammad Ali Bogra.
N: Sukarno does not open with pleasantries.

## DOCUMENT | Sukarno | Opening address to the Asian-African Conference, Bandung | April 18, 1955
N: Sukarno told the delegates what he thought of the claim that colonialism was finished.
M: We are often told, Colonialism is dead. Let us not be deceived or even soothed by that. I say to you, colonialism is not yet dead. How can we say it is dead, so long as vast areas of Asia and Africa are unfree?

## ASIDE
N: He was speaking to a room that proved his own point. North and South Vietnam both sat in it, two delegations from a country the Geneva Accords had split eleven months earlier. Turkey, a member of NATO, sat beside communist China.
N: Five delegations came from sub Saharan Africa. Ethiopia, Liberia, Libya, Sudan, and the Gold Coast, which was not yet Ghana. Japan sat in the room too, the only former colonial power present.
N: Of the twenty nine delegations, six had been independent before nineteen forty five. The rest had won their own sovereignty within the previous decade.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The bomb and the diplomat
N: One week before Sukarno spoke, on April eleventh, a bomb went off aboard the Kashmir Princess, a chartered Air India plane carrying the Chinese delegation's advance team from Hong Kong toward Jakarta.
N: The plane crashed into the South China Sea. Eleven people died. Three survived.
N: Zhou Enlai had been scheduled to board that flight. He changed his travel plans at the last moment and flew instead by way of Rangoon.
N: He came to Bandung anyway, leading a state that fewer than half the delegations in the room recognized.

## DOCUMENT | Zhou Enlai | Supplementary speech to the Asian-African Conference, Bandung | April 19, 1955
N: The next day, Zhou Enlai took the floor and told the conference what he said China wanted.
M: The people of Asia and Africa have no aggressive intent. All they want is to live in peace and be friends with all countries of the world.

## ASIDE
N: The bomb was attributed to Kuomintang agents operating from Taiwan. Zhou arrived and spoke anyway, three years after China had fought the United States in Korea.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | The Ten Principles
N: On April twenty fourth, the delegates adopted the Dasasila Bandung, the Ten Principles: sovereignty, non-aggression, non-interference in internal affairs, equality of all races and nations, peaceful coexistence.
N: The principles were not new that week. They drew on the Panchsheel Agreement, a nineteen fifty four treaty between India and China that Zhou Enlai and Jawaharlal Nehru had drafted to settle their own border dispute over Tibet.
N: At Bandung, a bilateral agreement between two governments became a multilateral framework for twenty nine.

## DOCUMENT | Jawaharlal Nehru | Address to the plenary session of the Asian-African Conference | April 22, 1955
N: Before the vote, Nehru told the plenary session what he wanted the conference to refuse.
M: We do not wish to be used as pawns in power politics. We do not wish to be the playthings of others.

## ASIDE
N: The principles carried no enforceable mechanism. No court, no army, no budget. What they carried was the signature of governments representing one point five four billion people, fifty four percent of the world's population that year.

## REST

## SCENE 4 | Washington and Moscow
N: Secretary of State John Foster Dulles had tried to stop the conference before it happened, pressuring American allies not to attend. Turkey, Pakistan, and the Philippines came anyway.
N: Moscow called Bandung a front for the CIA. The CIA's own internal assessment said the opposite. It classified the conference as a genuine threat to Western influence.
N: Both capitals had reason to worry. Twenty nine nations refusing to choose a side threatened the alliance system Washington had built across Asia, and the bloc discipline Moscow depended on.

## SCENE 5 | After Bandung
N: Within five years of the conference, Ghana became independent, in nineteen fifty seven. The All-African People's Conference met in Accra the following year. Seventeen African nations gained independence in nineteen sixty alone.
N: The Non-Aligned Movement was formally founded at Belgrade in nineteen sixty one, with twenty five member states. By twenty twenty six, it has one hundred twenty.
N: Sukarno, the conference's host, did not get to watch most of that legacy unfold. He was removed from power between nineteen sixty five and nineteen sixty seven, in a United States facilitated military transition that killed an estimated five hundred thousand to one million people.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: That is what happened at Bandung, roughly in the order it happened. What it meant depends entirely on which delegation you were sitting with.
N: Five accounts of that week exist. Each gets its own case, in its own terms, before anything is said against it.

## PERSPECTIVE | Third Worldist / Anti-Colonial | victor
N: The anti-colonial account holds that Bandung was the first time the colonized world organized as a political subject, not an object of great power competition.
N: The twenty nine delegations agreed on almost nothing else. India was socialist, Turkey was capitalist, China was communist. They agreed only that European control over Asian and African peoples was finished.
N: Its strongest fact is the chain that followed. Ghana's independence, the Accra conference, seventeen African nations free in a single year, and a Non-Aligned Movement that grew from twenty five founding members to one hundred twenty.
N: Sukarno described what he thought held the room together.
M: We are united by a common detestation of colonialism in whatever form it appears. We are united by a common detestation of racialism.
N: The colonized world did not wait for anyone's permission. It wrote its own rules.

## PERSPECTIVE | Cold War / Western Realist | bystander
N: The realist account starts with arithmetic, not sentiment. Twenty nine nations refusing to choose a side threatened the alliances Washington had built around the Soviet perimeter: NATO, SEATO, CENTO, and bilateral pacts across Asia.
N: Non-alignment was also partly illusory. India took Soviet aid, Egypt took Soviet weapons after Bandung, Indonesia took Soviet naval vessels. Both superpowers courted the room they claimed to be shut out of.
N: John Foster Dulles said what he believed the position Bandung had taken actually amounted to.
M: The concept of neutrality is an obsolete, immoral, and shortsighted conception.
N: Its strongest fact is a ratio. The delegates in that room represented fifty four percent of the world's population, and controlled less than eight percent of its industrial output.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | Chinese / Zhou Enlai | academic
N: The Chinese account begins with a diplomat who arrived isolated. Fewer than half the delegations recognized the People's Republic, and several of them belonged to American led military alliances built against communist expansion.
N: Zhou Enlai needed to reframe China, not as a communist threat but as a fellow post-colonial state committed to peaceful coexistence. On the floor, responding to accusations of Chinese expansionism, he set his prepared remarks aside.
M: China has no desire to interfere in the internal affairs of other countries. We are here to seek common ground, not to create differences.
N: He met privately with Nehru, Nasser, and U Nu of Burma. He left Bandung a statesman.
N: Its strongest fact is what came seven years later. China and India went to war over the Aksai Chin plateau, and the Panchsheel framework Zhou himself had helped draft did not survive it.

## PERSPECTIVE | Pan-African / Nkrumah | revisionist
N: Kwame Nkrumah watched Bandung from the Gold Coast, still a British colony. He could not be a delegate. His country did not yet exist as a sovereign state.
N: Two years later, Ghana became independent, and Nkrumah organized the All-African People's Conference in Accra, attended by delegates from twenty eight African territories, to carry Bandung's example across the continent.
N: His book, Neo-Colonialism, argued that formal independence without economic independence was a hollow achievement.
M: The essence of neo-colonialism is that the State which is subject to it is, in theory, independent and has all the outward trappings of international sovereignty. In reality its economic system and thus its political policy is directed from outside.
N: Its strongest fact outlasted him. The CFA franc, controlled by the French Treasury, was still the currency of fourteen African nations in twenty twenty six.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | Feminist / Women at the Margins | vanquished
N: The Final Communique of the Asian-African Conference runs to ten sections and twenty nine sub-articles. It mentions women zero times.
N: The delegates were overwhelmingly male, the heads of delegation exclusively so, in a room built to dismantle one hierarchy while it left another standing.
N: In Egypt, Doria Shafik had led fifteen hundred women in a march on Parliament in February nineteen fifty one, demanding suffrage. She was later placed under house arrest by Nasser.
N: One delegation was the exception. Turkey's included women. Turkey had granted women's suffrage in nineteen thirty four, earlier than France or Switzerland. It was treated as a marker of modernity, not a principle to be shared.
N: Kumari Jayawardena's history of the pattern across the Bandung world put the verdict plainly.
F: The promise of national liberation was selectively fulfilled.
N: Sovereignty for the nation was on the agenda that week. Sovereignty for the women inside it was not.

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: None of the five is a lie. Each has a gap, including the account a sympathetic listener wants to believe most.
N: The anti-colonial account leaves out that Turkey, Pakistan, and the Philippines clashed with the neutralists inside the same room, and that the principles it celebrates carried no institutional teeth.
N: The realist account leaves out the moral weight of one point five four billion people who had, within living memory, been ruled by someone else.
N: The Chinese account leaves out that the same non-interference principle that made Zhou a statesman also shielded Beijing's own suppression of Tibet from outside scrutiny.
N: The Pan-African account leaves out Nkrumah's own one-party rule in Ghana, and the divisions among African states that pan-African solidarity never fully closed.
N: The feminist account leaves out that the movements it criticizes still produced real, slow gains, and that the Western governments sitting outside that room were just as male run in nineteen fifty five.

## REST

## CLOSE
N: The Ten Principles said nothing about who owned the copper mines, the oil fields, or the banks.
N: In twenty twenty six, the CFA franc, controlled by the French Treasury, was still the currency of fourteen African nations.
