## OPEN
N: On the fourteenth of February, thirteen forty nine, Strasbourg took its Jewish community to the cemetery and burned two thousand people alive.
N: The plague had not reached Strasbourg yet. It arrived months later.
N: That same week the council divided the dead's property among the citizens and cancelled the debts the citizens owed them.
N: The Black Death is remembered as the moment Europe lost its mind. A panic does not keep a ledger.

## TITLE
N: The Black Death. Thirteen forty six to thirteen fifty three.

## SCENE 1 | Messina, October thirteen forty seven
N: Twelve Genoese trading ships come into the Sicilian port of Messina. Most of the sailors aboard are already dead.
N: The ones still alive are covered in black boils that ooze blood and pus.
N: The harbor authorities order the ships back out to sea. It is too late.
N: Within six months the plague is in Genoa, in Venice, in Marseilles. By thirteen forty nine it is in England, Germany and Scandinavia. By thirteen fifty one, Moscow.

## ASIDE
N: What was moving was a bacterium. Yersinia pestis, identified in two thousand and eleven from teeth in mass graves, carried by fleas that lived on black rats that lived on ships.
N: It came in three forms. In the lymph nodes it raised the swellings called buboes and killed inside a week. In the lungs it spread on the breath and killed in two to three days.
N: In the blood it could kill in hours, before a single symptom showed.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The arithmetic
N: Europe held about seventy five million people before the plague. By thirteen fifty three it held between thirty and fifty million.
N: In Cairo the historian al-Maqrizi records ten to twenty thousand dead a day at the peak.
N: In Avignon, where the papacy sat, the cemeteries overflowed, and Pope Clement the Sixth consecrated the river Rhone so that bodies could be thrown into it.

## DOCUMENT | Agnolo di Tura | Sienese Chronicle | c. 1348
N: In Siena, Agnolo di Tura wrote down what he had done himself, because no gravedigger would come.
M: And I, Agnolo di Tura, buried my five children with my own hands. And there were also those who were so sparsely covered with earth that the dogs dragged them forth.
M: There was no one who wept for any death, for all awaited death.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | Paris, October thirteen forty eight
N: King Philip the Sixth asks the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Paris to explain the pestilence.
N: The cause, it reports, is a conjunction of Saturn, Jupiter and Mars in the sign of Aquarius, drawing poisoned vapors up out of the land and the sea.
N: Behind the astrology sits the real conviction. God has sent this for human sin.
N: The flagellants cross Germany, the Low Countries and Hungary. Two hundred to three hundred men enter a city, whip themselves bloody for thirty three days, one for each year of Christ's life, then move on.

## SCENE 4 | Chillon, and what was copied out of it
N: In September thirteen forty eight, Jews held in the castle of Chillon on Lake Geneva are tortured until they confess to poisoning the wells.
N: The confession is taken from a surgeon named Balavigny, then copied and forwarded town to town, and it crosses Europe faster than the plague does.
N: In Basel the community is locked into a wooden building on an island in the Rhine and burned. In Erfurt three thousand die in one day. In Mainz six thousand fight back and are destroyed.
N: More than two hundred Jewish communities are annihilated in three years.

## DOCUMENT | Jacob von Konigshofen | Strasbourg Chronicle | c. 1349
N: The chronicler Jacob von Konigshofen recorded what Strasbourg did next.
M: The money was divided among the artisans. The council took the cash that the Jews possessed and divided the debts owed to the Jews among the citizens.

## REST

## SCENE 5 | One to two pence a day
N: Before the plague an English laborer with no land earned one to two pence a day. There were more people than land, so the lord set the wage.
N: About four in ten English peasants were villeins. A villein could not leave the manor, or marry, or sell land, without the lord's permission.
N: Then a third to a half of the country died, and by thirteen fifty one some farm workers were earning double and triple what they had been paid.
N: Parliament, the landlord class, passed the Statute of Laborers in thirteen fifty one, fixing wages at the thirteen forty six level and making refusal of the old rate a crime.
N: The manor court records show those same lords competing for workers with higher wages, free meals and lighter obligations, while backing the law in public.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of this, and they are not five theories about what killed people. That question closed in a laboratory in two thousand and eleven.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Christian west | bystander
N: The first account is the one the West told about itself, and its strongest evidence is not belief. It is who died.
N: Parish records in England show clergy mortality of forty to fifty percent, higher than the general population, because a priest was obliged to attend the dying.
N: Historians run a line from that shortage to John Wycliffe challenging the authority of the Church in the thirteen seventies, and on to the Reformation.
N: Giovanni Boccaccio survived the plague in Florence and set the collapse down in the opening of the Decameron. The sick were left by their families. Property changed hands by proximity rather than inheritance.
M: How many valiant men, how many fair ladies, took breakfast with their kinfolk and that same night supped with their ancestors in the other world!

## PERSPECTIVE | Islamic medicine | academic
N: The second account belongs to physicians who had the mechanism right five hundred years before germ theory.
N: In thirteen forty nine, while Paris read the planets, Ibn Khatima of Almeria wrote that the disease was caused by minute bodies that entered the body and produced infection.
M: It has been established by experience that if a person who is sick with this disease is placed near a healthy person, the healthy person will almost certainly fall sick.
N: His contemporary Ibn al-Khatib, in Granada, said why the rival theory failed. Corrupted air cannot explain why the plague takes one household and leaves the house beside it.
M: The existence of contagion is established by experience, investigation, the evidence of the senses, and trustworthy reports.
N: He knew what he was contradicting. A hadith attributed to the Prophet Muhammad holds that there is no contagion, and that disease comes from God alone.
N: He was accused of heresy and strangled in prison in thirteen seventy four. The charge was political. The unorthodoxy did not help him.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | The Jewish communities | vanquished
N: The third account begins with the calendar, and the calendar is the argument.
N: Many of the massacres came before the plague reached the town, and some came where it never struck hard. The disease was the pretext, not the cause.
N: What the killings reliably produced was money. Jewish communities were concentrated in moneylending, trade and skilled crafts, because those were often the only occupations legally open to them.
N: The opposition is on the record too. In July thirteen forty eight Pope Clement the Sixth issued a bull saying Jews were dying of the plague like everyone else and could not be its cause.
M: It was brought to our attention by public fame, or more accurately infamy, that certain Christians, seduced by the Devil, blame the pestilence on poisonings carried out by Jews.
N: The survivors moved east into Poland and Lithuania, where King Casimir the Third offered them charters and protection.

## PERSPECTIVE | The peasants and laborers | revisionist
N: The fourth account says the mass death was a transfer of power, and it can show the transfer.
N: By fifteen hundred, villeinage had effectively disappeared from England. The law written to prevent that had sat on the books for a century and a half.
N: Thirty years of wage suppression produced the English Peasants' Revolt of thirteen eighty one. The trigger was a poll tax, a flat charge per head that fell hardest on the poor.
N: Wat Tyler and John Ball brought perhaps sixty thousand people into London. They burned the Savoy Palace of John of Gaunt, opened the prisons, and cornered the king, who was fourteen, at Mile End.
M: When Adam delved and Eve span, who was then the gentleman?
N: Richard the Second promised to abolish serfdom. Tyler was killed at a parley at Smithfield, the revolt collapsed, and the king took back every concession.
M: Villeins ye are still, and villeins ye shall remain.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Mongol road | bystander
N: The fifth account says the story starts a long way east, and years earlier than any of this.
N: A study in Nature in two thousand and twenty two traced the ancestor of every known Black Death strain to graves near Lake Issyk Kul, in what is now Kyrgyzstan, dated thirteen thirty eight.
N: What carried it west was the Mongol Empire's own infrastructure. The same roads that moved silk, spices, paper and gunpowder moved rats, fleas and Yersinia pestis.
N: In thirteen forty six the Golden Horde besieged the Genoese post at Caffa, in Crimea. The notary Gabriele de Mussi wrote the account everybody repeats.
M: The dying Tartars, realizing that they had no hope of escape, lost interest in the siege and catapulted the infected corpses into the city.
N: China's population fell from roughly a hundred and twenty million in thirteen forty to sixty five million by thirteen ninety three. The Middle East lost a third of its people.
N: Ibn Khaldun, writing from the Maghreb, gave it the sentence it deserved.
M: Civilization in both the East and the West was visited by a destructive plague which devastated nations and caused populations to vanish.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: And here is what each of them cannot account for.
N: The Christian account explains the flagellants and the emptied monasteries honestly. It has nothing to say about the physicians in Granada who had the mechanism right while Paris read the planets, and it does not look at what its own cities did in those years.
N: The Islamic medical account is proud of putting evidence above doctrine, and it has earned that. It passes quickly over the teaching that a Muslim must not leave a plague stricken land, which the same account elsewhere credits as a crude quarantine.
N: The Jewish account has the fewest survivors and the clearest documents, and it leaves out everything that is not the persecution. The medicine. The labor collapse. The Christians dying in the same streets. Its own record shows a pope ordering the killing to stop, so the Church was never one actor.
N: The peasants' account has the weakest causation, and its honest version admits it. Robert Brenner's point is that the identical shock produced opposite results. In eastern Europe the landlords tightened the bonds instead, and the second serfdom ran five more centuries. It also tells a whole century with no priest and no burned community in it.
N: The Mongol account is the widest and the least certain. Its most repeated scene may not have happened as told. Gabriele de Mussi may never have been at Caffa, and the plague was probably already inside the walls, in the rats.

## REST

## CLOSE
N: Venice set forty days of isolation and called it the quarantina. The practice is still named after the number.
N: England did not have its people back until the sixteenth century.
N: And in Nuremberg, the council cleared the ground where the Jewish quarter had been and built a marketplace on it. The market is still there. It is called the Hauptmarkt.
