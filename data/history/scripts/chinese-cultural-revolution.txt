## OPEN
N: You think this was children rising up against their elders.
N: On August the fifth, nineteen sixty six, the Chairman of the Chinese Communist Party wrote four words in a big character poster: bombard the headquarters.
N: The headquarters he meant was his own party, the one he had led for seventeen years.
N: Two and a half months earlier, on May the sixteenth, that party's own Central Committee had circulated a document, drafted under his direction, accusing its own leadership of secretly restoring capitalism. The attack on the schools came after the attack on the government.

## TITLE
N: The Chinese Cultural Revolution. Nineteen sixty six to nineteen seventy six.

## SCENE 1 | The circular
N: May the sixteenth, nineteen sixty six. The Central Committee's circular calls on the masses to find and destroy counter revolutionary revisionists hiding inside the party itself.
N: Within weeks, students at Tsinghua University Middle School form the first Red Guard unit. By August there are millions of them, wearing red armbands, carrying Mao Zedong's Little Red Book.
N: They beat their teachers. They ransack homes. They destroy temples, libraries, ancestral shrines. Between August and November that year, Mao reviews more than eleven million of them, in eight mass rallies at Tiananmen Square.

## DOCUMENT | Mao Zedong | Big-character poster | August 5, 1966
N: On August the fifth, nineteen sixty six, Mao Zedong wrote his big character poster.
M: Bombard the headquarters. My big character poster.

## ASIDE
N: He was not going through the party's own institutions. He was going over their heads, directly to the masses, and the millions of teenagers carrying Little Red Books were the instrument he chose.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The reason
N: The timing was not an accident. Mao Zedong had been sidelined inside his own party after the Great Leap Forward killed an estimated fifteen to forty five million people through famine.
N: Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping pulled the country back from that catastrophe with pragmatic reforms, profit, bonuses, private plots, and in doing it, shrunk Mao's own authority. The Cultural Revolution was his way of taking it back.

## SCENE 3 | The cell in Kaifeng
N: Liu Shaoqi was the President of China. Red Guards denounced him as China's Khrushchev, and dragged him into struggle sessions where he was beaten.
N: He had diabetes, then pneumonia. Both went untreated. On November the twelfth, nineteen sixty nine, he died on the floor of a detention cell in Kaifeng.
N: His body was cremated the same day, under a false name. His own family was not informed. His children did not learn he was dead until years later.

## REST

## SCENE 4 | The Four Olds
N: The campaign called itself a war on the Four Olds: old customs, old culture, old habits, old ideas. In Qufu, the birthplace of Confucius, the Kong family cemetery was excavated and the remains scattered. Private libraries were hauled into the street and burned.
N: The playwright Wu Han had written a historical play, Hai Rui Dismissed from Office. Mao's wife, Jiang Qing, identified it as a coded attack on her husband. Wu Han became one of the revolution's first prominent victims, and died in prison in nineteen sixty nine.
N: In Tibet, an estimated six thousand of the region's six thousand two hundred fifty nine monasteries were damaged or destroyed.

## SCENE 5 | Guangxi and the countryside
N: By nineteen sixty eight, the Red Guards themselves had fractured into competing armed factions. In Guangxi province, that factional warfare killed an estimated one hundred thousand to one hundred fifty thousand people.
N: The writer Zheng Yi later documented cases of cannibalism in Wuxuan County, acts of political ritual carried out against the bodies of the dead.
N: Mao sent the People's Liberation Army in to restore order, then launched the Up to the Mountains, Down to the Countryside campaign. Seventeen million urban youth were relocated to rural villages between nineteen sixty eight and nineteen eighty. The generation that had destroyed its own schools spent the following decade planting rice.

## DOCUMENT | Jiang Qing | Statement at her trial | 1980
N: Mao Zedong died on September the ninth, nineteen seventy six. Within a month, party leaders arrested the Gang of Four, including his widow, Jiang Qing. Her trial, broadcast on Chinese television in nineteen eighty, is where she said this.
F: I was Chairman Mao's dog. Whoever he told me to bite, I bit.

## ASIDE
N: It was the only defense she offered the court. The man who had told her whom to bite had been dead for four years.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of one decade, and the disagreement is not whether it happened. It is whose fault it was, and whether calling it a mistake is even the right word. The death toll is estimated at five hundred thousand to two million, and the Chinese Communist Party's nineteen eighty one Resolution on Party History never gave a number at all.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Maoist Case | victor
N: The May Sixteenth Circular, issued under Mao Zedong's own direction, named the enemy plainly: representatives of the bourgeoisie who had sneaked into the party, the government, the army, the arts. Not abstractions. Liu Shaoqi. Deng Xiaoping. The cadres who had quietly reintroduced profit and private plots after the famine.
N: Mao's theory of continuous revolution held that class struggle does not end when a party seizes the state. The Soviet Union was the proof: after Stalin's death, Khrushchev denounced him and let a new bureaucratic class inherit the country. By the nineteen sixties, Mao saw China headed the same way.
N: The instrument was the masses. Red Guards were not directed by a central command. They were unleashed on the Four Olds, the buried feudal past inside the classroom and the temple.
M: To rebel is justified.
N: That line, from Mao Zedong's own writing decades earlier, is what Red Guards carried into every school they entered.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Persecuted Cadres and Intellectuals | vanquished
N: Liu Shaoqi's fall gets remembered because he held office. Most victims did not. In Beijing alone, one thousand seven hundred seventy two people were killed in August and September of nineteen sixty six, later called Red August.
N: Bian Zhongyun, vice principal of a girls' middle school in Beijing, was beaten to death by her own students on August the fifth, nineteen sixty six, the same day Mao posted Bombard the Headquarters. She was the first confirmed educator killed.
N: Intellectuals were sorted into the lowest of nine categories of class enemy, nicknamed the Stinking Ninth. The translator Fu Lei hanged himself with his wife after Red Guards ransacked their Shanghai home.
N: The novelist Ba Jin survived, and spent his life asking for one thing: a museum, so it could not be repeated.
M: The only way to prevent a recurrence is to remember.
N: No museum was ever built. Ba Jin died in two thousand five, and the proposal was never revived.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Red Guard Generation | bystander
N: They were fifteen, sixteen, seventeen years old, raised entirely inside the People's Republic, taught from birth that Mao Zedong was always right. When he told them their teachers and their parents' colleagues were secretly working to restore the old society, most did not doubt him.
N: A former Red Guard, unnamed in the record, was quoted years later trying to explain the belief itself.
M: I was a true believer. I believed Mao the way a Christian believes in God. When he told us to smash, we smashed.
N: By nineteen sixty eight the party that had used them had no more use for them. The Up to the Mountains, Down to the Countryside campaign sent seventeen million of them to the villages instead. The schools they had destroyed were the schools they no longer had.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Party's Historical Verdict | academic
N: The party's own account starts with an admission. The nineteen eighty one Resolution on Certain Questions in the History of the Party, drafted over two years by the Central Committee under Deng Xiaoping's supervision, called the decade the most severe setback the party and the state had suffered since the founding.
N: The same document then preserved the man responsible for it.
M: Comrade Mao Zedong was a great Marxist. It is true that he made gross mistakes during the Cultural Revolution, but his contributions to the Chinese revolution far outweigh his mistakes.
N: Privately, the formula got a shorthand: Mao was seventy percent correct and thirty percent wrong. It let the party condemn the decade without condemning the man who had founded it. Under Xi Jinping, whose father spent sixteen years in detention during these years, the space to discuss it has narrowed further.

## PERSPECTIVE | Tibet and the Ethnic Minorities | indigenous
N: Tibet had already been through seven years of what China called democratic reform when this decade began: monastic land seized. What followed did not soften that. It finished it.
N: Of Tibet's estimated six thousand two hundred fifty nine monasteries, about six thousand were damaged or destroyed. Monks were forced to denounce the Dalai Lama in public.
N: An unnamed Tibetan monk, quoted years later in exile, described what the campaign asked of them.
M: They destroyed our monasteries, burned our scriptures, and forced monks to copulate in public to prove that religion was a lie. What they could not destroy was what we carried in our memory.
N: In Inner Mongolia, an estimated three hundred forty six thousand people were detained, and sixteen thousand two hundred twenty two killed, accused of belonging to a separatist organization that likely never existed.
N: For Han Chinese, the Four Olds were customs revolutionary culture could replace. For Tibetans and Mongolians, the Four Olds were the civilization itself.

## REST | short

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: What each account cannot see from where it stands.
N: The Maoist case names a real danger of bureaucratic drift, and says almost nothing about the death toll, or about Mao Zedong reclaiming power he had lost after his own famine.
N: The persecuted cadres and intellectuals have the clearest claim to grievance, and say the least about the corruption inside the party bureaucracy that gave the revolution its opening argument, or about the neighbors and colleagues, not only Red Guards, who did the beating.
N: The Red Guard generation's account of being used and discarded is true, and it is also the account most likely to soften what teenagers actually did to the people they destroyed.
N: The party's own verdict admits the catastrophe in a single sentence and spends the rest of it protecting the man and the system that produced it. It has still never given the one number that would measure it.
N: And the Tibetan and minority account, the clearest evidence the destruction fell hardest along ethnic lines, says least about the Tibetan Red Guards who took part in it themselves, and the Han Chinese who suffered under the same campaign.

## DOCUMENT | Deng Xiaoping | Remark to Italian journalist Oriana Fallaci | 1980
N: Deng Xiaoping had been purged twice during the decade, and later led China's economic reforms from the authority of a man who had survived it. In nineteen eighty, asked to explain what had happened, this is what he told a foreign journalist.
M: There are things about the Cultural Revolution which I cannot explain clearly to you, and we don't want to.

N: In two thousand twenty one, the party's new historical resolution folded the entire ten years into a single paragraph.
