## OPEN
N: You think you know this one. The market crashed in October nineteen twenty nine, and that crash was the Great Depression.
N: The record says otherwise. The crash was a symptom, not the disease. The disease spread slowly, then everywhere.
N: American unemployment did not peak the week of the crash, or even the year after it. It peaked in nineteen thirty three, four years later, at twenty four point nine percent, about fifteen million people out of work.
N: Four years is a strange length for one bad afternoon on a trading floor.

## TITLE
N: The Great Depression. October nineteen twenty nine to nineteen thirty nine.

## SCENE 1 | Wall Street, October 29, 1929
N: Sixteen point four million shares change hands on the New York Stock Exchange today, as prices collapse.
N: In two days, the Dow has lost about a quarter of its value.
N: Nobody trading on this floor calls it the Great Depression yet. It is Black Tuesday, and the crash itself is only a symptom. The disease is still ahead of them.

## SCENE 2 | What the crash left behind
N: Between nineteen twenty nine and nineteen thirty three, roughly nine thousand American banks fail. Each one takes the ordinary savings of ordinary depositors down with it.
N: US industrial production falls forty seven percent. Real output falls about thirty percent. Consumer prices fall roughly a quarter. Global industrial output falls by about a third.
N: Unemployment climbs from near zero in nineteen twenty nine to twenty four point nine percent in nineteen thirty three, fifteen million people with no work, in a country that six years earlier had believed poverty was almost abolished.
N: There is no battlefield number attached to any of this. Hunger and suicide reach across four continents, and historians still debate how many deaths that adds up to.

## SCENE 3 | Austria, May 1931
N: It does not stay in America. In May nineteen thirty one, Austria's largest bank, the Creditanstalt, reveals it has lost most of its capital.
N: Foreign creditors pull their money out of Central Europe. Germany's Danat-Bank shuts its doors on July thirteenth, nineteen thirty one. Germany leaves the gold standard days later. Britain abandons it on September twenty first, nineteen thirty one.
N: Herbert Hoover had signed the Smoot-Hawley Tariff on June seventeenth, nineteen thirty, raising US duties on imports. Other countries retaliate. By nineteen thirty three, world trade has shrunk by about two thirds in value.

## DOCUMENT | Andrew Mellon, recounted by Herbert Hoover | The Memoirs of Herbert Hoover, Volume 3 | 1952
N: Hoover's own Treasury Secretary, Andrew Mellon, had one prescription for all of it, and Hoover later wrote down what Mellon told him.
M: Liquidate labor, liquidate stocks, liquidate the farmers, liquidate real estate. It will purge the rottenness out of the system.

## ASIDE
N: That is the entire liquidationist argument in one sentence. Let the pain run its course, and do not prop up what should fall.

## SCENE 4 | Germany, the winter of 1932
N: German unemployment has climbed toward six million, more than forty percent of the workforce.
N: In nineteen twenty eight, before the crash, the Nazi Party won eight hundred thousand votes, a fringe. By July nineteen thirty two it wins thirteen point four million and becomes the largest party in the Reichstag.
N: On January thirtieth, nineteen thirty three, President Hindenburg appoints Adolf Hitler chancellor.

## SCENE 5 | Washington, March 1933
N: A few weeks after Hitler takes power in Germany, Franklin Roosevelt delivers his first inaugural address in Washington.

## DOCUMENT | Franklin D. Roosevelt | First Inaugural Address | March 4, 1933
N: Roosevelt tells the country what he believes the moment actually requires.
M: So, first of all, let me assert my firm belief that the only thing we have to fear is fear itself, nameless, unreasoning, unjustified terror.

## ASIDE
N: Roosevelt's New Deal follows, and it slows the collapse. It does not end it. Unemployment is still fourteen percent by nineteen forty.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of one collapse, and each one answers a different question about what actually happened.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Keynesian Account | academic
N: The Keynesian account starts with a simple observation. In nineteen thirty three there are idle factories, idle workers, and unmet needs, all at once. Nothing physical has been destroyed.
N: John Maynard Keynes named the mechanism in The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money, published in nineteen thirty six. Frightened households and businesses hoard cash instead of spending it, total demand falls below what the economy can produce, and output and employment settle into a low, miserable equilibrium with no automatic way out.
N: In this account, Hoover's response made it worse. Balancing the budget in a slump and defending the gold standard both drained demand exactly when demand was already failing.
N: The remedy was government spending large enough to replace what the private sector would not spend. Keynes said so plainly, arguing for the opposite of what Andrew Mellon had prescribed.
M: The boom, not the slump, is the right time for austerity at the Treasury.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Monetarist and Austrian Account | revisionist
N: The monetarist account rewrites the story from data, not sentiment. Milton Friedman and Anna Schwartz found that the Federal Reserve let the American money supply fall by about a third in the years nineteen twenty nine to nineteen thirty three.
N: When thousands of banks failed, the Federal Reserve was designed to be the lender of last resort. Instead it stood by.
N: The Austrian economist Friedrich Hayek located the fault earlier, in the credit boom of the nineteen twenties. Cheap credit had funneled investment into projects that made no sense at honest interest rates, and the bust was simply the market correcting those errors.
N: Both schools reject the Keynesian conclusion that the market is inherently prone to collapse. For both, this was a policy failure, not a market failure.
N: Decades later, Milton Friedman said as much directly to Federal Reserve Governor Ben Bernanke.
M: You're right, we did it. We're very sorry. But thanks to you, we won't do it again.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Unemployed | vanquished
N: The third account belongs to the people behind the twenty four point nine percent. Roughly nine thousand banks failed between nineteen thirty and nineteen thirty three, and there was no insurance to protect a depositor's savings until the New Deal created it.
N: Shantytowns of packing crates and tar paper rose on the edge of every large city. People called them Hoovervilles, after the president they blamed.
N: In the summer of nineteen thirty two, about twenty thousand First World War veterans, the Bonus Army, camped in Washington to demand early payment of a bonus they had been promised. On July twenty eighth, the US Army drove them out with cavalry, tanks, and tear gas, and burned their camp.
N: This account refuses to let the causation debate stand in for the experience. Whether the fault was demand or the money supply meant nothing to the child sent to school without breakfast.
N: John Steinbeck wrote what that felt like from the inside.
M: How can you frighten a man whose hunger is not only in his own cramped stomach but in the wretched bellies of his children?

## PERSPECTIVE | The Global Periphery | revisionist
N: The fourth account says the Depression is remembered as an American story that spread to Europe, and its cruelest effects fell on places with no stock exchange and no vote in the policies that ruined them.
N: The prices of coffee, cotton, rubber, jute, and wheat fell by roughly half between nineteen twenty nine and nineteen thirty one, and entire nations lost their income at once.
N: In colonial India, wheat lost nearly half its value and raw jute more than sixty percent, yet the land tax stayed fixed in cash. Indebted farmers sold the only wealth they had left, their gold ornaments and coins. By the end of nineteen thirty one, gold worth about one hundred seventy four point six million rupees had been exported to Britain as what the record calls distress gold.
N: In ten of Latin America's twenty republics, a single product earned more than half of all foreign exchange. When export markets closed, Brazil burned or dumped tens of millions of bags of unsellable coffee into the sea.
N: The historian Rosemary Thorp put the structural point in one sentence.
F: The Depression revealed how completely the fortunes of the colonial peasant had been tied to a world market he neither saw nor controlled.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Political Crisis | academic
N: The fifth account says the most lasting product of the Depression was not economic but political. Mass unemployment discredited the democracies that presided over it.
N: In Germany the slump did not merely coincide with Hitler's rise. German unemployment climbed toward six million, more than forty percent of the workforce, and the Nazi vote climbed with it, from eight hundred thousand in nineteen twenty eight to thirteen point four million in July nineteen thirty two.
N: The contrast that haunted the decade was the Soviet Union. Sealed off by a state monopoly on trade and its Five-Year Plans, it reported no unemployment at all, and to many watching from outside, the comparison seemed to prove that capitalism was finished.
N: The historian Karl Polanyi gave the deepest reading of what that choice, between one economic order and another, actually decided.
M: The origins of the cataclysm lay in the Utopian endeavor of economic liberalism to set up a self-regulating market system.

## REST | short

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: And here is what each account cannot hold.
N: The Keynesian account says little about the Federal Reserve policy that let the money supply itself collapse, or about how much of the eventual recovery came from monetary expansion rather than government spending.
N: The Monetarist and Austrian account has almost nothing to say about the lived human cost of leaving deflation and unemployment to run their course, or about the international collapse of trade that a central bank alone could not fix.
N: The Unemployed account has little patience for the causation debate, and it says almost nothing about the global reach of the same collapse, or the policy reforms that eventually came out of it.
N: The Global Periphery account counts the export income precisely, and it says little about the local elites who profited inside the same colonial system that broke the peasant farmer.
N: And the Political Crisis account, generous to no one else, is quiet about the famine and repression the Soviet Union concealed behind its reported full employment, and about the democracies, including the United States, that survived the crisis intact.

N: US industrial output did not return to its nineteen twenty nine level until nineteen thirty nine.
N: Ten years to get back to where it started, and even then, it took the wartime mobilization of a second global war to finish the job.
