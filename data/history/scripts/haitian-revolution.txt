## TITLE

N: The Haitian Revolution, seventeen ninety one to eighteen oh four.

## SCENE 1 | The night of the twenty second
# MOOD: rupture
N: Saint-Domingue, August seventeen ninety one. France's part of the island of Hispaniola, in the Caribbean, grows forty per cent of the sugar in the world, and five hundred thousand enslaved Africans grow it.
N: This month the enslaved of the northern plain rise and burn the plantations. The war that follows lasts thirteen years and beats Spanish, British and French invasions.
N: It ends on the first of January, eighteen oh four, when the generals of what calls itself the native army declare the colony independent and name it Haiti, the first independent Black republic.
N: Three accounts of how it began are held here, and disagree.
N: Thomas Madiou, the Haitian historian, tells of the night of the fourteenth: two hundred delegates of the workshops, the enslaved of each plantation, met at one plantation, heard a supposed decree of the king read out, and fixed the twenty second.
N: The commission of the Convention, the revolutionary government in Paris, records no ceremony: the fire began towards midnight, and by the morning of the twenty third it counted thirty seven proprietors, attorneys and refiners killed.
N: Antoine Dalmas, a colonist writing afterwards, is the one source here for a sacrifice: a black pig, in a wooded ground called the Caiman. He dates the meeting before it, not the sacrifice.

## PERSPECTIVE | The enslaved revolutionaries | indigenous
# MOOD: testimony
# extract: src-madiou-1848-t3.p471.en
N: The first account belongs to the people who did it. By the event record they organised through vodou, a spiritual practice forged from West African traditions under enslavement, the markets and the drums.
N: None of the enslaved speaks here in his or her own words; the case is made by the state they founded.
N: In May eighteen oh five Dessalines, now emperor, gave it a constitution. As Madiou prints it, in a translation made by Void.
M: The people inhabiting the island formerly called St. Domingue agree here to form themselves into a free, sovereign state, independent of all other powers of the universe, under the name of empire of Haiti.
N: Its second article abolishes slavery for ever. Its fourteenth says Haitians will be known only under the generic name of blacks.
N: Women fought at every stage, by the event record; it names Marie-Jeanne Lamartiniere. No source here is a woman's account.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Haitian nationalist account | revisionist
# MOOD: reckoning
# extract: src-dubois-garrigus-2006.sec11
N: The third account is the Haitian state's own, from the declaration to the legislature that took on the debt. It holds that the people freed themselves and France set the price of recognising it.
N: The declaration Dessalines signed puts the claim as a question, in the Dubois and Garrigus translation.
M: We have dared to be free, let us be thus by ourselves and for ourselves.
M: What people fought for us? What people wanted to gather the fruits of our labor?
N: In eighteen twenty six the Haitian legislature declared that price, a hundred and fifty million francs, a national debt, and voted a contribution over ten years to extinguish what it called a sacred engagement.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Atlantic academic account | academic
# MOOD: reckoning
# extract: src-yakubu-opondo-2025.p166
N: The fourth account belongs to the scholars. The event record names its subject as silence: the scholar Michel-Rolph Trouillot's argument that the revolution was unthinkable to those who watched it, and so went unwritten.
N: Moses Joseph Yakubu and Paul Abiero Opondo, writing from two African universities in twenty twenty five, trace the abolition of slavery to the slave revolts, Saint-Domingue among them.
M: While the former protest was short-lived, the latter soared on to serve as an impetus for the freedom of black slaves across the globe

## PERSPECTIVE | The indemnity | revisionist
# MOOD: procedure
N: The fifth account is a receipt, and this record holds it.
N: Paris, the seventeenth of April, eighteen twenty five. Charles the Tenth, King of France, signs an ordinance, a royal edict.
N: It requires the inhabitants of the former colony to pay a hundred and fifty million francs in five annual terms, an indemnity to compensate the former colonists, and on that condition concedes the independence, full and entire, of their government.
N: It does not name the enslaved. It names the colonists. The lost property, by the event record, was five hundred thousand human beings.
N: How long Haiti paid is disputed here: the one article that dates the indemnity runs it to eighteen eighty three, apart from the later loans; the event record carries the debt to nineteen forty seven.

# MOOD: reckoning

N: The account of the revolutionaries has no witness of its own here, and leaves out April eighteen oh four, which Madiou counts and defends.
N: The French account leaves out the note of the thirteenth of November and the law of May eighteen oh two, the French state's papers.
N: The Haitian nationalist account leaves out twenty three heads of state in the first century, most removed by force, by the event record.
N: The academic account reads the record in translation, and none of its holders here counts the dead.
N: The indemnity account has the ordinance and no treasury book. What was paid after eighteen twenty six is known here through two French abstracts.
N: And no woman speaks here in her own words. The women appear as an arrested wife, an article of a constitution, and counted victims.
