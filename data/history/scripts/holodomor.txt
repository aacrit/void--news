## OPEN
N: You think of this as a famine, meaning there was not enough food.
N: There was food. The nineteen thirty two Ukrainian harvest came in at fourteen point six million tons, against a normal year of twenty three million, and Moscow's quota for that harvest was seven point seven million tons anyway.
N: Between nineteen thirty and nineteen thirty three the Soviet Union exported roughly five point eight million tons of grain, and at the exact peak of the famine, the winter of nineteen thirty two into nineteen thirty three, one point eight million tons of it left the country by ship.
N: A famine is a shortage. This was a surplus, requisitioned, and shipped out the door while the people who grew it were sealed inside to starve.

## TITLE
N: The Holodomor. Nineteen thirty two to nineteen thirty three.

## SCENE 1 | The walking tour
N: The tenth of March, nineteen thirty three. A rural railway station somewhere between Kharkiv and Poltava.
N: Gareth Jones steps off the official Soviet press train and starts walking. Twenty seven years old, a Welsh journalist who has visited the Soviet Union twice before, speaks fluent Russian, and carries credentials from his old job advising David Lloyd George on foreign affairs.
N: For three days he sleeps in peasant houses and eats nothing but the black bread and a few frozen potatoes he is carrying. Village after village tells him the same thing. There is no bread. The government took it. People are dying.
N: On the twenty ninth of March he lands in Berlin, walks out of Tempelhof Airport, and calls a press conference.

## DOCUMENT | Gareth Jones | Press statement, Berlin | March 29, 1933
N: Jones told the reporters this, in his own words.
M: I walked through villages and twelve collective farms. Everywhere was the cry, there is no bread, we are dying. This cry came from every part of Russia, from the Volga, Siberia, White Russia, the North Caucasus, Central Asia.

## ASIDE
N: The Soviet Union banned him from returning. Maxim Litvinov, the Soviet Foreign Minister, told a British diplomat that Jones would never receive another visa. In August nineteen thirty five he was killed under unclear circumstances in Japanese-occupied Manchuria. He was twenty nine years old.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The grain itself was the point
N: The confiscation had a method. Brigades went house to house, testing the temperature of the stoves for food someone had cooked recently, probing walls, digging up floors.
N: In December nineteen thirty two, eighty nine Ukrainian villages that had missed their quota were placed on what was called the black board. No trade goods at all: no matches, no kerosene, no salt, no sugar.
N: Internal passports were introduced that same December, and collective farm workers were not issued one. Roadblocks went up around some villages. There was nowhere left to walk to.

## SCENE 3 | The law and the number
N: On the seventh of August nineteen thirty two, the Soviet Central Executive Committee passed a law nicknamed the Law of Spikelets: up to ten years in a labor camp, or death, for taking so much as a stalk of grain left in a harvested field.
N: By January nineteen thirty three, more than one hundred thousand people had been convicted under it.

## DOCUMENT | Joseph Stalin and Vyacheslav Molotov | Joint directive of the Soviet Central Committee and Council of People's Commissars | January 22, 1933
N: On the twenty second of January nineteen thirty three, Stalin and his deputy Vyacheslav Molotov signed a joint directive answering the villages that were emptying out.
M: In order to prevent the mass exodus of peasants, party and Soviet organs in Ukraine and the North Caucasus are directed to adopt immediate measures to stop the mass departure of peasants.

## ASIDE
N: No passport meant no legal way to leave. The directive did not just block an exodus. It sealed people inside the region where the quota had already taken the food.

## REST

## SCENE 4 | The count
N: By the spring of nineteen thirty three, the dead were collected each morning on carts. In Kharkiv, sanitation brigades gathered bodies from the streets at dawn. In the villages, whole families were found dead in their homes.
N: The secret police, the OGPU, kept its own count and did not flinch from it: over two thousand five hundred cases of cannibalism recorded in nineteen thirty three alone. The same reports blamed kulak sabotage for a famine the numbers in those reports already described.
N: The Soviet government suppressed all of it until nineteen eighty seven, when a policy called glasnost allowed the first public discussion. The word Holodomor, hunger inflicted as death, only entered common use after that.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts, and here the argument is not about whether people died. Both sides in it accept the same number: three point nine million excess deaths in Ukraine. What they argue about is intent. Whether Moscow set out to destroy a nation, or ran a policy that killed millions without picking Ukrainians out for it.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Soviet state | victor
N: The government's own case, made at the time. Collectivization was not cruelty. It was the price of not being destroyed. Stalin told his industrial managers in February nineteen thirty one that to slow down meant to lag behind, and those who lagged behind got beaten.
M: To slow down would mean to lag behind. And those who lag behind get beaten.
N: Grain paid for the plan. Between nineteen thirty and nineteen thirty three the Soviet Union exported roughly five point eight million tons of it, and that money bought the machinery for Dneprostroi, Magnitogorsk, the Urals-Kuznetsk Combine. Any shortfall, in the government's account, came from kulak sabotage, not the quota Moscow had set.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Ukrainian survivor | vanquished
N: Petro Mostovyi was seven years old in nineteen thirty three, in the village of Stavyshche in Kyiv Oblast. His father had been dekulakized: the family's cow, horse, and grain confiscated, and sent to a labor camp in Siberia. His mother fed the children on beet greens, bark soup, and dried acacia blossoms.
N: He walked past the village church one day and saw bodies stacked outside the door, wrapped in rags, with no one left to bury them. He counted eleven. He was seven. He told the U.S. Commission on the Ukraine Famine that story in nineteen eighty six.
N: One survivor, unnamed in that same record, remembered exactly what the confiscation sounded like.
F: They took everything. The grain, the beets, the potatoes in the cellar. They even took the pot from the stove. My mother stood in the doorway and said, you are killing us, and the man with the list said, you are killing yourselves.
N: There was nowhere to walk to for more. No passport for a farm worker, and roadblocks on the roads that led to a city where there was still bread.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | The Western press corps | bystander
N: The witnesses from outside did not agree with each other. Jones had no bureau and no institutional backing, just what he had walked and seen. Duranty had the New York Times and a Pulitzer prize, awarded in nineteen thirty two, and the access that came with Moscow's trust. Two days after Jones's press conference, Duranty published his answer.
M: Russians hungry, but not starving. There is no actual starvation or deaths from starvation but there is widespread mortality from diseases due to malnutrition.
N: He told the British Embassy in Moscow, in private, that he thought as many as ten million people had died. He never printed that number.
N: Malcolm Muggeridge, reporting the same famine for the Manchester Guardian, had his dispatches buried by editors sympathetic to the Soviet experiment. He kept his own verdict on his rival for his diary.
M: Duranty is the greatest liar of any journalist I have met in fifty years of journalism.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Ukrainian national account | revisionist
N: The case for genocide starts with the number itself. The nineteen thirty two quota for Ukraine, seven point seven million tons, was set after Ukrainian officials had already assessed that year's harvest at fourteen point six million, against a normal year of twenty three million. It took more than half of what had actually grown. When Ukrainian officials appealed to reduce it, Stalin sent Lazar Kaganovich and Vyacheslav Molotov to Kharkiv to enforce it harder, with a second sweep for whatever grain was still hidden.
N: What followed applied only to Ukraine and to the neighboring Kuban region, where Ukrainians were the majority: the blacklisting, the closed border with Russia and Belarus, the passport peasants there were not given. And it ran beside a second campaign. Ukrainian writers, academics, and clergy were arrested or driven to suicide, in what is called the Executed Renaissance.
N: The demographic study behind the number both sides use, three point nine million excess deaths, is this account's strongest evidence: concentrated in one nation, alongside an attack on who that nation's writers and thinkers were.
N: Raphael Lemkin, the lawyer who had coined the word genocide itself, said as much at a commemoration in New York in nineteen fifty three.
M: What I want you to understand is that this is, was, a case of genocide, of destruction, not of individuals only, but of a culture and a nation.
N: The argument still runs. When Russia invaded Ukraine in twenty twenty two, Ukrainian officials invoked the Holodomor by name.

## PERSPECTIVE | The archival record | academic
N: The archives that opened after nineteen ninety one did not settle the argument. They gave both sides in it more evidence. Robert Conquest's case for deliberate genocide held up against the new documents. So did a very different reading of the same documents, offered in two thousand four by R. W. Davies and Stephen Wheatcroft.
M: The question is not whether millions died. The question is whether the Soviet leadership intended to destroy Ukrainians as a national group, or whether it pursued policies whose catastrophic effects it accepted without specifically targeting Ukraine.
N: Their case starts outside Ukraine entirely. Kazakhstan lost an estimated one and a half million people to the same collectivization: thirty eight percent of the Kazakh population, a larger share than Ukraine lost. If the aim was to destroy Ukrainians specifically, the Kazakh famine sits awkwardly beside it.
N: What the archives do show is that the leadership knew. OGPU reports reached Stalin describing starvation and cannibalism, and the Politburo answered with more procurement, not less.
N: Both readings rest on the same demographic study, three point nine million excess deaths in Ukraine between nineteen thirty two and nineteen thirty four. Neither side disputes that number. They dispute what it proves.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: What each account cannot see from where it stands.
N: The Soviet state calls this an all-Soviet tragedy, and it applied border closures, blacklists, and a ban on leaving to one specific region while it says so. Grain kept leaving the country by ship the whole time it says there was none to spare.
N: The survivor's account has the least reason to lie, and it cannot see past the village. It says nothing about the geopolitics that set the quota in Moscow.
N: The Western witnesses make Jones and Duranty the whole story. They have little to say about Ukrainian resistance, and nothing about how the procurement system kept running once the correspondents stopped watching.
N: The Ukrainian national account, the one most people arrive already holding, rests on the fact that Ukraine was hit hardest. It says far less about Kazakhstan, which lost a greater share of its own population to the very same policy, and about the Ukrainian officials who enforced the quota themselves.
N: And the archival account, weighing intent so carefully, has the least to say about what it actually felt like. The number three point nine million came from a table. The eleven bodies outside the church in Stavyshche did not.

N: In two thousand three, the Pulitzer Board commissioned Columbia University historian Mark von Hagen to review Duranty's Soviet reporting. He recommended the prize be revoked.
N: The board declined, and said the prize had been awarded for reporting in a different period.
