## OPEN
N: You think this war ended.
N: July the twenty seventh, nineteen fifty three. An armistice. The guns went quiet.
N: Look at who signed it. A United States Army general, for the United Nations Command. A North Korean general, for the other side. South Korea refused to sign.
N: Seventy three years later, that is still the only document that ends the fighting. No peace treaty has followed. North and South Korea remain, on paper, at war.

## TITLE
N: The Korean War. Nineteen fifty to nineteen fifty three.

## SCENE 1 | Thirty minutes and a map
N: August the tenth, nineteen forty five. Two young United States Army colonels, Dean Rusk and Charles Bonesteel, are given thirty minutes and a National Geographic map.
N: Their task: divide the Korean peninsula into an American zone and a Soviet one. Neither has set foot in Korea.
N: They draw the line along the thirty eighth parallel because it puts the capital, Seoul, inside the American zone.
N: Five years later, thirty six thousand five hundred seventy four Americans will die defending that line.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The bridges
N: June the twenty fifth, nineteen fifty, four in the morning. Seventy five thousand North Korean soldiers cross the thirty eighth parallel. Seoul falls in three days.
N: At two thirty in the morning on June the twenty eighth, the army's own chief of staff orders the Han River bridges destroyed while refugees are still crossing them.
N: The explosions kill about eight hundred civilians and strand two of the army's divisions on the wrong side of the river.
N: By mid September, South Korean and American forces hold a perimeter around Pusan covering four percent of the peninsula.

## DOCUMENT | Harry S. Truman | Personal diary entry | June 30, 1950
N: Five days into the invasion, President Truman wrote this in his own diary.
M: If we let Korea down, the Soviets will keep right on going and swallow up one piece of Asia after another. If we were to let Asia go, the Near East would collapse, and no telling what would happen in Europe.

## ASIDE
N: Two days later, he sent American troops in without a declaration of war. He called it a police action.
N: That word would follow the war for seventy years. A police action does not kill three million people.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | Inchon
N: September the fifteenth, nineteen fifty. MacArthur lands seventy five thousand troops at Inchon. Naval officers had put the odds at five thousand to one against: thirty foot tides, a narrow channel, a seawall instead of a beach.
N: It works. Seoul is recaptured by September the twenty eighth. United Nations forces cross the thirty eighth parallel in October, pushing north toward the Yalu River and the Chinese border.
N: Zhou Enlai warns, through the Indian ambassador, that China will not sit back with folded hands and let the Americans come up to the border.
N: At Wake Island on October the fifteenth, MacArthur tells Truman that Chinese intervention is unlikely.

## SCENE 4 | The river at night
N: October the nineteenth, nineteen fifty. Three hundred thousand soldiers of the Chinese People's Volunteer Army cross the Yalu River at night. No vehicles, no fires, their food carried in tubes of dried millet.
N: By late November they surround the American Tenth Corps at the Chosin Reservoir: fifteen thousand American and United Nations troops encircled by sixty seven thousand Chinese. The temperature drops to minus thirty five degrees Fahrenheit. Weapons freeze. Morphine syrettes freeze.
N: In two weeks, four thousand three hundred eighty five Americans die and seven thousand three hundred thirty eight are wounded. The survivors fight a seventy eight mile breakout to the sea. Their general later calls it not a retreat, but an attack in a different direction.

## DOCUMENT | Mao Zedong | Reported by Peng Dehuai, quoted in Jung Chang and Jon Halliday, Mao: The Unknown Story | Late November 1950
N: Mao Zedong had sent his eldest son, Mao Anying, to Peng Dehuai's staff near the Chosin Reservoir. On November the twenty fifth, American bombers spotted a cooking fire and dropped napalm on the building. Mao Anying was twenty eight. When Mao Zedong was told, days later, this is what he said.
M: In a revolution, you have to pay a price.

## ASIDE
N: His son's body was buried in Korea. Mao never visited the grave.

## REST

## SCENE 5 | The wrong war
N: By January the fourth, nineteen fifty one, Seoul falls for the second time in six months. The front settles along the thirty eighth parallel by the middle of the year.
N: MacArthur wants more: bombing runs into China, a blockade of its coast, as many as thirty to fifty atomic bombs along the border. Truman fires him on April the eleventh.
N: He comes home to a ticker tape parade and an address to Congress.

## DOCUMENT | Douglas MacArthur | Address to a Joint Meeting of Congress | April 19, 1951
N: Eight days after dismissal, MacArthur addresses Congress. The line outlived the speech.
M: In war there is no substitute for victory.

## ASIDE
N: Matthew Ridgway replaces him and holds the line. The talks start at Kaesong in July of nineteen fifty one, move to Panmunjom, and last two years while diplomats argue over twenty one thousand Chinese and North Korean prisoners who refuse to go home. More than half the war's casualties happen inside that stalemate.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: A war that killed at least three million people does not even have one name. Americans call it the Korean War, or the Forgotten War. South Koreans call it six two five, the date it started. North Korea calls it the Fatherland Liberation War. China calls it the War to Resist American Aggression and Aid Korea.
N: Five accounts follow. Listen to each on its own terms first.

## PERSPECTIVE | American / UN Coalition | bystander
N: The United Nations authorized this force only because the Soviet Union was boycotting the Security Council that month, over its refusal to seat the People's Republic of China. Had the Soviet delegate been in his chair, the veto would have killed it. Twenty one nations sent troops. The United States alone supplied eighty eight percent of the non Korean forces.
N: Then came Inchon: five thousand to one against, and it worked. Within two weeks Seoul was recaptured and the North Korean supply lines were cut. The last clean American victory of the war.
N: When MacArthur pushed further, demanding bombs on Chinese cities and a blockade of the coast, Truman fired him. General Omar Bradley told the Senate what that decision had prevented.
M: The wrong war, at the wrong place, at the wrong time, and with the wrong enemy.

## PERSPECTIVE | North Korean / DPRK, Fatherland Liberation War | academic
N: In this account the war does not begin on June the twenty fifth, nineteen fifty. It begins in September of nineteen forty five, when American forces occupied the south, kept the Japanese colonial police, and outlawed the Korean People's Republic that independence activists had formed in Seoul.
N: Between nineteen forty eight and nineteen fifty, thirty thousand to one hundred thousand people died in political violence in the south before a single North Korean soldier crossed the parallel. June the twenty fifth, to this account, is not the start of a war but the escalation of one already underway.
N: What this account holds more fully than any other is the bombing. The Air Force dropped six hundred thirty five thousand tons of bombs on Korea, more than the entire Pacific theater of the Second World War, plus thirty two thousand five hundred fifty seven tons of napalm. Every North Korean city over fifty thousand people was destroyed.
N: Curtis LeMay ran that bombing campaign as head of Strategic Air Command. This is what he said, decades later.
M: We burned down every town in North Korea and some in South Korea, too. Over a period of three years or so, we killed off, what, twenty percent of the population of Korea.

## PERSPECTIVE | South Korean / ROK, 6.25 War | vanquished
N: South Koreans call this war by its date: Yugio, six two five. It marks a life the way an American generation marks one by September the eleventh.
N: What this account carries that the others do not, for half a century, is what its own government did. Between June and September of nineteen fifty, South Korean security forces executed members of the Bodo League, a registration list of suspected leftists whose names were already on file. The files became kill lists. The South Korean Truth and Reconciliation Commission, working from two thousand five to two thousand ten, documented between one hundred thousand and two hundred thousand killed.
N: Between July the twenty sixth and the twenty ninth, nineteen fifty, American soldiers killed an estimated two hundred fifty to three hundred refugees at a village called No Gun Ri. An Associated Press investigation confirmed it in nineteen ninety nine and won the Pulitzer Prize.
N: By the armistice, every South Korean city over fifty thousand people had been destroyed, income per person about sixty seven dollars, less than Ghana's one hundred seventy nine. What came after does not erase what came before.

## PERSPECTIVE | Chinese, War to Resist American Aggression and Aid Korea | academic
N: China's Politburo voted against intervening four times. The treasury was empty, the People's Republic was one year old, and the army had no air force and no navy. Mao's most capable commander, Lin Biao, declined the command on grounds of illness, a refusal widely read as political dissent.
N: Peng Dehuai took the command instead. On October the second, nineteen fifty, he told the Politburo why the risk had to be taken.
M: If the American troops reach our border, they could find a pretext at any time to invade.
N: Stalin had promised Soviet air cover, then withdrew it. The Chinese crossed the Yalu without it, without artillery superiority, without adequate winter clothing, carrying their food in cloth bandoliers of dried millet.
N: At the Chosin Reservoir, nine Chinese divisions under Song Shilun surrounded fifteen thousand United Nations troops in minus thirty five degree cold. Chinese casualties there: nineteen thousand two hundred two dead in battle, twenty eight thousand nine hundred fifty four more, most from frostbite. That they held, and pushed the front back to the thirty eighth parallel with worse equipment and no air cover, is the central fact of the war in Chinese memory.

## PERSPECTIVE | Civilian, Divided Families | vanquished
N: Lee Bok-soon was twenty two when the war began, in Kaesong, a city in the South before the war and the North after. Her husband had left for work in Seoul three weeks earlier. When the line shifted, he was on one side and she was on the other. She never saw him again. She is one of ten million.
N: In nineteen eighty three, a South Korean broadcast called Finding Dispersed Families ran four hundred fifty three hours over one hundred thirty eight days, the longest live television broadcast in history. A hundred thousand nine hundred fifty two people applied to appear. Ten thousand one hundred eighty nine were reunited.
N: One elderly South Korean woman on that broadcast said only this.
F: I just want to know if he is alive. That is all. Just whether he is alive.
N: Between nineteen eighty five and twenty eighteen, the Red Cross organized twenty one rounds of reunions at the Diamond Mountain resort in the North: three days, eleven hours, after decades apart. Four thousand seven hundred forty eight South Korean families took part before the program was suspended.
N: An estimated one hundred thirty three thousand South Koreans registered for the lottery. By twenty twenty four, more than half had died waiting.

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: What each of these leaves out.
N: The American account never mentions that its army photographed the Bodo League killings and kept the photographs classified.
N: The North Korean account leaves out that Stalin approved the invasion, and that Kim Il-sung asked him to.
N: The South Korean account is silent on the raids its own side ran before June the twenty fifth, and on the years of authoritarian rule that followed.
N: The Chinese account never says what happened to Peng Dehuai afterward. He was purged during the Cultural Revolution and died in a Chinese prison in nineteen seventy four.
N: And the divided families account has no interview and no broadcast for the North Korean families who lived the same separation.

## REST

## CLOSE
N: Lee Bok-soon applied for the reunion lottery in nineteen eighty five. She reapplied every year. She died in two thousand seven, at seventy nine years old, still waiting. Her husband's fate was never known.
