## OPEN
N: The movement that opened Japan to the world took power on a promise to throw the world out.
N: Its slogan was sonno joi. Revere the Emperor, Expel the Barbarians. That demand united the opposition to the shogunate through the eighteen sixties, and the barbarians were the Americans, and Britain, and France.
N: Within three years of winning, the leading men of the new government were on a ship to America, and then to Europe, for twenty two months.
N: And the emperor they had sworn to revere was fifteen years old and held no power at all.

## TITLE
N: The Meiji Restoration. Eighteen sixty eight to nineteen twelve.

## SCENE 1 | Four ships that go against the wind
N: The eighth of July, eighteen fifty three. Four warships come into Edo Bay under their own power, against the wind, which nobody on that shore has seen a ship do.
N: Their hulls are painted black, and the word that sticks to them is kurofune. Black ships.
N: Matthew Perry commands them. He carries a letter from President Millard Fillmore asking for trade, for coaling stations, and for the protection of shipwrecked sailors.
N: He will not deal with any official below the top rank. He fires his guns and calls it a salute, and gives them a year to think about it.

## DOCUMENT | Matthew Perry | Personal journal | 1853
N: Perry put in his own journal what the President's letter was too polite to say.
M: If the Japanese seek by any action of theirs, to oppose the march of American commerce, they must be taught a lesson.

## ASIDE
N: The treaties that followed fixed Japanese tariffs low, so no new industry could be protected, and granted extraterritoriality. A foreigner who committed a crime in Japan could not be tried in a Japanese court.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The gates at Kyoto
N: The third of January, eighteen sixty eight. Samurai from Satsuma, Choshu, Tosa and Hizen seize the gates of the Imperial Palace in Kyoto and announce that imperial rule has been restored.
N: The emperor in whose name they do it, born Mutsuhito, has been on the throne six weeks. His father died suddenly. He is fifteen.
N: The last shogun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, had already surrendered. His armies fight anyway.
N: At Toba-Fushimi, five thousand Satsuma and Choshu troops with modern rifles meet fifteen thousand Tokugawa troops carrying mostly swords and matchlocks. It is decided in four days.

## DOCUMENT | Emperor Meiji | Charter Oath, Article 5 | April 6, 1868
N: Three months after the gates, that fifteen year old swore five articles before the court. The fifth article of Emperor Meiji's Charter Oath became the motto of everything after it.
M: Knowledge shall be sought throughout the world so as to strengthen the foundations of imperial rule.

## ASIDE
N: The men running the government under that emperor were not restoring anything. Okubo Toshimichi. Kido Takayoshi. Ito Hirobumi. Yamagata Aritomo. They were assembling a modern state out of parts, and were about to go and collect them.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | The audit
N: Eighteen seventy one. Okubo, Kido and Ito leave with forty six other officials, on a mission led by Iwakura. Sixty students go too, five of them young women.
N: They go inside factories, shipyards, parliaments, courtrooms, postal systems, mines and museums. They meet Bismarck. They meet Grant. They meet Queen Victoria.
N: Kume Kunitake writes it up in five volumes. Not a travel diary. An audit of Western civilisation by men who meant to copy the results and reject the verdict about who ranked above whom.

## SCENE 4 | Eight years
N: Eighteen seventy one. The han are abolished. Two hundred and sixty semi autonomous domains become prefectures run from the centre.
N: Eighteen seventy two. Compulsory elementary education, for boys and for girls.
N: Eighteen seventy three. The Conscription Ordinance makes every male citizen a potential soldier, ending the samurai monopoly on arms. The same year, rice levies become cash taxes.
N: Eighteen seventy six. The Sword Abolishment Edict takes away the caste marker, and the samurai stipends, forty per cent of state revenue, become bonds that lose value fast.
N: Roughly one million nine hundred thousand people were told, inside eight years, that everything they were no longer counted.

## DOCUMENT | Fukuzawa Yukichi | An Encouragement of Learning | 1872
N: The book that carried the new order to ordinary readers was written by an educator, Fukuzawa Yukichi. It opens with one sentence.
M: Heaven does not create one man above or below another man.

## REST

## SCENE 5 | A hillside above Kagoshima
N: The twenty fourth of September, eighteen seventy seven. Shiroyama, a hillside above the castle town of Kagoshima.
N: Saigo Takamori is wounded in the hip in the final charge. His attendant, Beppu Shinsuke, beheads him and buries the head so it cannot be taken as a trophy.
N: Of the forty thousand samurai who joined him, about twenty thousand are dead. The army that beat them was the peasant conscript force Saigo had helped create.
N: Seven centuries of rule by a warrior class end in a bamboo thicket with a short sword.

## DOCUMENT | Saigo Takamori | Final letter, before Shiroyama | September 24, 1877
N: Saigo Takamori's last letter says one thing.
M: I will never forget the time I spent with you. Farewell.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of the same thirty years. Not five positions on whether it worked. Five questions about what it cost, and who paid.

## PERSPECTIVE | The oligarchs | victor
N: The first belongs to the men who ran it, and it rests on results.
N: By eighteen ninety nine the unequal treaties were renegotiated and extraterritoriality was finished. Japan defeated China in eighteen ninety five and took Taiwan. It defeated Russia in nineteen oh five, where Admiral Togo's fleet sank twenty one of thirty eight Russian ships at Tsushima.
N: Ito Hirobumi drafted the constitution after studying with Rudolf von Gneist in Berlin and Lorenz von Stein in Vienna, and chose the Prussian model deliberately. A strong executive, and rights the law could restrict.
N: Okubo Toshimichi set out the reasoning in a memorial on industry in eighteen seventy four.
M: If we wish to maintain our national independence among the powers of the world at the present time, it is not enough merely to open our eyes to the conditions of the various countries.

## PERSPECTIVE | The samurai | vanquished
N: The second says a class was deleted, and that the man who led its last stand was no reactionary.
N: Saigo Takamori was one of the three architects of the Restoration, alongside Okubo and Kido, and had commanded imperial forces in the Boshin War. He broke with the government over Korea and resigned.
N: His grievance was that the reformers had promised to restore the emperor and the old values, and built instead a bureaucracy for Western educated technocrats, discarding the men who made it possible.
N: And the order they replaced was not stagnant. Two hundred and sixty five years of internal peace. Male literacy estimated at forty to fifty per cent, comparable to Britain. Edo holding more than a million people by seventeen twenty.
N: The slogan those loyalists marched under puts the case in two lines.
M: The foreigner is the beast. The samurai is the shield.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | The Western observer | bystander
N: The third is kept in Western archives, and it indicts itself.
N: It opens with Perry as the external shock. Townsend Harris, the first American consul, wrote that his job was to bring a new country and a new people into the family of nations. Japan had been a country for a very long time.
N: Then it records what happened to that admiration once Japan succeeded. The defeat of China unsettled commentators who assumed Asian weakness was permanent. The defeat of Russia alarmed them. Kaiser Wilhelm the Second coined the phrase yellow peril.
N: And at Versailles in nineteen nineteen, Japan proposed a racial equality clause for the League of Nations Covenant. It won the vote, eleven to six. Woodrow Wilson, in the chair, ruled that it had not achieved unanimous consent and struck it from the record.
N: Harris had sent this to the State Department in eighteen fifty nine.
M: The Japanese are, in truth, a remarkable people. Their progress in all that constitutes national greatness is without precedent.

## PERSPECTIVE | The comparativist | academic
N: The fourth is not really about Japan. It is about why only Japan.
N: China met the same pressure earlier and harder. The Opium Wars began fourteen years before Perry. The Ottoman Empire, Qajar Persia, Siam and Ethiopia all faced Western expansion in the same decades. None came through with sovereignty and industry intact.
N: The answer this account gives is that Japanese feudalism helped. Two hundred and sixty domains competing for prestige meant Satsuma and Choshu were already buying Western weapons in the eighteen fifties. China's centralised bureaucracy had no such competition, and when Zeng Guofan and Li Hongzhang tried selective modernisation, the Empress Dowager Cixi undercut it.
N: The same scholarship refuses to call the outcome a democracy. E. H. Norman, writing in nineteen forty, called it a bourgeois revolution from above. The government suppressed the Freedom and People's Rights Movement, and the constitution left the Diet with advisory powers.
M: Japan was the pupil of Western civilization, but it was a pupil who intended to surpass its teachers.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Korean account | vanquished
N: The fifth begins with three warships.
N: The twenty sixth of February, eighteen seventy six, off Ganghwa Island, at the mouth of the river running up to Seoul. The Japanese envoy, Kuroda Kiyotaka, has brought eight hundred soldiers. The Treaty of Ganghwa is signed the next day, opening three ports and granting extraterritorial rights to Japanese nationals.
N: Every clause replicates what Perry imposed on Japan twenty two years earlier. That is this account's strongest fact and it does not need a second.
N: Japan annexed Korea in nineteen ten. Korean language teaching was banned in schools in nineteen thirty eight. From nineteen thirty two the military ran a network of sexual slavery stations across occupied Asia, and estimates of the women coerced run from twenty thousand to two hundred thousand.
N: On the twenty sixth of October, nineteen oh nine, a Korean independence activist named An Jung-geun shot Ito Hirobumi dead at Harbin railway station. He was executed five months later. This is what he said.
M: I am not afraid of death. I came here to fight for Korean independence and for peace in Asia.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: The oligarchs leave out everyone they drove over. More than two hundred peasant uprisings between eighteen sixty eight and eighteen eighty five, suppressed without altering one policy. The Ainu and the Ryukyuan people, assimilated by force. And women, given the schools and none of the politics.
N: The samurai account leaves out who paid for the samurai. That privilege rested on the other ninety three to ninety five per cent of the country, fixed at birth. And the unequal treaties were evidence of real military weakness, not an insult to be talked away.
N: The Western account leaves out Japan. It reads the transformation as a response to Perry, when the domains and the factions were domestic and already running.
N: The academic account leaves out what it felt like. Thirty five million people had their society rebuilt inside one lifetime, and that appears in no comparative table.
N: And the Korean account leaves out the railways, the hospitals and the schools the colonial government built, and how mixed that inheritance was. It leaves out the Koreans who worked inside that administration and did well from it.

## CLOSE
N: In eighteen seventy one, sixty students sailed with the mission that went to audit the West. Five were young women.
N: The youngest was six years old. Her name was Tsuda Umeko.
N: She came home, and in nineteen hundred she founded a university for women, in a country that had given girls compulsory schooling since eighteen seventy two and no political rights at all.
