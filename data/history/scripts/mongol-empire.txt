## OPEN
N: Think of one man, Genghis Khan, riding out of the steppe and putting all of Eurasia to the sword. The gates of Vienna. The House of Wisdom in Baghdad. The Yangtze river.
N: He died in August of twelve twenty seven, probably on campaign against the Xi Xia kingdom.
N: The Mongol army did not reach Hungary and Poland until twelve forty one, fourteen years after he was gone. Baghdad fell in twelve fifty eight, to his own grandson, thirty one years after he was gone. The conquest of southern China did not finish until twelve seventy nine, fifty two years after he was gone, under another grandson.
N: The empire everyone pictures inside one lifetime took three generations to build. The man whose name it carries saw less than half of it happen.

## TITLE
N: The Mongol Empire. Twelve oh six to thirteen sixty eight.

## SCENE 1 | The cangue
N: Look at the boy first, not the khan. His name was Temüjin. When he was nine, Tatars poisoned his father, Yesügei, and the clan that owed the family loyalty abandoned them on the steppe.
N: His half brother Begter challenged his authority. Temüjin killed him.
N: He was captured by the Tayichi'ud and forced to wear a cangue, a wooden collar locked around his neck, until he escaped with help from a family that sheltered him at the risk of their own lives.

## ASIDE
N: Every policy that followed traces back to this. The destruction of tribal aristocracies. The promotion of commoners by merit. The loyalty he demanded from everyone under him.
N: He did not inherit power. He built it from nothing, and he rebuilt Mongol society so that no one could be abandoned the way he had been.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | The Onon river, 1206
N: In twelve oh six, a kurultai, a gathering of Mongol tribal chiefs, met on the banks of the Onon river and proclaimed Temüjin as Genghis Khan, universal ruler. He was about forty four.
N: He had spent twenty years unifying the Mongol, Tatar, Merkit, Naiman and Kereit tribes, through conquest, through strategic marriages, through blood brotherhood, and through the destruction of rival aristocracies.
N: The confederation he had built held perhaps one million people. Within sixty years his grandsons would rule from the Pacific Ocean to the Danube.

## DOCUMENT | Attributed to Genghis Khan | Recorded by Rashid al-Din, Compendium of Chronicles | c. 1300
N: This is what Genghis Khan is recorded as saying was a man's greatest joy.
M: The greatest joy a man can know is to conquer his enemies and drive them before him, to ride their horses and take away their possessions, to see the faces of those who were dear to them bedewed with tears, and to clasp their wives and daughters in his arms.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | Otrar, 1219
N: The governor of Otrar executed a caravan of Mongol trade envoys. Under Mongol law, envoys were inviolate.
N: Genghis Khan invaded with somewhere between a hundred thousand and a hundred fifty thousand troops.
N: Otrar was razed. Bukhara was burned. Samarkand surrendered and was sacked anyway. Merv, Nishapur and Herat were destroyed so thoroughly that chroniclers counted the skulls in pyramids.

## ASIDE
N: To Genghis Khan, the governor's act was not a local crime. It was, by Mongol law, a declaration of war as clear as any that had ever been issued.

## DOCUMENT | Ibn al-Athir | Al-Kamil fi al-Tarikh | 1231
N: Ibn al-Athir wrote his history from Mosul, a city the Mongols never took.
M: In the space of a single year they seized the most populous, the most beautiful, and the best cultivated part of the earth whose inhabitants excelled in character and urbanity. This is a thing the like of which had not been heard.

## REST

## SCENE 4 | Karakorum, 1254
N: A Franciscan friar named William of Rubruck, sent by Louis the Ninth of France, reached the Mongol capital of Karakorum in twelve fifty four.
N: He found Nestorian Christian churches, Buddhist temples and mosques standing on the same streets.
N: In the khan's palace, a Parisian goldsmith named Guillaume Boucher, captured by Mongol forces in Hungary, had built a silver fountain that dispensed four different drinks through four lion heads.

## ASIDE
N: This was not tolerance in the modern sense. It was imperial pragmatism. But on the street, the effect was the same.

## REST | short

## SCENE 5 | Yamen, 1279
N: Kublai Khan's conquest of the Song dynasty took twenty years and ended at sea.
N: At the naval Battle of Yamen, the last Song emperor was an eight year old boy. When the battle was lost, his minister jumped into the water carrying the boy on his back.
N: Both of them drowned. China was reunified under foreign rule for the first time.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of one empire, and the same conquest reads differently depending on which side of it you were born on.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Mongol court | victor
N: The Mongol court's own record does not start with conquest. The Secret History of the Mongols traces Temüjin's origin to a blue grey wolf and a fallow doe who crossed the Tengis Sea, a claim to the steppe older than any city or scripture.
N: In this telling, the killing is consequence, not goal. Cities that surrendered were absorbed, their craftsmen and administrators folded into the Mongol system. Cities that resisted were destroyed as an example to the next city down the road.
N: The Yasa, the law code compiled at the twelve oh six kurultai, gave the empire something no contemporary state had: absolute religious freedom, inviolate diplomatic envoys, and promotion by merit over birth.
N: Genghis Khan himself described the terms he offered the people of Bukhara.
M: I am the punishment of God. If you had not committed great sins, God would not have sent a punishment like me upon you.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Chinese court | vanquished
N: The Jin dynasty's own numbers make the Chinese case better than any adjective could. The Jin had registered about fifty three million people in twelve oh seven. After the Mongol conquest, the registered population of northern China was eight and a half million.
N: Zhongdu fell in twelve fifteen after a siege that starved a city of one million. The Jin court fled to Kaifeng. When Kaifeng fell in twelve thirty three, the last Jin emperor hanged himself.
N: The Yuan dynasty that followed imposed a racial hierarchy with Mongols at the top and the Chinese majority at the bottom, and it suspended the examination system that had run Chinese government for a thousand years.
N: A report reached the Song court describing what the Jin territories looked like afterward.
M: The Tatars have utterly destroyed the northern regions. The bones of the dead cover the land like a great snowfall.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | The Persian world | vanquished
N: Juvaini wrote his history of the Mongol conquests while serving as governor of Baghdad under the very rulers he was describing. His position is the Persian case in miniature: survival through service, memory through complicity.
N: At Merv, he recorded that Tolui Khan ordered each Mongol soldier to execute three to four hundred prisoners. He put the dead at one million three hundred thousand.
N: The irrigation systems that had sustained Central Asian farmland for centuries were destroyed and never rebuilt. Some scholars believe the region did not recover to its pre Mongol population until the twentieth century.
N: This is Juvaini's own judgment on what he had lived through.
M: I say to you that no day in the history of the world has passed that has seen such slaughter.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Russian principalities | vanquished
N: When the papal envoy Giovanni da Pian del Carpine passed through Kiev six years after its fall, he counted fewer than two hundred houses still standing.
N: In December twelve thirty seven, Batu Khan's army crossed the frozen Volga into the Ryazan principality. The Ryazan princes sent to Vladimir for help. None came.
N: Kiev, the mother of Russian cities, had fallen in December twelve forty. What followed was two centuries of tribute, and Moscow's own rise is inseparable from it: Ivan the First served as the Golden Horde's chief tax collector in return for the right to collect from every other Russian prince.
N: Giovanni da Pian del Carpine wrote down what he found in Kiev's ruins.
M: We found lying in the fields countless skulls and bones of dead people, and the city has been reduced to nothing.

## PERSPECTIVE | The revisionist argument | academic
N: The connective case does not deny any of the numbers above. It argues they are not the whole story.
N: Before the Mongols, the Silk Road was a chain of local tolls under different powers. The Mongols unified it under one authority, and a letter could cross the empire in two weeks on the Yam relay system.
N: Chinese siege engineers served in the Ilkhanate's campaigns in the Middle East. Persian astronomers worked at Kublai Khan's observatory in Beijing.
N: Jack Weatherford put the case for why this gets left out of the story.
M: The Mongol Empire as a whole is the most important thing that didn't get into most people's view of world history.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: And here is what each one cannot hold at the same time as its own case.
N: The Mongol court's account calls the killing a consequence rather than a goal, and it says nothing about the forced population transfers, the enslavement, or the wars its own descendants fought each other over succession.
N: The Chinese account's own numbers are the strongest evidence in this episode, and it says little about the Chinese officials and merchants who served the Yuan willingly, or the cultural flourishing the Yuan patronized alongside the hierarchy.
N: The Persian account is written by a man on the empire's payroll, and it says less about the Ilkhanate's later conversion to Islam, or the Persian renaissance that the caliphate's own collapse helped enable.
N: The Russian account calls two centuries a yoke, and it says little about the Russian princes who collaborated with the Horde profitably, or the Orthodox Church, which the Horde exempted from tribute entirely.
N: And the revisionist case, built on postal stations and trade routes, is the one least willing to sit with its own conclusion: forty million dead cannot be balanced against a network, no matter how well the network worked.

## CLOSE
N: Genghis Khan's face is on Mongolian currency now, and his name is on the airport at Ulaanbaatar.
N: Fifty four kilometers east of the capital, a stainless steel statue of him on horseback stands forty meters tall.
N: In China, he is classified as a Chinese emperor, the founder of a legitimate dynasty. In Iran, Iraq and Afghanistan, he remains a byword for destruction.
N: A two thousand three genetic study found that sixteen million men alive today carry a Y chromosome lineage traced to his family line.
