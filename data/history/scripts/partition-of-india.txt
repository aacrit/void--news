## TURN | Five accounts
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Here is the part that does not resolve. Five accounts of what happened here mostly agree about the documents, and disagree about which documents are the story. Each gets its own case first.

## PERSPECTIVE | British Imperial | victor
# MOOD: procedure
N: The British account is administrative. Both Congress and the Muslim League accepted the plan. The Indian leaders chose the commissions and drafted their terms. The Viceroy kept out of the award; Ismay's letter says so.
N: On the speed, the argument is uncomfortable but serious. Administrative collapse was already under way, and a slower handover might have killed more people. Attlee told the Commons they were constrained by urgency.

## PERSPECTIVE | Indian Nationalist | victor
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The Indian nationalist account holds that Congress never wanted partition and accepted it only to stop independence itself being delayed indefinitely. Freedom came with a partition, not because of one.
N: It points at what the violence tends to bury: five hundred and sixty two princely states integrated, a secular constitution written, and the dead counted in hundreds of thousands, from witnesses.

## PERSPECTIVE | Pakistani Nationalist | victor
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The Pakistani account begins with an election. In nineteen forty six, the Muslim League won four hundred and forty six of the four hundred and ninety five seats reserved for Muslim voters. That is close to a mandate, and it is the strongest fact in this case. On the state, Jinnah addressed its assembly three days before it existed.

## TURN | The shape of the disagreement
# MOOD: reckoning
N: None of those five is a lie. Each is a true account, and each has a hole in it.
N: The British account leaves out what the Viceroy's own staff wrote about Gurdaspur before the award, and the strength at which the Governor found the Force. The Indian account leaves out the Muslim dead of the east, carried in its own count only as an equal number, believed. The Pakistani account leaves out the award's two stated grounds, and the Sikh note of nineteen forty two that called Gurdaspur balanced by an older census.
N: The subaltern account leaves out the state building, the institutions, the treasury on the other side of the line. The women's account leaves out the recovered women who wished to return, and the men killed in the same columns.
N: And none of the five is a Sikh account, though the Viceroy said on the fourth of June that the plan divided that community into two almost equal parts.
N: The holes are not random. Each account omits the thing that would cost its own side the most. That is the reason to hear all five, and to say which is missing.

## CLOSE
# MOOD: grief
N: The Radcliffe Line left a hundred and sixty two enclaves stranded on the wrong side of the border with what became Bangladesh. They were not resolved until twenty fifteen. In nineteen seventy one, Pakistan itself broke, and Bangladesh became a third country born from the first partition.
N: Both states hold nuclear weapons now. They have fought four wars. Kashmir is still contested.
N: One census, taken in wartime. Any map, read with it. Five weeks.
