# standard of docs/proposals/HISTORY-FIRST-LISTENER.md (O-1..O-8), after the CEO's note on
# the eight Orpheus episodes: the voice is right, but is a newcomer told where, when, who
# and why? The fifth Orpheus 3B episode (narrator "tara"; a male reader of record for M:
# lines, a female reader for F: lines), in the Ken Burns register: short declarative
# sentences, documents read plainly, the silence placed by the writer. Re-cut from the live
# script against the evidence ledger (data/history/evidence/scramble-for-africa/) and the
# published thesis (data/history/theses/scramble-for-africa.md): every line the document
# voice reads is verbatim from a stored extract, and the extract id follows the marker in a
# comment. Sources are the event YAML, the ledger and the thesis, nothing else.
# What the revision adds: an establishing passage at the head of SCENE 1 (the continent,
# the year, the powers at the table and the Africans who were not, what happened in thirty
# years, the two states left standing); a role with every name on its first mention
# (Bismarck the German Chancellor, Fitzmaurice answering for the Foreign Office, Uzoigwe a
# historian of the UNESCO General History, Rhodes Prime Minister of the Cape Colony,
# Labouchere a member of Parliament, Leopold King of Belgium, Casement the British consul,
# Trotha commanding the German troops, Kariko an under-chief, Kamatoto a servant to a German

# now Zimbabwe, Bolobo a town on the upper Congo); every SCENE opening on a time and a place;

# the motif. "Indunas" is gone: the record uses the word without defining it, and Lobengula's
# own letter calls the two men his messengers, so the record's word is the one spoken.
# "South West Africa" is not spoken: the ASR splits it, and the YAML's "present-day Namibia"
# licenses the plain name. To pay for the orientation within H-07: the Kamatoto aside now
# runs as trailing narration inside the Trotha DOCUMENT, and the short REST after SCENE 2
# and the REST after SCENE 3 are gone (the silence grammar prices every segment); the Pan
# African account's own refusal
# and "the concession itself is not held here" fold into the how-we-know line; "By the
# Crown's record" becomes "by the British record of treaties" (a name the YAML carries); the
# OPEN's thirty-years line moves to SCENE 1; the second TURN runs at 15 to 25 words a line;
# the 2004 paper leaves the CLOSE (SCENE 6 carries it). The Casement girl is spoken as
# "T seven" because the Blue Book's letter and figure are read aloud, not printed. There is
# no clip slot: this episode has no admitted recording. There is no F: line: no stored

# his Cape Dutch and Ferry from his French, and the narration says so before each. Where
# the record disagrees with itself (whether Berlin partitioned anything, what the Congo's
# people died of, how many Herero died) the script states both and chooses neither. Where
# a figure is known to this programme only from the event YAML, the narration says "the
# event record". Lines beginning with "#" are inert: the parser skips them

## OPEN
# MOOD: dread
N: On the twenty sixth of February, eighteen eighty five, fourteen governments signed a General Act at Berlin. It names no border and gives no territory to anyone.
N: Six documents, no border, and nobody from Africa in the room.

## TITLE
# MOOD: dread
N: The Scramble for Africa. Eighteen eighty four to nineteen fourteen.

## SCENE 1 | The table
# MOOD: procedure
N: Berlin, the fifteenth of November, eighteen eighty four. In eighteen seventy Europe held about ten per cent of Africa.
N: Otto von Bismarck, the German Chancellor, opens a conference on Africa at his residence: fourteen governments, European and American, and no African of any kind.
N: It writes the rules by which a European power may claim African land; within thirty years, by the event record, those powers hold over ninety per cent of it.
N: On the twentieth, in London, Lord Edmond Fitzmaurice, answering for the Foreign Office in the House of Commons, is asked about Portugal's claims on the Congo, the great river of Central Africa.

# MOOD: procedure

M: I have already stated that the decision of territorial claims is not among the bases of the Conference.

## SCENE 2 | The paper
# MOOD: testimony
N: Matabeleland, in what is now Zimbabwe, the thirtieth of October, eighteen eighty eight. Lobengula, king of the Ndebele, grants a concession, a right to dig, to the British South Africa Company of Cecil Rhodes, Prime Minister of the Cape Colony.
N: Told what he has granted, the king sends two messengers to find out whether England exists and has a queen.

## DOCUMENT | Lobengula | letter to Queen Victoria, as Henry Labouchere read it to the House of Commons | 1889
# extract: src-hansard-18931109.p546
# MOOD: testimony
N: They carry a letter. Lobengula, to Queen Victoria, as Henry Labouchere, a member of Parliament, read it to the House in eighteen ninety three.
M: A document was read and presented to me for signature. I asked what it contained, and was told that in it were my words and the words of those men. I put my hand to it.

## DOCUMENT | Lobengula | second letter to Queen Victoria, as Labouchere read it to the House of Commons | August 10, 1889
# extract: src-hansard-18931109.p547
# MOOD: testimony
N: On the tenth of August Lobengula writes again.
M: If the Queen hears that I have given away the whole country it is not so. I have no one in my country who knows how to write.
N: In October, Labouchere told the House, the Company received a royal charter to govern the land, with no further word to Lobengula. In eighteen ninety three its guns settled it, by the event record; the country became Rhodesia.

## SCENE 3 | Hornkrans
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Hornkrans, in what is now Namibia, the thirtieth of May, eighteen ninety. Hendrik Witbooi, chief of the Witbooi Nama, has learned that Maharero, chief of the Herero, has given himself into German protection.
N: The two peoples are at war; Witbooi writes to his enemy anyway.

M: you will have everlasting regret, that you have given your land and your right of government into the hands of the white people

## SCENE 4 | The river
# MOOD: procedure
N: The Congo, nineteen oh three. The Congo Free State, which governs the river basin, is the personal colony of Leopold the Second, King of Belgium, by the event record; its people must bring in rubber.
N: In May the House of Commons asks the Government to confer with the other signatories of the Berlin Act; that year Roger Casement, the British consul, goes up the river.

## DOCUMENT | Roger Casement | the statement of a girl the report calls T 7, as the consul set it down in the third person, Africa No. 1 (1904) | 1903
# extract: src-casement-1904.p75

M: they began catching and tying the people, and killed lots of them.
N: At Lake Mantumba the consul saw a boy of eleven or twelve whose right hand had been cut off at the wrist.

# MOOD: dread
N: Namibia, then a German colony, nineteen oh four. The Herero have risen. Daniel Kariko, an under-chief, swore afterwards to the cause, before the British administration that followed.

N: An addendum tells the troops how to read it.
M: the shooting at women and children is to be understood as shooting above their heads, so as to force them to flee.
N: It assumes that no more male prisoners will be taken.

N: In December nineteen oh five a new governor called the survivors in; those who came went to the camps.

## TURN | Five accounts
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Five accounts of what was done and what to call it, argued from what this programme could read: the Act, parliamentary papers, three rulers' letters, a consul's report, the Free State's own inquiry, a German order and a German parliament's paper.
N: The standard books, and the concession Lobengula signed, could not be read here. Each gets its own case first.

# MOOD: procedure
# extract: src-lugard-1922.p617

N: Frederick Lugard, a British colonial governor, called it a dual mandate in nineteen twenty two.
M: Let it be admitted at the outset that European brains, capital, and energy have not been, and never will be, expended in developing the resources of Africa from motives of pure philanthropy

## PERSPECTIVE | The kingdoms that resisted | vanquished
# MOOD: reckoning
# extract: src-menelik-1897.p425
N: The second account is the rulers' own. Three speak here in their own words: two have been heard; the third kept his throne.
N: At Adwa, on the first of March, eighteen ninety six, Menelik the Second, Emperor of Ethiopia, defeated the Italians, and Ethiopia kept its independence.
N: The next year Menelik wrote to James Rennell Rodd, with whom he had made a treaty in Amharic and English, on which text should settle a dispute. In the Foreign Office translation.
M: let this translation, which is written in the French language, and which I enclose in this letter, be the witness between us, and if you accept this proposal, send me word of your acceptance by letter.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Pan African critique | revisionist
# MOOD: reckoning
# extract: src-uzoigwe-1985.p30
N: The third account holds that the Scramble was not an episode but a structure that outlived the flags.
N: Its texts are Walter Rodney on how Europe underdeveloped Africa, Frantz Fanon on the colonized, and Kwame Nkrumah, Ghana's first president, who named the continuation neo-colonialism.
N: The UNESCO historians argue it from the conference itself. Uzoigwe, on what Berlin did.
M: To all intents and purposes the appropriation of territory did take place at the conference and the question of future appropriation is clearly implied in its decisions.

## PERSPECTIVE | The economic ledger | academic
# MOOD: procedure
# extract: src-roes-2010.p12-13
N: The fourth account follows the money through rubber, diamonds and copper; this programme holds documents for rubber alone.
N: The consul counted places he had known: Bolobo, a town on the upper Congo, fully forty thousand people once, believed to be seven or eight thousand.
N: Nothing in the primary record counts the territory entire. The historian Aldwin Roes, who names ten million as the campaign's figure, writes this.
M: a firm estimate of the number of victims must indeed remain elusive

## PERSPECTIVE | North Africa's own war | vanquished
# MOOD: reckoning
# extract: src-ferry-1885.secalger.en
N: The fifth account is the half the word leaves out. North Africa was taken by the same powers in the same decades.
N: The event record carries its resistance: Abd el-Kader, who fought the French in Algeria; the Mahdi, who took Khartoum in Sudan; Omar al-Mukhtar, who fought the Italians in Libya.
N: It holds that this resistance drew on Islamic political and military traditions with no equivalent south of the Sahara.
N: The one North African document held is by Jules Ferry, a former premier of France, defending the conquest of Algeria in eighteen eighty five, rendered by Void from his French.
M: When we went to Algiers to destroy piracy and to secure the freedom of trade in the Mediterranean, were we doing the work of pirates, of conquerors, of devastators?
N: The account answers with a count from the event record: an estimated eight hundred and twenty five thousand dead in the conquest of Algeria.

## TURN | What each one leaves out
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Each is true as far as it goes.
N: The colonizers leave out what their own records say was done in the basin the Act protected: the hostages, and the hands.
N: The rulers leave out the wars they fought with one another, and the rulers who did sign.
N: The Pan African account leaves out what African governments did with the structure after independence.
N: The economic ledger leaves out the share of the dead the consul himself gave to disease.
N: North Africa's own account leaves out the elites who worked with the powers it fought.
N: And no Congolese, no North African, no Herero woman, no Nama survivor speaks here in their own words.

# MOOD: grief

## CLOSE
# MOOD: grief
N: Fourteen governments signed the Act at Berlin in eighteen eighty five; no African was in the room. Within thirty years Europe held over ninety per cent of Africa; Ethiopia and Liberia alone were independent.
N: The Congo passed from its king to Belgium in nineteen oh eight. Germany recognised the Herero and Nama genocide in twenty twenty one, by the event record.
N: The report that printed the Herero affidavits was withdrawn in nineteen twenty six from libraries throughout the British Empire; in South Africa and Namibia copies were destroyed.
N: Six documents, no border, and nobody from Africa in the room.
