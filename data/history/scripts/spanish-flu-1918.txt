## SCENE 1 | The camp
# MOOD: rupture
N: September, nineteen eighteen, and the First World War is in its final campaigns. The war has built the roads a disease travels: troopships and railways carry millions of young men between continents, and an influenza goes with them.
N: In the spring it was mild and governments ignored it. By late summer the virus had changed; now it kills the young and the healthy in the greatest numbers.
N: Later figures for its dead will run from fifteen million to a hundred million; none is a count.
N: Camp Devens, an army camp outside Boston, in Massachusetts. On the twenty fourth a soldier writes home that he has kept quiet about being sick.

## CLOSE
# MOOD: grief
N: The influenza of nineteen eighteen travelled with the First World War and reached every inhabited continent. Its dead were estimated, never counted: about seven million in British India in one year, seven thousand five hundred and forty two in Western Samoa, and for the world a range from fifteen million to a hundred million.
N: Britain's Ministry of Health, looking back in nineteen twenty, wrote that the disease simply had its way. It came, the report says, like a thief in the night and stole treasure.
