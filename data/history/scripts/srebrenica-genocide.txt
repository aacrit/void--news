## OPEN
N: A safe area, the Security Council called it. A resolution passed on the sixteenth of April, nineteen ninety three: free from any armed attack or any other hostile act.
N: The same resolution asked for more peacekeepers, with a view to monitoring the humanitarian situation.
N: At any one time there were no more than six hundred of them. They never fired directly on the forces that took the town.
N: A resolution is a sentence. It cannot stop a tank.

## TITLE
N: The Srebrenica genocide. July, nineteen ninety five.

## SCENE 1 | The enclave
N: The first United Nations troops arrive two days after the vote, lightly armed, a much smaller force than was first requested. They man thirteen observation posts, and a main compound about five kilometres north of the town, at Potocari.
N: Around them, between one thousand and two thousand soldiers from three brigades of the Bosnian Serb Drina Corps, with tanks, artillery and mortars. The tribunal and the Secretary-General's report give the same figure.
N: Inside, the Bosniak twenty eighth division, three to four thousand men with no heavy weapons, the report says.
N: From the outset, the tribunal found, both parties violated the safe area agreement.

## SCENE 2 | Potocari, July eleventh
N: By the evening of July ninth, the Drina Corps is one kilometre short of the town. Late that night Radovan Karadzic authorises it to capture Srebrenica.
N: The Dutch commander, Thom Karremans, sends urgent requests for NATO air support. At least three, and possibly five, are turned down at various levels of the chain of command.
N: On the afternoon of July eleventh, two NATO aircraft drop two bombs.
N: The Bosnian Serb army radios a threat to shell the town and the compound, and to kill the Dutch soldiers it holds. The Dutch defence minister telephones to ask that the air action stop. It is halted.
N: Late that afternoon, General Ratko Mladic walks through the empty streets with his officers, among them Radislav Krstic. A Serbian journalist films it.

## DOCUMENT | Thom Karremans | Report after meeting Mladic, as quoted in the Secretary-General's report, paragraph 315 | July 1995
N: After two meetings with Mladic, Thom Karremans sent a report. The Secretary-General's report quotes how it ended.
M: there are now more than 15,000 people within one square kilometre, including the battalion, in an extreme vulnerable position: the sitting duck position, not able to defend these people at all

## ASIDE
N: By the evening of July eleventh, the tribunal found, twenty to twenty five thousand people had gathered at Potocari. Several thousand had pressed inside the compound. The rest spread through the neighbouring factories and fields.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | The factory, then the fields
N: The evacuation from Potocari begins on the afternoon of July twelfth, with the Dutch battalion's cooperation. The Bosnian Serbs separate the men from the other refugees.
N: The men who never went to Potocari had left the enclave on foot shortly after midnight that morning, single file through the minefields. There may have been up to fifteen thousand of them.
N: On the afternoon of July thirteenth, between one thousand and one thousand five hundred men captured from that column are bussed or marched to a warehouse at Kravica.
N: At around six in the evening, when it is full, the soldiers start throwing grenades and shooting into the men packed inside.
N: The tribunal's judgment lists nine mass execution sites. The Jadar River and the Cerska valley. Kravica. Tisca. Orahovac. Petkovci. The Branjevo Military Farm. The Pilica Cultural Dom.
N: Eight are dated, from the thirteenth to the sixteenth of July. The ninth, Kozluk, carries no date in its heading.

## DOCUMENT | Drazen Erdemovic | Statement at his guilty plea, in the court's English, ICTY Sentencing Judgement, paragraph 10 | 1996
N: At Branjevo, from ten in the morning, the men were taken off the buses in groups of ten and lined up with their backs to a firing squad. Drazen Erdemovic was in it. When he pleaded guilty, he told the tribunal what happened when he refused. In the court's English:
M: When I refused, they told me: 'If you're sorry for them, stand up, line up with them and we will kill you too.'

## ASIDE
N: The shooting went on until about three in the afternoon. The court records that he believes he personally killed about seventy people.

## REST

## SCENE 4 | The graves
N: From nineteen ninety six, the tribunal's prosecutors dig. By two thousand they have exhumed twenty one graves. Fourteen are primary graves, where bodies went straight after the killing. Eight of those have already been dug up, the bodies taken elsewhere.
N: At ten sites, at least four hundred and forty eight blindfolds. At thirteen, four hundred and twenty three ligatures, most of them wire. These, the tribunal held, are inconsistent with combat casualties.

## SCENE 5 | The second grave
N: In September and early October of nineteen ninety five, Bosnian Serb forces dig up many of the primary graves and rebury the bodies in still more remote places. The tribunal read it as a concerted campaign to conceal the bodies.
N: The move broke the corpses apart. The tribunal found a minimum of two thousand and twenty eight bodies in the graves exhumed, and put the total likely within seven to eight thousand men.
N: In two thousand twenty four, a General Assembly resolution recited a figure of at least eight thousand three hundred and seventy two lives lost. That is a recital, not a count of its own.

## DOCUMENT | ICTY Trial Chamber | Prosecutor v. Radislav Krstic, Judgement, paragraph 598 | August 2, 2001
N: In two thousand one, the Trial Chamber that tried Radislav Krstic gave the crime its legal name.
M: The Chamber concludes that the intent to kill all the Bosnian Muslim men of military age in Srebrenica constitutes an intent to destroy in part the Bosnian Muslim group within the meaning of Article 4 and therefore must be qualified as a genocide.

## ASIDE
N: The resolution that made Srebrenica a safe area opens by taking note of an order of the International Court of Justice, eight days old. It said Yugoslavia should take all measures within its power to prevent the crime of genocide.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of Srebrenica. One of them is not a rival version of events. It is the case made by those who deny the crime was genocide, in their own words, set down here to be answered, not balanced.

## PERSPECTIVE | Bosniak Survivors and the Legal Record | vanquished
N: The men at Kravica speak in this record through witnesses the tribunal names only by letters. The Krstic judgment records that Witness K was only slightly wounded, and crossed the warehouse to escape through a window after the shooting stopped. It quotes him.
M: I was not even able to touch the floor, the concrete floor of the warehouse… After the shooting, I felt a strange kind of heat, warmth, which was actually coming from the blood that covered the concrete floor, and I was stepping on the dead people who were lying around.
N: The judgments followed. In two thousand four, the Appeals Chamber held that the forces targeted for extinction the forty thousand Bosnian Muslims living in Srebrenica. It found Krstic guilty of aiding and abetting genocide, and sentenced him to thirty five years.
N: In two thousand seventeen, the chamber that tried Mladic convicted him of genocide and sentenced him to life.

## PERSPECTIVE | The UN Safe Area and the Dutch Peacekeepers | bystander
N: The United Nations' own account begins with the Council. The safe areas, the Secretary-General's report says, were established without the consent of the parties and without any credible military deterrent.
N: The tribunal counts the peacekeepers at no more than six hundred. The report counts the fighting men among them at one hundred and fifty, lightly armed, facing two thousand Serbs with armour and artillery.
N: It is not possible to say with any certainty, the report says, that stronger action by the battalion would have saved lives.
N: In nineteen ninety nine, the Secretary-General's report on the fall of Srebrenica said this.
M: Through error, misjudgement and an inability to recognize the scope of the evil confronting us, we failed to do our part to help save the people of Srebrenica from the Serb campaign of mass murder.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | Genocide Denial versus the International Juridical Finding | revisionist
N: The denial position, in its strongest form, is an argument about intent. In two thousand twenty one, Marko Atlagic and Aleksandar Martinovic wrote that they searched, and could not find a single statement by a Republika Srpska official, before the war, during it, or during the operation, saying they wanted to destroy the Muslim population.
N: They write that the Bosnian Serb army passed through forty two Muslim villages without leaving a single civilian dead. In Serbian, they put the real number of Muslim dead at between eight hundred and one thousand two hundred, and call the figure of eight thousand exaggerated and politically constructed.
N: They also write that Bill Clinton told Alija Izetbegovic at least five thousand Muslims must be killed for NATO to bomb the Serbs.
N: Their English summary opens this way.
M: There was no genocide against the Muslim population in Srebrenica in July 1995 because genocide did not occur. Crimes were committed in Srebrenica. The Republika Srpska Army did not intend to commit genocide.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Mothers of Srebrenica | vanquished
N: A foundation called the Mothers of Srebrenica, and others with it, took the Netherlands and the United Nations to court. In two thousand thirteen, the European Court of Human Rights found the grant of immunity to the United Nations was not disproportionate.
N: In two thousand nineteen, the Supreme Court of the Netherlands held that the state was not liable for the fall of the town. But at the end of the afternoon of July thirteenth, male refugees were still inside the compound, about three hundred and fifty by the Dutch courts' count. On them, the court found this.
M: Dutchbat failed to offer the male refugees in the compound the choice of staying behind there, even though that was possible. As a result, Dutchbat withheld from these male refugees the chance of escaping from the Bosnian Serbs. That was wrongful.
N: It put that chance at ten per cent, and limited the state's liability to ten per cent of the damage suffered by their surviving relatives.

## PERSPECTIVE | Scholarship on Ethnic Cleansing in the Yugoslav Wars | academic
N: The phrase was in the Council's own resolution in April of nineteen ninety three. It condemned the forced evacuation of civilians from Srebrenica as part of the Bosnian Serb party's campaign of ethnic cleansing.
N: Where ethnic cleansing ends and genocide begins is a legal line. The chamber that convicted Mladic of genocide acquitted him on the other genocide count, in the municipalities, finding the killers there lacked the intent to destroy a substantial part of the group.
N: Scholars press on that line. Hikmet Karcic cites the Islamic Community's report of May nineteen ninety five, which counts fifty one imams in the Srebrenica area, and reads their killing, from nineteen ninety two, as one segment of a genocide against the Bosniaks of the Drina valley.
N: The Appeals Chamber said why that line is hard to cross.
M: The gravity of genocide is reflected in the stringent requirements which must be satisfied before this conviction is imposed.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: What each of these does not carry.
N: The legal record settles what was done to the captured men, not what the Council, the peacekeepers or the Bosnian government could have done before July eleventh.
N: The United Nations' account passes over the perpetrators' chain of command, and leaves the count of the dead to the tribunal.
N: The denial account asks for a statement of intent. The tribunal did not rest on one: it found the intent to kill all the Bosnian Muslim men of military age in Srebrenica was itself an intent to destroy the group in part. And the account's ceiling of one thousand two hundred dead sits below the two thousand and twenty eight bodies counted by two thousand, before a single unexhumed grave.
N: The mothers' case reaches only the men inside the compound, and a suit against a state cannot name as defendants the men who fired.
N: And the scholarship's count of the imams rests on its own war reports and testimonies. No judgment or United Nations document in this record counts them.

## REST

## CLOSE
N: The agreement on the peacekeepers' status, of the fifteenth of May, nineteen ninety three, required a claims commission: the one place an individual could seek a remedy against the United Nations for what its force did or failed to do.
N: In two thousand thirteen, the European Court of Human Rights looked for it. It would appear, the court wrote, that this has not been done.
