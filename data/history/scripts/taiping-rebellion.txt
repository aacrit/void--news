## OPEN
N: You think the worst war of the nineteenth century was fought over empire, or slavery, or land.
N: This one started with a written exam. A man sat for it in Canton four times, in eighteen twenty eight, eighteen thirty six, eighteen thirty seven, and eighteen forty three, and failed every time.
N: After the fourth failure, he reread a stack of Christian tracts he had once set aside, saw a series of visions, and decided he was the younger brother of Jesus Christ, sent to destroy demon worship in China.
N: Between twenty and thirty million people were dead before it was over. More than World War One.

## TITLE
N: The Taiping Rebellion. Eighteen fifty to eighteen sixty four.

## SCENE 1 | Jintian village, January 1851
N: January the eleventh, eighteen fifty one, Jintian village in Guangxi province. Hong Xiuquan declares the Taiping Tianguo, the Heavenly Kingdom of Great Peace.
N: His following is already tens of thousands strong: Hakka farmers, miners, charcoal burners, and former secret society members, displaced by population pressure and the disruption of the First Opium War.
N: The Confucian establishment that failed him at the exam hall has become, in his preaching, the demonic order he is called to overthrow.
N: Within two years his army sweeps north through Hunan and reaches the Yangtze.

## DOCUMENT | Hong Xiuquan | Imperial decree, Taiping Tianguo | 1853
N: That same year, Hong Xiuquan issued an imperial decree naming himself sovereign of every nation on earth.
M: God the Heavenly Father has sent me to descend into the world to become the true Sovereign of all nations. Both China and the barbarian nations are mine.

## ASIDE
N: The decree did not stop at China. It named Britain and America too, barbarian nations under the same sovereignty.
N: That claim mattered later, when the West had to decide whether to help him, or destroy him.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | Nanjing, March 1853
N: March the nineteenth, eighteen fifty three. The Taiping army takes Nanjing, a city of five hundred thousand people.
N: Hong Xiuquan enters through the gates carried in a golden palanquin. He is thirty nine years old. He renames the city Tianjing, the Heavenly Capital.
N: At its peak the Taiping state governs approximately thirty million people. Private land ownership is abolished. Opium, alcohol, tobacco, gambling, and prostitution are banned outright. Foot binding is banned. Women are admitted to the civil service examinations and given military command.
N: The social program is enforced by terror. Violators are executed. The liberation and the tyranny come from the same source.

## DOCUMENT | British consular report | Report on the fall of Nanjing to the Taiping | 1853
N: A British consular report described what happened when the gates fell.
M: The rebels entered the city and put to fire and sword all who opposed them. Not a Chinese soldier was left alive within the walls.

## ASIDE
N: The city renamed for heaven had just seen its Qing garrison erased to take it. Terror was not a side effect of this revolution. It was how the revolution was enforced.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | Nanjing, September 1856
N: September, eighteen fifty six, inside the Taiping capital. Yang Xiuqing, the Eastern King and the movement's chief strategist, claims to receive direct revelations from God the Father, a claim that challenges Hong's own authority as the Son.
N: Wei Changhui, the Northern King, kills Yang and twenty thousand of his followers in a single night.
N: Hong then has Wei killed. Shi Dakai, the Assistant King and the Taiping's best field commander, leaves Nanjing in disgust, taking one hundred thousand troops with him. He is captured and executed by the Qing seven years later.
N: The movement that preached the Heavenly Kingdom devours its own architects. It never recovers Yang's strategic mind, or Shi's army.

## REST

## SCENE 4 | Zeng Guofan's tent, Hunan
N: The Qing dynasty's own armies, the Manchu Eight Banners and the Green Standard Army, cannot stop the Taiping.
N: Zeng Guofan, a Confucian scholar from Hunan, raises an army from nothing, the Xiang Army, funded through provincial taxes and trained as a professional force.
N: Li Hongzhang builds a second one, the Anhui Army. Both are loyal to their commanders, not to Beijing.
N: These provincial armies defeat the Taiping. The precedent they set, provincial military power independent of the central government, outlives the war that produced it.

## SCENE 5 | Nanjing, July 1864
N: Hong Xiuquan dies on June the first, eighteen sixty four, possibly from illness, possibly by swallowing gold leaf.
N: Nanjing falls on July the nineteenth. Zeng Guofan's forces kill an estimated one hundred thousand people within three days.
N: Hong's teenage son, the Young Monarch, is captured and executed. The last organized Taiping resistance ends two years later, in eighteen sixty six.
N: Fourteen years after Jintian, the deadliest civil war in human history is over.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: Five accounts of the same fourteen years, and the disagreement is not about the death toll. It is about what the Taiping were, and who is to blame for what they cost.

## PERSPECTIVE | Taiping Heavenly Kingdom | vanquished
N: This is the account of the movement itself, and it says the social program was never a cover story. It was the point.
N: The Hakka heard a theology of equality before God and the destruction of idols, and it spoke to people already denied the best land by the Cantonese-speaking majority.
N: The Land System of the Heavenly Dynasty proposed the most radical redistribution of property in Chinese history: all land held by God, redistributed by family size.
N: Women were admitted to the Taiping's own civil service examinations, the first in Chinese history. Hong Xiuquan's own sister, Hong Xuanjiao, led troops in combat during the early campaigns.
N: Hong Xiuquan's own land decree stated its purpose plainly.
M: All shall eat food, all shall have clothes, money shall be shared, and in all things there shall be equality. No one shall be without food or warmth.

## PERSPECTIVE | Qing Imperial | victor
N: This is the account of the dynasty that survived, and it says the Taiping were never a legitimate government. They were bandits who stole the trappings of a foreign religion.
N: The Qing's own regular armies had already failed. The Manchu Eight Banners had not fought a real campaign in decades, and the Green Standard Army was riddled with fraud.
N: Zeng Guofan answered with Confucian order: discipline, loyalty, scholarship, against what this account calls Taiping millenarian chaos. By eighteen sixty he commanded one hundred and twenty thousand troops and controlled the revenue of four provinces.
N: Zeng Guofan called the scholars of Hunan to arms in a proclamation.
M: These bandits steal the ways of the foreign barbarians and worship their God. They call themselves brothers and sisters. They destroy the temples of our sages. How can any scholar-official remain silent?
N: In this account, the massacre at Nanjing was the suppression of criminals, not a war crime against belligerents.

## PERSPECTIVE | Western Missionary / Diplomatic | bystander
N: This is the account of the outsiders who came to watch, and hoped, and then chose against what they were watching.
N: A Christian state conquering China from within looked, at first, like everything three hundred years of mission work had failed to achieve.
N: Issachar Jacox Roberts, who had briefly tutored Hong in Canton, traveled to Nanjing in eighteen sixty and stayed thirteen months. He left in January of eighteen sixty two.
N: The diplomatic calculation mattered more than the theological one. The Treaty of Tientsin, extorted from the Qing during the Second Opium War, gave Britain and France the trade concessions they wanted, and a Taiping victory would have voided them.
N: Frederick Townsend Ward, an American adventurer from Salem, Massachusetts, organized the Ever Victorious Army, Chinese soldiers under Western officers and Western weapons, and won a string of victories before he was killed at the Battle of Cixi in eighteen sixty two. Charles George Gordon, a British officer, replaced him.
N: Roberts wrote his verdict in a letter from Nanjing.
M: I find him to be a crazy man, entirely unfit to rule without any organized government or code of laws.

## PERSPECTIVE | Marxist / PRC Historiography | academic
N: This is the account written by the state that came next, and it does not ask who was right in eighteen fifty three. It asks what the rebellion was actually for.
N: Mao Zedong called the Taiping revolutionary predecessors, part of a lineage of Chinese peasant rebellion stretching back two thousand years, one only the Communist Party would finally complete.
N: The land program was the centerpiece of that rehabilitation. Chinese Marxist historians argued it anticipated socialist land reform by a century, and blamed its failure on the leadership's feudal habits, not its ideas. Hong Xiuquan, in this telling, withdrew into his palace and accumulated concubines, eighty eight of them by some accounts, while the revolutionary program stagnated.
N: During the Cultural Revolution, the Taiping's attack on Confucianism, the destroyed temples and burned classics, was reframed as its most revolutionary act. Historians called it a prefiguring of the assault on the Four Olds a century later.
N: Mao set the lineage down in writing in nineteen thirty nine.
M: The Taiping Uprising was the first great peasant war in Chinese history to raise the slogan of equality and the abolition of the feudal land system.

## PERSPECTIVE | Demographic / Humanitarian | academic
N: This account does not ask who was right. It asks what the number costs.
N: Between twenty and thirty million people died, more than the deaths of World War One, in the deadliest civil war ever recorded.
N: The provinces of Jiangsu and Zhejiang, the economic heart of Qing China, lost between fifty and seventy percent of their populations.
N: The killing ran in both directions. Zeng Guofan's armies were known for what were called the three cleanings: kill clean, burn clean, loot clean.
N: The war overlapped with separate uprisings elsewhere in China at the very same time. The combined death toll across all of them may exceed fifty million.
N: A British naval officer aboard HMS Furious, unnamed in the record, described the Yangtze valley in eighteen sixty one.
M: The whole countryside for miles around was a scene of desolation. Not a living soul was to be seen, not a house left standing.

## REST | short

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: What each of these accounts leaves outside its own frame.
N: The Taiping account leaves out the violence its own forces used against civilians, Buddhist monks, and Confucian scholars, and that its land reform was never fully carried out.
N: The Qing account leaves out its own military's corruption, the thing that let the rebellion grow at all, and the hundred thousand people killed at Nanjing after the city had already fallen.
N: The Western account leaves out its own complicity in the opium trade that helped destabilize China in the first place, and the Second Opium War it was fighting at the very same time.
N: The Marxist account leaves out the religious character of a movement built on visions and a claimed brotherhood with Christ, and the authoritarian machinery underneath its revolutionary language.
N: And the demographic account, so careful with the numbers, says almost nothing about the ideology those numbers were fought over, or the reforms the Taiping actually tried to build.
