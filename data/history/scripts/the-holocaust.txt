## OPEN
N: The thing most people get wrong about this is not whether it happened. It is how.
N: The picture in most heads is a secret, improvised in the dark by a small number of monsters, hidden from everyone not in the room.
N: The record says otherwise. The record is a meeting that lasted ninety minutes, a railway that billed by the kilometre, and a fare schedule with a children's discount.

## TITLE
N: The Holocaust. Nineteen forty one to nineteen forty five.

## SCENE 1 | Ninety minutes at Wannsee
N: The twentieth of January, nineteen forty two. A villa on a lake outside Berlin.
N: Fifteen men sit down at a table. Reinhard Heydrich chairs. Adolf Eichmann takes the minutes. The meeting runs ninety minutes and then they have lunch.
N: The agenda is the final solution of the Jewish question. The minutes list the Jewish population of every country in Europe, eleven million people.
N: The list includes Britain at three hundred and thirty thousand, neutral Switzerland at eighteen thousand, Ireland at four thousand. Germany held none of them.
N: The word killing does not appear anywhere in the document. The words used are evacuation, natural diminution, and treated accordingly.
N: Eight of the fifteen men at that table held doctorates.

## ASIDE
N: And the meeting did not decide anything. That is the part that matters.
N: By January nineteen forty two the killing had been running for seven months. Wannsee was not a decision. It was a coordination meeting, called so that the ministries would stop getting in each other's way.

## REST

## SCENE 2 | Before the camps
N: The first phase was not industrial at all. It was men with rifles at the edge of holes.
N: From June nineteen forty one the Einsatzgruppen followed the army into the Soviet Union, killing Jewish communities in ravines and forests and ditches.
N: At Babi Yar, outside Kyiv, on the twenty ninth and thirtieth of September nineteen forty one, one unit shot thirty three thousand seven hundred and seventy one people in two days.
N: The reports that came back complained of a problem, and the problem was the effect on the shooters. Himmler watched an execution at Minsk and ordered more humane methods.
N: More humane for the men doing it. That request produced the gas van, and then the gas chamber.

## REST

## SCENE 3 | Six hundred metres by four hundred
N: Treblinka was a rectangle of about six hundred metres by four hundred. You could walk its perimeter in twenty minutes.
N: It was run by twenty to thirty SS men and about a hundred auxiliaries, most of them Soviet prisoners of war who had volunteered rather than starve.
N: Between July nineteen forty two and October nineteen forty three, roughly nine hundred thousand people were killed there, at a peak of twelve to fifteen thousand a day.
N: There were no crematoria. The bodies were burned in the open, on grates built out of railway track.

## SCENE 4 | The supply chain
N: None of it could run without the ordinary machinery of a modern state, and that machinery billed for its work.
N: The German state railway moved people to the camps in freight cars and charged the SS a group fare, per person, per kilometre, the way it would price any excursion.
N: The census that identified who was Jewish by ancestry was run on punch card machines supplied by the German subsidiary of IBM.
N: And IG Farben built a synthetic rubber plant beside Auschwitz to use the prisoners as labour. The average life expectancy of a worker at that plant was three months.
N: This was not a secret kept from German industry. It was a set of contracts.

## DOCUMENT | Primo Levi | If This Is a Man | 1947
N: Primo Levi was an Italian chemist, twenty four years old, taken as a partisan and sent to that rubber plant in February nineteen forty four. Of the six hundred and fifty Italian Jews on his transport, ninety six men and twenty nine women were selected for labour. The rest were killed that day. He survived eleven months and weighed eighty five pounds at liberation.
M: If this is a man. Who works in the mud. Who knows no peace. Who fights for a crust of bread. Who dies at a yes or a no.

## REST

## TURN | Five accounts
N: What follows is five accounts, and one thing has to be said before they begin.
N: These are not five positions on whether this happened. There is no such argument, and this programme will not stage one. They are five vantage points on the same documented fact, and each one sees something the others cannot.

## PERSPECTIVE | The survivors | vanquished
N: The first is testimony, and its authority is that the people giving it were there.
N: What it describes is not only cruelty. It is the deliberate removal of a person: the shaving, the number tattooed on the arm, the name replaced by it. Primo Levi's number was one seven four five one seven.
N: And it refuses to make itself comfortable. Levi wrote at length about what he called the grey zone, where staying alive required complicity, where prisoners were made to burn other prisoners, where a man might take bread from someone who was dying.
N: His own verdict on his own book was that the survivors are not the true witnesses. The complete witnesses, he wrote, are the ones who did not come back.
N: Elie Wiesel arrived at Birkenau from Romania in May nineteen forty four, aged fifteen. His mother Sarah and his younger sister Tzipora were killed on arrival. His father died at Buchenwald in January nineteen forty five. Forty years later he accepted the Nobel Peace Prize with an instruction.
M: For the dead and the living, we must bear witness.

## PERSPECTIVE | The bureaucracy | academic
N: The second account reads the crime through its paperwork, and it is the reason we know as much as we do.
N: In nineteen sixty one Adolf Eichmann sat in a bulletproof glass booth in a courtroom in Jerusalem, a thin bald man in a grey suit, and answered for the deportation of millions. He spoke almost entirely in the passive voice. He cited regulations. He described himself as a transportation specialist.
N: Hannah Arendt covered that trial and took from it the phrase the banality of evil, and she was precise about it. Not that Eichmann was ordinary. That he had replaced moral thought with administrative obedience, and that the substitution was available to very many people.
N: Raul Hilberg, who served in the American unit that captured the railway records, traced it as a process in four stages. Definition, who counts as Jewish. Expropriation, take the property. Concentration, into the ghettos. Then annihilation.
N: Thirty copies of the Wannsee minutes were made. Twenty nine were destroyed. The one that survived was found in a Foreign Office file in nineteen forty seven.

## REST | short

## PERSPECTIVE | The bystander nations | bystander
N: The third account is about the countries that were not doing the killing.
N: In July nineteen thirty eight, delegates from thirty two countries met at a hotel in Evian to discuss Jewish refugees. The Dominican Republic offered to take a hundred thousand. No other country made a comparable offer. The Australian delegate explained that his country had no racial problem and was not anxious to import one. The American quota stayed at twenty seven thousand three hundred and seventy a year.
N: A German newspaper summarised the conference in three words. Nobody wants them.
N: Then there is Jan Karski, a Polish Catholic courier for the underground, taken into the Warsaw ghetto in October nineteen forty two and, in a stolen uniform, into a transit camp where he watched people loaded into cattle cars.
N: He carried it to London and told the Foreign Secretary. He carried it to Washington in July nineteen forty three and told the President.
M: I told him about the plight of the Jews. He didn't say a word.
N: He also told a Supreme Court justice, who was himself Jewish, and who answered that he did not say Karski was lying. He said he was unable to believe him, and that there was a difference.
N: By December nineteen forty two the Allies had confirmed enough to issue a joint declaration condemning a bestial policy of cold blooded extermination. Nothing military followed. In nineteen forty four, requests to bomb the rail lines into Auschwitz, or the crematoria, were refused.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Zionist answer | academic
N: The fourth account is about what the survivors concluded, and what was built on it.
N: David Ben-Gurion told the Jewish Agency in December nineteen forty two, eight days before the Allied declaration, that he estimated two million Jews were already dead. The Jewish community in Palestine dropped thirty seven parachutists into occupied Europe in nineteen forty four. Seven were captured and executed.
N: Before the war, Zionism was one Jewish answer among several. There was the Bund, Jewish socialism, rooted in Poland. There was assimilation, strongest in Germany and Austria.
N: After nineteen forty five those arguments were not defeated. Their constituencies were dead. The Bund's heartland had been destroyed, and the assimilated Jews of Berlin and Vienna had been killed alongside the ones who never assimilated.
N: In nineteen forty six about two hundred and fifty thousand Jewish survivors were living in displaced persons camps inside occupied Germany and Austria, with no country asking for them. The case that Jews could only be safe in a state with an army became, for many of them, simply obvious.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Roma | vanquished
N: The fifth account has the thinnest archive, and the reason it is thin is part of the account.
N: The Romani word for it is Porajmos. The Devouring.
N: Between two hundred and twenty thousand and five hundred thousand Roma and Sinti were murdered. The range is that wide because Roma were never systematically counted, many carried no papers, and after the war no government was in a hurry to find out.
N: The apparatus was not invented by the Nazis. Bavaria opened a Gypsy Information Service in eighteen ninety nine. The Weimar Republic fingerprinted Roma and made them carry identity cards. The regime inherited the files and expanded them.
N: A racial hygiene unit in Berlin classified thirty thousand Roma by racial purity. Its researcher wrote a doctoral thesis on Romani children in foster care, concluding they could not be educated. Many of those children were later sent to Auschwitz.
N: A family camp operated at Birkenau from February nineteen forty three. Families were kept together, not out of mercy but because they were wanted as specimens. On the night of the second of August nineteen forty four the SS liquidated it. Two thousand eight hundred and ninety seven people were gassed, and witnesses reported that the Roma fought them with improvised weapons and with their hands.

## REST

## TURN | What each one leaves out
N: And now what each account cannot see from where it stands.
N: The survivors' account names its own gap, which is why it is trustworthy. It is the account of the people who lived, and Levi said plainly that the complete witnesses did not.
N: The bureaucratic account can make the crime sound like a machine with nobody driving it. The banality of evil describes the clerk. It does not describe the men who decided, and the first of Hilberg's four stages is not a procedure. It is a choice.
N: The bystander account, taken alone, quietly moves the subject. A rail line not bombed is not the crime. And the defence that nobody could have known does not survive contact with Jan Karski.
N: The Zionist account leaves out what the founding of the state did to the people already living there. The displacement of Palestinian Arabs is part of this history, not a separate one.
N: And the account of the Roma, in insisting on one forgotten group, sits beside others. The eleven million includes disabled people killed under the euthanasia programme, and more than three million Soviet prisoners of war.

## CLOSE
N: The word genocide did not exist until nineteen forty four, when a Polish Jewish lawyer named Raphael Lemkin built it out of Greek and Latin, because there was no word for what was being reported.
N: Fifty two thousand survivors have since recorded their testimony.
N: Anne Frank died at Bergen-Belsen, of typhus, a few weeks before the camp was liberated. She was fifteen.
N: And the railway, when it invoiced the SS for the transports, charged the full adult rate above ten, half for children under ten, and nothing at all for children under four.
