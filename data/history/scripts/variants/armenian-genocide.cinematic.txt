# Cinematic variant, 2026-10-04. The eighth Orpheus 3B episode (narrator "tara"; a male
# reader of record for M: lines, a female reader for F: lines), in the Ken Burns
# register: short declarative sentences, documents read plainly, the silence placed by
# the writer. Re-cut from the live script against the evidence ledger
# (data/history/evidence/armenian-genocide/) and the published thesis
# (data/history/theses/armenian-genocide.md): every line the document voice reads is
# verbatim from a stored extract, and the extract id follows the marker in a comment.
# Sources are the event YAML, the ledger and the thesis, nothing else. Lines beginning
# with "#" are inert: the parser skips them (pipeline/history/script_format.py::parse_script).
# The mood directive on every segment is the one docs/proposals/HISTORY-AUDIO-ARCHIVAL.md
# defines. There is no clip slot: this episode has no admitted recording, and a candidate
# belongs in the ledger, not here. Two women speak in F: lines, both from stored extracts:
# the deported woman of Kheiban, whose words an Armenian of Moush wrote down on 25 July
# 1915 (the narration says so), and Aurora Mardiganian, whose memoir was written up with
# H. L. Gates (the narration says so). The German documents are read in the English
# translation the Gust edition publishes, and every such read says "in the Gust
# translation"; the words Talaat spoke to the German embassy and to Morgenthau are read
# as those two men reported them, said aloud each time, and the diary's variant of the
# memoir's sentence is stated beside it. The Ottoman pamphlet of 1916 and Rustem's book
# are held in French with no rendering, so the state's own words are narrated from them
# and read from the foreign ministry's page. The live script's Jesse Jackson dispatch,
# its Hitler sentence in the event record's wording, and its Lemkin and Mardiganian
# quotations are in no extract this programme holds (the Nuremberg volume prints the
# Hitler line in other words and the attribution is disputed, so it is narrated with that
# said). Where the record disagrees with itself (how many Armenians there were, how many
# died, what the deportation was for) the script states the counts with their producers
# and chooses none. This file is not the live script: nothing globs this directory.

## OPEN
# MOOD: dread
N: Constantinople, the seventh of July, nineteen fifteen. The German ambassador writes to his chancellor that the government is pursuing its purpose of eradicating the Armenian race from the Turkish Empire.
N: Three days later the American ambassador telegraphs Washington that the measures are directed from Constantinople, often in districts where no military operations are likely.
N: One government was the ally of the Porte, the other neutral, and neither report cites the other.
N: The ally wrote it down. The neutral wrote it down. Neither of them stopped it.

## TITLE
# MOOD: dread
N: The Armenian genocide, nineteen fifteen to nineteen twenty three.

## SCENE 1 | The night of the twenty fourth
# MOOD: rupture
N: Constantinople, the night of the twenty fourth of April, nineteen fifteen. Ottoman police arrest two hundred and thirty five Armenians: writers, clergy, physicians, editors, lawyers.
N: By the end of May the number reaches six hundred. Lepsius, a German pastor, wrote that eight were released again and the rest vanished without trace.

## DOCUMENT | Talat Pasha (Talaat Bey), Ottoman Minister of the Interior, as reported by Hans von Wangenheim, German Ambassador at Constantinople | Report No. 372 to the Reichskanzler, 17 June 1915, in the English translation of the Gust edition | June 17, 1915
# extract: src-gust-19150617-en.para4
# MOOD: procedure
N: The seventeenth of June. Hans von Wangenheim, the German ambassador, reports that the interior minister, Talat, has spoken without reservation to an embassy official. As reported by Wangenheim, in the Gust translation.
M: that the Porte is intent on taking advantage of the World War in order to make a clean sweep of internal enemies
N: The banishment of the Armenians, the ambassador concludes, is not motivated by military considerations alone.
N: On the seventh of July he reports the expulsion reaching Trebizond, Sivas and a third province, none threatened by any invasion, and names the purpose: eradicating the Armenian race from the Turkish Empire.

## DOCUMENT | Henry Morgenthau Sr., American Ambassador at Constantinople | Telegram 841 to the Secretary of State | July 10, 1915

# MOOD: dread

M: These measures are not in response to popular or fanatical demand, but are purely arbitrary and directed from Constantinople in the name of military necessity, often in districts where no military operations are likely to take place.
N: Side by side on six points, the two reports agree on five.
N: The ally wrote it down. The neutral wrote it down. Neither of them stopped it.

## SCENE 2 | The road
# MOOD: grief
N: Erzurum, the fifth of August. Scheubner-Richter, the German administrator there, writes that the plains hold only women, children and old men, driven out towards Mesopotamia and attacked on the road by Kurds and Turkish volunteers.
N: He puts the dead on that road at ten to twenty thousand; the government, he writes, at three to four thousand.

## DOCUMENT | Walter Rössler, German Consul at Aleppo | Report to the Reichskanzler, Aleppo, 27 July 1915, in the English translation of the Gust edition | July 27, 1915
# extract: src-gust-19150727-en.para5
# MOOD: grief
N: Aleppo, the twenty seventh of July. Rossler, the German consul, reports bodies floating past on the Euphrates for twenty five days, in the Gust translation.
M: The corpses were all bound together in the same way, two by two, back to back. This regularity indicates that they were not the result of butchery, but had been killed intentionally by the authorities.

## SCENE 3 | The woman from Kheiban
# MOOD: testimony
N: Moush, the twenty fifth of July. An Armenian named Vartkes, in hiding, writes down what a woman tells him that night, for an Armenian journal a British compilation reprinted in nineteen sixteen.
N: They thought she was a ghost, he writes. She had saved her two-year-old boy.

## DOCUMENT | A deported woman of the village of Kheiban, Moush district, as recounted to Vartkes of Moush | Narrative recorded on 25 July 1915, published in the Armenian journal Van-Tosp, reprinted in The Treatment of Armenians in the Ottoman Empire, 1915-16, Document 24 | July 25, 1915
# extract: src-bryce-1916.p92
# MOOD: testimony
N: Her words, as told to him and as he wrote them down. Village after village had been gathered into one company of eight thousand to ten thousand.
F: Our company moved on slowly, leaving heaps of corpses behind. Most of us were almost naked.
F: They gave us bread once every other day, though many did not get even that. When all our provisions were gone, we gathered wheat from the fields and ate it. Many a mother lost her mind and dropped her baby by the wayside.

## SCENE 4 | The counts
# MOOD: grief
N: In May nineteen sixteen the American Committee for Armenian and Syrian Relief computed between four hundred and fifty thousand and eight hundred and fifty thousand dead, and called six hundred thousand safe. This programme states no count as the number.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Ottoman government and the Turkish state | victor
# MOOD: procedure
# extract: src-mfa-tr.para7
N: The first account is the state's: a pamphlet of nineteen sixteen, a former ambassador's book of nineteen eighteen, and the page of the foreign ministry.
N: The pamphlet, in French, holds that the army commander, finding the Armenians making common cause with the enemy, ordered the Armenian element in localities considered military zones transferred southward, to secure the rear of his troops.
N: Rustem Bey, formerly ambassador at Washington, argued that hundreds of thousands of Ottoman Armenians still existed, so there had been no extermination.
N: The Republic holds the line. Armenians in or near the war zone, its foreign ministry writes, were relocated away from the supply routes of the advancing Russian army, and greater numbers of Turks died in those years.
N: The ministry, on the plan and on the word.
M: Nevertheless, no authentic evidence exists to support the claim that there was a premeditated plan by the Ottoman Government to kill off Armenians.
M: However, it is factually problematic, morally unsound and legally unfounded to call this episode a “genocide.”

## PERSPECTIVE | The survivors and the Republic of Armenia | vanquished
# MOOD: testimony
# extract: src-mardiganian-1918.p81-82
N: The second account is the survivors', and the record holds two women: the woman from Kheiban, and Aurora Mardiganian, fourteen when the gendarmes came for her family.
N: Her memoir, Ravished Armenia, as told to H. L. Gates, was published in nineteen eighteen. She describes the first dawn of her convoy, when Kurdish riders came down on the camp.
F: The riders quickly gathered up all the donkeys and horses belonging to the families, and when these had been driven off they dismounted and began to walk among us and pick out young women to steal.
F: Lusanne and I clung close to mother, who tried to hide us, but one of three Kurds who walked near us saw me.
N: The Republic of Armenia, in its declaration of independence, stands in support of international recognition of the nineteen fifteen genocide.
N: Armenian scholarship reports that in July nineteen nineteen an Ottoman court-martial sentenced four men to death, Talat and Enver among them. Three of twenty death sentences were carried out.

N: The third account belongs to the foreigners who wrote it down. The memoir of Morgenthau calls the deportation a new method of massacre.
M: When the Turkish authorities gave the orders for these deportations, they were merely giving the death warrant to a whole race; they understood this well, and, in their conversations with me, they made no particular attempt to conceal the fact.

## PERSPECTIVE | Genocide scholarship | revisionist
# MOOD: reckoning
# extract: src-kurt-2016.para1
N: The fourth account is the scholars', and most of its books are closed to this programme: Suny, Akcam, Bloxham and Kieser are held by title only.
N: What is held is three open articles and a German preprint. Umit Kurt, in twenty sixteen.
M: During World War I, mass murder, deportation, and forced assimilation were three faces of the same process of destruction organized by the Young Turk regime.
N: Whether the design came before the war or in the spring of nineteen fifteen, the field is divided, and the archive that could settle it is not here.

## PERSPECTIVE | The Convention and the parliaments | academic
# MOOD: procedure
# extract: src-un-convention-1948.art2
N: The fifth account is the law's, and it begins without the word. The Allied declaration of May nineteen fifteen spoke of crimes against humanity and civilization.
N: The Treaty of Sevres of nineteen twenty bound Turkey to hand over those responsible for the massacres, and was never ratified.
N: Raphael Lemkin published the word in nineteen forty four, in a book this programme could not open. The United Nations General Assembly adopted the Convention in nineteen forty eight. One of its acts is this.
M: Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part
N: The European Parliament resolved in nineteen eighty seven that the events constitute genocide within the meaning of the convention, and that no claim against present-day Turkey follows.
N: France adopted a law of recognition in two thousand one. The United States used the word in twenty twenty one, for one and a half million dead.

## TURN | What each one leaves out
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Each is true as far as it goes.
N: The account of the state explains a war zone, not Trebizond, Sivas, or towns fifty miles from the capital, and its count of the Muslim dead rests on a comparison no document here can test.
N: The survivors have no Armenian archive of their own here: the woman from Kheiban comes through a British reprint, and the memoir was written up for American readers.
N: The witnesses pass over the conspiracy a German vice-consul called a fact, and the risings the pamphlet of the government describes.
N: The law has no judgment. And no Kurdish participant speaks here, though the consuls and the deported woman both name Kurds as the killers on the road.
