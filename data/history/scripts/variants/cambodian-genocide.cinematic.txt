## SCENE 1 | The road
# MOOD: rupture
N: The seventeenth of April, nineteen seventy five. Phnom Penh, the capital of Cambodia, in Southeast Asia, between Vietnam and Thailand.
N: The Khmer Rouge, a communist movement led by Saloth Sar, known as Pol Pot, have taken the city from its military government. Their state will call itself Democratic Kampuchea.
N: Within forty eight hours the soldiers march at least two million people out at gunpoint, saying it is for three days, until the Americans stop bombing.
N: The United States bombed eastern Cambodia from nineteen sixty nine to nineteen seventy three; it had already stopped.
N: Chum Mey, a mechanic, walks out asking to see Angkar and is told to move on; he could not find, he said, who Angkar was.

## SCENE 5 | The count
# MOOD: grief
N: Phnom Penh, August nineteen seventy nine. The government Vietnam has installed holds a tribunal and charges about three million dead, on a survey of one city, one commune, and a journalist's figures from three provinces.
N: Nobody knows how many died. What exists is a set of counts that disagree, each with a name on it.
N: In nineteen ninety nine three experts the United Nations General Assembly had asked for put the scholars at one and a half million and nearly one point seven million, about twenty per cent of the population. Historians, they wrote, had rejected two to three million.

## DOCUMENT | Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia, Trial Chamber | Case 002/01 Judgement, paragraph 174 | August 7, 2014

N: In two thousand fourteen the Trial Chamber surveyed the estimates it had been given, and wrote this.
M: Overall, estimates indicate that between 600,000 and 3 million died as a result of Khmer Rouge policies and actions. Within this range, experts accept estimates falling between 1.5 and 2 million excess deaths as the most accurate.
N: In two thousand eighteen the same court added that the absence of reliable statistical data leaves inherent uncertainty around any number. This programme states no single number.

## SCENE 6 | The word
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Phnom Penh, the same August. The tribunal defines genocide by decree, to include forced evacuation, forced labour and the abolition of religion, and finds it committed against the whole people.
N: In nineteen ninety nine the experts apply the genocide convention of nineteen forty eight instead, the treaty that defines the crime as acts meant to destroy a national, ethnic, racial or religious group.
N: They name the Cham, Cambodia's Muslim minority, the Vietnamese and the Buddhist monkhood, and stop at the Khmer, the majority.
