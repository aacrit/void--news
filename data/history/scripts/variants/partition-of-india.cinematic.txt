# First-time-listener variant, 2026-10-04. The approved cinematic script
# (partition-of-india.cinematic.txt, untouched) revised to the orientation standard of
# docs/proposals/HISTORY-FIRST-LISTENER.md, after the CEO's note on the eight Orpheus
# episodes: the voice is right, but is a newcomer told where, when, who and why? Same
# cast (narrator "tara"; a male reader of record for M: lines, a female reader for F:
# lines), same Ken Burns register, same rules. What is added: an establishing passage
# at the head of SCENE 1 (the country, the year, the two new states and their names,
# the religious basis of the division, the two provinces the line must cross), a role
# with every name on its first mention, a gloss with every term a newcomer would not
# know (Viceroy, Dominion, Constituent Assembly, princely state, tehsil, award, Congress
# and the League, West Punjab), a one-line "how we know" before the five accounts, and a
# CLOSE that restates the core facts as particulars. Every orientation fact is from the
# event YAML, the evidence ledger (data/history/evidence/partition-of-india/) or the
# published thesis (data/history/theses/partition-of-india.md), and nothing else:
# Rule 1. Every line the document voice reads is byte-for-byte a stored extract, and the
# extract id follows the marker in a comment. The Nehru clip slot is carried unchanged
# from the cinematic variant, signed in the ledger. To pay for the orientation, these
# were cut from the cinematic variant: the Mountbatten press-conference READ (its fact
# is narrated in SCENE 3, so the public/private contrast survives without the second
# voice), Rajendra Prasad (a name without a role a newcomer could use), the line on
# larger death figures in unread books (its substance survives in the women's account),
# the Governor's 13 August letter in the Force aside, the second Hindustani clause, the
# "signed and dated" aside (the CLOSE now carries it), the elders' trailing line, and a
# sentence each from the Indian account and the second TURN. Four short asides now run
# as trailing narration inside the DOCUMENT segment they follow, as the Cambodian
# variant does, because the silence grammar prices every segment. A gloss of "lakh" was
# written and cut: the record defines it only by example, so H-18 cannot see the value.
# Lines beginning with "#" are inert: the parser skips them
# (pipeline/history/script_format.py::parse_script). This file is not the live script:
# nothing globs this directory.

## OPEN
# MOOD: dread
N: There is a border in South Asia, between India and Pakistan, that the two countries have fought four wars across. Both hold nuclear weapons.
N: You would assume a line like that was drawn slowly, by people who knew the ground.
N: It was drawn in five weeks, by a man who had never been to India, from one census, six years old.
N: One census, any map, and five weeks.

## TITLE
# MOOD: dread
N: The Partition of India. August, nineteen forty seven.

## SCENE 1 | The map
# MOOD: procedure
N: The summer of nineteen forty seven. India is British, and Britain is leaving.
N: On the third of June the Prime Minister reads the plan to the House of Commons in London: from the fifteenth of August, two independent Dominions, two new countries, India and Pakistan.
N: The division runs on religion: Muslim-majority districts to Pakistan, the rest to India. Two provinces hold both kinds, the Punjab in the west and Bengal in the east.
N: The line through them is not drawn. One sentence of the statement decides what it will be made of.

## DOCUMENT | Clement Attlee | statement to the House of Commons | June 3, 1947
# extract: src-hansard-19470603.p36
# MOOD: procedure
N: Clement Attlee, the Prime Minister of Britain, reading the statement to Parliament.
M: For the purpose of determining the population of districts, the 1941 census figures will be taken as authoritative.

## ASIDE
# MOOD: procedure
N: A census taken in wartime, six years before.
N: Two commissions are to draw the line, one for the Punjab and one for Bengal. Their secretaries asked for a map, and Sir George Abell, who answered them, sent none: any map, read with the figures of nineteen forty one, would suffice.
N: Cyril Radcliffe, a British barrister who had never been to India, chaired both. He had until the fifteenth of August.

## DOCUMENT | Sir Cyril Radcliffe | the Punjab award, paragraph ten | August 12, 1947
# extract: src-radcliffe-punjab.para10
# MOOD: procedure
N: Cyril Radcliffe, in his decision, which the record calls the award.
M: I have hesitated long over those not inconsiderable areas east of the Sutlej River and in the angle of the Beas and Sutlej Rivers in which Muslim majorities are found.
N: He wrote that he had hesitated long. He had five weeks. One census, any map, and five weeks.

# MOOD: grief

## SCENE 3 | Gurdaspur
# MOOD: reckoning
N: One district of the Punjab explains why the line is still argued about. In Gurdaspur every count puts the Muslim share a little over half, and the judges of the commission could not agree which count should rule.
N: On its northern edge lies Kashmir, a princely state with a ruler of its own, so placed that it could join either country.
N: Lord Mountbatten, the last Viceroy of India, overseeing the handover, told the press on the fourth of June that the commission was unlikely to put the whole of Gurdaspur on the Pakistani side.

## DOCUMENT | Record of an interview between Lord Mountbatten and the Nawab of Bhopal, by the Viceroy's staff | The Transfer of Power, volume XII, number 335 | August 4, 1947
# extract: src-top-xii.item335
# MOOD: reckoning
N: In private, two months later and eight days before the award was signed, Mountbatten received the Nawab of Bhopal, a prince with a state to place. The record his staff kept has him saying this.
M: Kashmir, which was so placed geographically that it could join either Dominion, provided part of Gurdaspur were put into East Punjab by the Boundary Commission.

## ASIDE
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The award gave India three of the four tehsils that make up Gurdaspur, citing a canal and a hydro-electric scheme. Its reasons do not mention Kashmir.
N: Lord Ismay, answering for the Viceroy, told Liaquat Ali Khan, who protested from the Pakistani side, that the Viceroy had said from the outset he must have nothing to do with the commissions.
N: Nothing in the record shows that sentence reaching Radcliffe. This programme stops where the record stops.

## REST
# MOOD: grief

## SCENE 4 | How many
# MOOD: grief
N: Nobody knows how many people died. What exists is a set of counts that disagree, each with a name on it.
N: The Fact Finding Organisation of the Government of India examined nearly fifteen thousand witnesses, from two thousand and ninety four of the nineteen thousand nine hundred and fourteen villages of West Punjab, the half that went to Pakistan.
N: Khosla, who wrote the report, said the census of nineteen forty one was not accurate even when compiled.

## DOCUMENT | G. D. Khosla, Fact Finding Organisation report | Stern Reckoning | 1949
# extract: src-khosla-1949.p299
# MOOD: grief
N: Khosla, for the Fact Finding Organisation.
M: Taking these factors into consideration the loss of non-Muslim life has been estimated at a figure between 200,000 and 250,000. It is believed that an equal number of Muslims perished in the course of the riots in India.
N: One body, counting one side and believing the other.
N: A Sikh committee's compilation of nineteen fifty put the dead at about a million; the economists Bharadwaj and Mirza write of nearly a million.
N: The migration too: the House of Commons was told in December that eight and a half million had moved between the two halves of the Punjab, Hindus and Sikhs east into India, Muslims west into Pakistan.
N: The economists count fourteen and a half million displaced in four years, across both borders.

# MOOD: grief

## SCENE 5 | The force that was supposed to hold
# MOOD: dread
N: A force had been assigned to prevent this; go back a week. The Punjab Boundary Force, under Major General Rees, was to hold the twelve districts the line cut.
N: On the eighth of August the Governor of the Punjab, Sir Evan Jenkins, telegraphed the Viceroy with the count.

## DOCUMENT | Sir Evan Jenkins, Governor of the Punjab | telegram to the Viceroy | August 8, 1947
# extract: src-top-xii.item382
# MOOD: dread
N: Evan Jenkins, to the Viceroy, by telegram.
M: This means that in addition to police we have a strength of say 7500 effective rifles to control 12 districts with a population of no less than 12 million.
N: Seven thousand five hundred rifles for twelve million people, one rifle for at least sixteen hundred of them before the police were counted. He had estimated the need at twenty thousand men.

## SCENE 6 | The drum
# MOOD: testimony
N: Sarla Dutta was thirteen. Her father was killed on the road, and she and two or three other girls were taken by a group of men, converted and renamed.
N: Months later soldiers came looking for abducted girls, and the man who held her put her inside the drum he kept his seeds in.

# MOOD: grief
N: That December, the two governments agreed that abducted persons were to be restored to their own country, even against their wishes.
N: The following April, the officer India kept in Lahore for the recovery reported about a hundred women still to be found in one district, and sixteen recovered between the first and the twenty fifth.

# MOOD: grief

## TURN | Five accounts
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Five accounts of what happened mostly agree about the documents, and disagree about which documents are the story.
N: They are argued from what this programme could read: the statements, awards, telegrams and letters of that year, one official count of the dead, and four survivors recorded by the Partition Archive. Each gets its own case first.

## PERSPECTIVE | British Imperial | victor
# MOOD: procedure
N: The British account is administrative. The Indian National Congress, the party of Nehru, and the Muslim League, the party of Jinnah, both accepted the plan; Indian leaders chose the commissions and drafted their terms, and the Viceroy kept clear of the award.
N: On the speed, it holds that collapse was under way and a slower handover would have cost more lives; Attlee told Parliament they were constrained by urgency.

## PERSPECTIVE | Indian Nationalist | victor
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The Indian nationalist account holds that Congress never wanted partition, and accepted it only once a united India had become ungovernable. Freedom came with a partition, not because of one.
N: It points at what the violence buries: five hundred and sixty two princely states brought into the Indian Union, and a secular constitution written.

## PERSPECTIVE | Pakistani Nationalist | victor
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The Pakistani account begins with an election: in nineteen forty six the Muslim League won four hundred and forty six of the four hundred and ninety five seats reserved for Muslim voters.
N: On Gurdaspur, it holds that a Muslim majority district went to India by a political decision, not a judicial one.
N: On the state, it holds that something was built from almost nothing. Muhammad Ali Jinnah, leader of the League and founder of Pakistan, addressed its assembly on the eleventh of August.

## DOCUMENT | Muhammad Ali Jinnah | presidential address to the Constituent Assembly of Pakistan, Karachi | August 11, 1947
# extract: src-jinnah-19470811.whole
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Jinnah, to the assembly.
M: On both sides, in Hindustan and Pakistan, there are sections of people who may not agree with it, who may not like it, but in my judgment there was no other solution and I am sure future history will record its verdict in favour of it.

## PERSPECTIVE | Subaltern and displacement | vanquished
# MOOD: testimony
N: The fourth account is not about states at all. It holds that national narratives erase the people who walked.
N: Khushi Muhammad Jut was seven, one of the Muslims of a village in Ludhiana district that the line left in India. The Partition Archive summarised his interview, and recounts what his elders said.

## DOCUMENT | The elders of Aloona Tola, as recounted by Khushi Muhammad Jut | The 1947 Partition Archive, the Archive's summary of his interview | 1947
# extract: src-pa-khushi-muhammad.para8
# MOOD: testimony
N: His elders, as he recounted them.
M: For generations we have been living here, how can we leave our homes?

## PERSPECTIVE | Women's history | vanquished
# MOOD: grief
N: The fifth account is about what was done to women, and then what the two states did: a recovery that moved women across the border by religion, against their wishes.
N: It cannot count them; the books with a count could not be read here. Its witness is a poem, written by Amrita Pritam on a train out of Lahore, to the eighteenth century Punjabi poet Waris Shah.

## DOCUMENT | Amrita Pritam (Void translation from the Punjabi) | Ajj Aakhaan Waris Shah Nu, rendered by Void from the Punjabi | 1949

N: Amrita Pritam, in a translation made by Void from the Punjabi.
F: today lakhs of daughters weep, and say to you, Waris Shah: rise, you who felt for the grieving, rise and look at your own Punjab

## REST
# MOOD: grief

## TURN | The shape of the disagreement
# MOOD: reckoning
N: None of the five is a lie. Each is true, and each has a hole in it.
N: The British account leaves out what the staff of the Viceroy wrote about Gurdaspur, and how thin the Governor found the Force.
N: The Indian account leaves out the Muslim dead of the east, believed rather than counted.
N: The Pakistani account leaves out the two stated grounds of the award, and the Sikh note of nineteen forty two that called Gurdaspur balanced.
N: The subaltern account leaves out the states that were built; the account of the women, the recovered women who wished to return, and the men killed in the same columns.
N: The holes are not random. Each omits what would cost its own side the most.
N: And none of the five is a Sikh account, though the Sikhs were one of the three communities of the Punjab and the plan cut them in two; the Viceroy, once he had sent for a map, said he was astounded.

## REST
# MOOD: grief

## CLOSE
# MOOD: grief
N: Two countries were made from one on the fifteenth of August, nineteen forty seven. The line through the Punjab was signed on the twelfth and published after the ceremonies.
N: It left a hundred and sixty two enclaves on the wrong side of the border with what became Bangladesh; they were exchanged in twenty fifteen.
N: In nineteen seventy one, East Pakistan broke away, and Bangladesh became a third country born from the first partition.
N: Both states hold nuclear weapons now. They have fought four wars. Kashmir is still contested.
N: One census, any map, and five weeks.
