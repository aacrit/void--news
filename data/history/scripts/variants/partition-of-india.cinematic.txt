## TURN | Five accounts
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Five accounts of what happened mostly agree about the documents, and disagree about which documents are the story. Each gets its own case first.

## PERSPECTIVE | British Imperial | victor
# MOOD: procedure
N: The British account is administrative. Both Congress and the League accepted the plan; the Indian leaders chose the commissions and drafted their terms. The Viceroy kept out of the award, and Ismay said so in writing.
N: On the speed, it holds that collapse was already under way, and a slower handover would have cost more lives. Attlee told Parliament they were constrained by urgency.

## PERSPECTIVE | Indian Nationalist | victor
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The Indian nationalist account holds that Congress never wanted partition, and accepted it only after concluding that a united India had become ungovernable. Freedom came with a partition, not because of one.
N: It points at what the violence tends to bury: five hundred and sixty two princely states integrated, a secular constitution written, the dead counted from witnesses.

## PERSPECTIVE | Pakistani Nationalist | victor
# MOOD: reckoning
N: The Pakistani account begins with an election. In nineteen forty six the Muslim League won four hundred and forty six of the four hundred and ninety five seats reserved for Muslim voters.
N: On Gurdaspur, it holds that a Muslim majority district went to India by a political decision, not a judicial one.
N: On the state, it holds that something was built from almost nothing. Jinnah addressed its assembly on the eleventh of August.

## TURN | The shape of the disagreement
# MOOD: reckoning
N: None of the five is a lie. Each is a true account, and each has a hole in it.
N: The British account leaves out what the staff of the Viceroy wrote about Gurdaspur before the award, and how thin the Governor found the Force.
N: The Indian account leaves out the Muslim dead of the east, carried in its own count only as an equal number, believed.
N: The Pakistani account leaves out the two stated grounds of the award, and the Sikh note of nineteen forty two that called Gurdaspur balanced, by an older census.
N: The subaltern account leaves out the states that were built. The account of the women leaves out the recovered women who wished to return, and the men killed in the same columns.
N: The holes are not random. Each omits what would cost its own side the most.
N: And none of the five is a Sikh account. The Viceroy, on the fourth of June, said he had sent for a map, and was astounded to find that the plan divided that community into two almost equal parts.
