# primary_source_excerpt, and the extract id follows the DOCUMENT marker in a comment.
# Nine excerpts were appended to the YAML for this script, each a verbatim span of a held
# extract: the cable's paragraph 5 (the 1,700 men), the Bagosora chamber's acquittal on
# conspiracy, Kambanda's dates in office, the Carlsson chronology's 270 from 2,539, McDoom's
# range, Human Rights Watch on the RPF, the UN's Gacaca count and closing date, and the
# Akayesu chamber's paragraph on the 1930s cards. The cable's "ALL TUTSI" sentence is
# narrated, never read: the OCR prints "ALL.TUTSI". The live script's Uwilingiyimana phone
# call and the Annan "bitter regret" line do not return; neither is held. The death toll is
# said per producer and never averaged; the tribunal's acquittal on conspiracy before 7
# April is said aloud each time planning comes up. Cut, from the brief's do-not-say list:
# "within one hour", the typed lists, the Falcon 50 and the missile, "cockroaches", the
# Butare "Tutsi prefect" and "ten days later", the Nyamata and Murambi figures, Kambanda
# "do not know how to stop", "first head of government to plead guilty", the rape and HIV
# figures, "no court had recognised before", "shook hands with the devil", "tribal ...
# ancient hatreds", the eighty five / fourteen / one split (the record says about 84 / 15
# / 1), "twelve thousand courts" beside "one point nine six million" (two producers), the
# GDP, aid and parliament figures, R2P, "five times the rate of the Nazi camps". Also not
# said because no extract carries it: that Rwanda is small or in central Africa (the record
# places it in Africa), the hour of the Prime Minister's death, the thirty-six hours, the
# RPF's killing estimates as numbers. Lines beginning with "#" are inert: the parser skips
# them (pipeline/history/script_format.py::parse_script). SAY entries carry the "orpheus:"

# the live script: nothing globs this directory.

## OPEN
# MOOD: dread
N: Kigali, the capital of Rwanda, the eleventh of January, nineteen ninety four. A cable goes to New York from the United Nations force in the city.
N: An informant, a trainer of militia, says he has been ordered to register all Tutsi in Kigali, and says what he suspects it is for. New York answers the same day.
N: Every Rwandan carried a card that named what he was.

## TITLE
# MOOD: dread
N: The Rwandan genocide. April to July, nineteen ninety four.

## SCENE 1 | The cable
# MOOD: procedure
N: Rwanda, a country in Africa, in the spring of nineteen ninety four. Its people carry identity cards marked Hutu, Tutsi or Twa, three groups the Belgian authorities fixed in the early nineteen thirties: about eighty four percent Hutu, about fifteen percent Tutsi.
N: The government of President Habyarimana has been at war since nineteen ninety with the Rwandan Patriotic Front, the RPF, a Tutsi-led rebel army under Paul Kagame. The Arusha Accords, a peace agreement, bind the two sides to share power.
N: UNAMIR, the United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda, created by the Security Council in October nineteen ninety three, is there under a Canadian general, Romeo Dallaire.
N: In about a hundred days from April, in the words of the UN's own inquiry, Rwandans will kill Rwandans. How many, no two producers agree.
N: On the eleventh of January Dallaire's headquarters reports an informant: a top-level trainer of the Interahamwe, the armed militia of the ruling party.
N: Seventeen hundred men, he says, trained in army camps and scattered through Kigali in groups of forty; Belgian soldiers to be provoked and killed, to bring a Belgian withdrawal; and a register of all Tutsi in Kigali.

N: The mission is told instead to take what it knows to the President.

## SCENE 2 | The seventh of April
# MOOD: rupture
N: Kigali, the evening of the sixth of April. The plane carrying the Presidents of Rwanda and Burundi is shot down. The army and the militia immediately put up roadblocks around the city.
N: Before dawn on the seventh, across the country, the Presidential Guard and the militia begin killing Tutsi, and Hutu known to favour the Accords. Among the first is the Prime Minister, Agathe Uwilingiyimana.
N: Ten Belgian peacekeepers had been sent to escort her to Radio Rwanda. Her compound was attacked; she fled over the wall to a United Nations compound, where soldiers found her later that morning and shot her.
N: Dallaire had asked New York about using force to save her, and was told: not to fire until fired upon. The ten Belgians were killed at Camp Kigali; Belgium withdrew its contingent, the core of the force.
N: On the twenty first of April the Security Council cut the mission: to two hundred and seventy, from two thousand five hundred and thirty nine, says the UN's own inquiry; to four hundred and fifty troops, says the tribunal's Akayesu judgment.
N: A retired colonel, Theoneste Bagosora, was tried for the first killings; in two thousand and eight the tribunal found them an organised military operation under orders.

## DOCUMENT | International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, Trial Chamber I | Prosecutor v. Bagosora and others, Judgement and Sentence, paragraph 14 | December 18, 2008
# extract: src-ictr-bagosora-tj.para14
# MOOD: reckoning
N: On whether the genocide was planned before the seventh of April, the chamber accepted indications of a plan, found them also consistent with a power struggle in a war with the RPF, and ruled.
M: Consequently, the Prosecution has not proven beyond reasonable doubt that the only reasonable inference to be drawn from the evidence is that the four Accused conspired amongst themselves or with others to commit genocide before it unfolded from 7 April 1994.
M: The Chamber has acquitted them of the count of conspiracy.

## SCENE 3 | The hundred days
# MOOD: procedure
N: Rwanda, the eighth of April. Jean Kambanda becomes Prime Minister of the interim government, until the seventeenth of July; his guilty plea, in nineteen ninety eight, admits what that government did.
N: It armed the Interahamwe and a second party's youth wing, trained before April, he admits, for the massacres; one man's admission, where the tribunal acquitted Bagosora of conspiring before the seventh.
N: It dismissed the official in charge of Butare for opposing the massacres, and in cabinet followed their course and took no action to stop them.
N: A radio station, Radio Television Libre des Mille Collines, whose broadcasts incited killing, told listeners to go to work; one of its broadcasters admitted the phrase came to mean killing Tutsi and the Hutu who opposed the interim government.

# MOOD: reckoning

## TURN | Five accounts
# MOOD: reckoning
N: What happened is admitted, in pleas and judgments. Who let it happen, who planned it, and how many died is where the accounts part.
N: Five accounts, from what this programme could read: two cables, Security Council papers, the tribunal's judgments and pleas, a State Department transcript, the UN and French inquiries, a presidency's text, two counts of the dead. The memoirs could not be read.

## TURN | The shape of the disagreement
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Each of the five accounts is true, and each leaves something out.
N: The peacekeepers' account leaves out that the cable's own author recorded reservations, and a possible trap.
N: The colonial account leaves out what Rwanda's President said: that the people who planned and carried it out were Rwandans.
N: The state's account leaves out the RPF's own killings, counted by estimate and by denial.
