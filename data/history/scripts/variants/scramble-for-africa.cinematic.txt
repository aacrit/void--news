# Cinematic variant, 2026-10-04. The fifth Orpheus 3B episode (narrator "tara"; a male
# reader of record for M: lines, a female reader for F: lines), in the Ken Burns
# register: short declarative sentences, documents read plainly, the silence placed by
# the writer. Re-cut from the live script against the evidence ledger
# (data/history/evidence/scramble-for-africa/) and the published thesis
# (data/history/theses/scramble-for-africa.md): every line the document voice reads is
# verbatim from a stored extract, and the extract id follows the marker in a comment.
# Sources are the event YAML, the ledger and the thesis, nothing else. Lines beginning
# with "#" are inert: the parser skips them (pipeline/history/script_format.py::parse_script).
# The mood directive on every segment is the one docs/proposals/HISTORY-AUDIO-ARCHIVAL.md
# defines. There is no clip slot: this episode has no admitted recording, and a candidate
# belongs in the ledger, not here. There is no F: line: no stored extract for this event is
# a woman's words. The one girl in the record, whom the consul's report calls T 7, is held
# in the consul's third person, so the reader of record reads the consul and the narration
# says so. Two reads are Void translations, Witbooi from his Cape Dutch and Ferry from his
# French, and the narration says so before each, as the Partition variant did for Amrita
# Pritam. The live script's Leopold, Rhodes, Kipling, Morel, Yaa Asantewaa, Fanon, Casement
# letter and al-Mukhtar quotations are in no extract this programme holds (ledger gaps:),
# so none is read. Where the record disagrees with itself (whether Berlin partitioned
# anything, what the Congo's people died of, how many Herero died) the script states both
# and chooses neither. North Africa is in the event record and in no document this
# programme holds; its account is stated from the record and says so. Where a figure is
# known to this programme only from the event YAML, the narration says "the event record".
# This file is not the live script: nothing globs this directory.

## TURN | What each one leaves out
# MOOD: reckoning
N: Each is true as far as it goes.
N: The colonizers leave out what their own records say was done in the basin the Act protected: the hostages, and the hands.
N: The rulers leave out the wars they fought with one another, and the rulers who did sign.
N: The Pan African account leaves out what African governments did with the structure after independence, and the Act's silence on territory, which its own historian concedes.
N: The economic ledger leaves out everyone who was not a count, and the share of the dead the consul himself gave to disease.
N: North Africa's own account leaves out the elites who worked with the powers it fought, and the women of its resistance, unwritten in the record.
N: And no Congolese, no Herero woman and no Nama survivor speaks here in his or her own words.
