## The question

::: episode chapter=0

On 24 May 1915 the Allied governments told the Sublime Porte that they would hold personally responsible for “these new crimes of Turkey against humanity and civilization” every member of the Ottoman government implicated in the massacres.[^src-frus-d1398 whole] On 7 July 1915 the German ambassador, the ally's, reported to his chancellor that the relocation showed the government “pursuing its purpose of eradicating the Armenian race from the Turkish Empire”.[^src-gust-19150707-en para3][^src-de-19150707 para3][^src-de-19150707 secdate] On 10 July the American ambassador, a neutral's, telegraphed that the measures were “purely arbitrary and directed from Constantinople in the name of military necessity, often in districts where no military operations are likely to take place”.[^src-frus-d1400 whole] The government's own pamphlet of 1916 called the same measures a transfer of the Armenians from the military zones to secure the rear of the army.[^src-verite-1916 sectitle][^src-verite-1916 p11]

This thesis asks whether the deportation was the relocation the state named or the destruction the ambassadors named, and what the free record holds about who decided it and how many died. It makes three claims. First, that the ally's file and the neutral's file agree, on 5 of 6 points, on measures extended beyond any war zone, decided in the capital, and aimed at the eradication of a people. Second, that the count of the dead is a set of counts with their producers and is stated here as that, and that the interior ministry's own tally of 1917 counted about a quarter of the Armenians in its own column for 1914, and fewer than a quarter of the census total in its notes. Third, that the Turkish state's account is held here in its own words and tested against its ally's record, and that where the test needs the Ottoman archives, this record says so and stops.

## The record

The primary record this thesis holds is of five producers, and the reader should know whose paper each verdict stands on.{i} The American file is the ambassador's telegrams of 1915 as the Department of State printed them, with the telegram of 16 July read in a facsimile.[^src-frus-d1400 whole][^src-morgenthau-19150716 whole] The German file is the Foreign Office's own reports, read in the transcription an online edition publishes from the Berlin archive, in the original German with the edition's English translation held beside it as a separate work.[^src-de-19150707 secdate][^src-gust-19150707-en para2] The British compilation of 1916 is a belligerent government's, and its documents are American, Armenian, German and Swiss witnesses; its historical summary is Toynbee's own.[^src-bryce-1916 secpreface] The Ottoman state speaks here through its pamphlet of 1916, in French, and through the interior minister's tally of 1917, read in an English edition.[^src-verite-1916 sectitle][^src-talat-1917 p21] The survivors speak through a narrative an Armenian of Moush recorded on 25 July 1915, printed first in the Armenian journal Van-Tosp and reprinted by the British ministry.[^src-bryce-1916 p92] The other survivor voice held is a memoir written up for American readers.[^src-mardiganian-1918 p81-82]

What the free record refused is listed by name in the ledger's gaps, and the largest are the Ottoman archives, the court-martial verdict of 1919 as gazetted, the Strasbourg judgment on the word and every standard monograph of the last 20 years, each behind a paywall or a challenge page.[^src-anumyan-2025 p38][^src-masseret-2001 p113] The scholarship is therefore held through open articles in French, German, Turkish and English and through the monographs' titles only, and the Turkish state's own archival case is known here through the historians who describe its fonds, not through the fonds.[^src-kevorkian-2013 para3] The one Turkish-language article held argues the state's reading, that the Armenians were subjected to relocation for an internal security problem.[^src-genc-2020 p294] No Armenian-language scholarship is held: the Yerevan articles are read in English, and the only Armenian on the page is the Republic's declaration.{i} Four further gaps are named here because the page leans on what they hold: the Whitaker report to the United Nations Sub-Commission, served only as a scan without text; the stenographic record of the Tehlirian trial; Lemkin's book; and the Near East Relief history that the orphans photograph comes from.{i} Where two counts disagree they are set out below as counts with their producers, and the thesis states none of them as the number.{i}

## The argument

### 1. What the two ambassadors wrote in the same week

On 31 May 1915 the German ambassador telegraphed that Enver Pasha meant to close a large number of Armenian schools, suppress Armenian newspapers, forbid Armenian postal correspondence and settle in Mesopotamia every family from the insurgent Armenian centres that was not wholly beyond reproach, and that he urgently asked Germany not to stay his hand.[^src-de-19150531 secdate][^src-de-19150531 para1] On 17 June the same ambassador reported that the banishment “is not only motivated by military considerations”, because the interior minister, Talaat Bey, had told an embassy official “that the Porte is intent on taking advantage of the World War in order to make a clean sweep of internal enemies”.[^src-gust-19150617-en para4][^src-de-19150617 para4][^src-de-19150617 secdate] A few days later the Armenian Patriarch told the same official that the measures were intended “to expel them from Turkey or rather to eradicate them altogether”.[^src-gust-19150617-en para5][^src-de-19150617 para5]

::: exhibit ex-wangenheim-433

On 7 July the ambassador wrote that until 14 days before, the expulsion had been limited to the provinces nearest the eastern theatre and parts of Adana, and that the Porte had since extended it to Trebizond, Mamuret-ul-Aziz and Sivas, “even though these parts of the country are not threatened by any enemy invasion for the time being”.[^src-gust-19150707-en para2][^src-de-19150707 para2][^src-de-19150707 secdate] About 30,000 people in the Vilayet of Trebizond alone were to be deported through Erzincan to Mesopotamia, the route ran through the Kurdish district of Dersim, and the Vali of Trebizond declared openly that he could guarantee the transport only as far as Erzincan; the consul at Erzurum put the Armenians killed on the road to Harput at 3,000.[^src-gust-19150707-en para6][^src-de-19150707 para6] The same report records the memorandum the ambassador handed to the Grand Vizier on the 4th, approving deportation only insofar as military considerations required it, and states why it was written: to be able to answer later accusations by Germany's enemies of complicity in the rigorous Turkish measures.[^src-de-19150707 para10]

Three days later the American ambassador telegraphed Washington that reports from widely scattered districts indicated “systematic attempt to uproot peaceful Armenian populations”, that the measures were “not in response to popular or fanatical demand”, that “Most of the sufferers are innocent and have been loyal to Ottoman Government”, and that the German embassy would, he believed, “simply content itself with giving advice and a formal protest probably intended for record and to cover itself from future responsibility”.[^src-frus-d1400 whole] He added the consul at Harput's line, “There seems to be a systematic plan to crush the Armenian race”.[^src-frus-d1400 whole] Neither report cites the other; one government was the Porte's ally, the other neutral.{i}

::: analysis an-two-ambassadors

On 16 July the neutral telegraphed that “it appears that a campaign of race extermination is in progress under a pretext of reprisal against rebellion.”[^src-morgenthau-19150716 whole] On the same day his German colleague forwarded his consul at Samsun's report, which held that “a great Armenian conspiracy was excellently organised in the whole of Anatolia” and, in the next paragraph, that the countermeasures involved “nothing less than the destruction or enforced Islamization of a whole people, whose relatives for the greatest part did not even participate in the revolutionary movement”.[^src-gust-19150716-en para12][^src-gust-19150716-en para13][^src-de-19150716 secdate] The German record holds both the conspiracy and the destruction in one file, and this thesis quotes both.{i}

### 2. The road

::: episode chapter=8

The German administrator at Erzurum wrote on 5 August 1915 that the inhabitants of the plains of Erzurum and Passin, by then “only women, children and old men”, were driven out of their villages towards Mesopotamia, that on the way to Erzincan they were attacked, robbed and killed by Kurds and Turkish volunteers, and that “Between 10 and 20,000 people were probably killed, but the government's figures fall between only 3 and 4,000.”[^src-gust-19150805-en para4][^src-de-19150805 para5][^src-de-19150805 secdate] He judged that a measure “which might have been justified militarily and politically in some ways, has turned into a campaign of revenge, destruction and robbery”, and that in the Vilayet of Erzurum “neither weapons nor compromising documents were found”.[^src-gust-19150805-en para16][^src-gust-19150805-en para20][^src-de-19150805 para17][^src-de-19150805 para21] A landowning bey of standing, whose guest he had been, told him that the earlier massacres had been fights among men, and that now, against the Koran, innocent women and children were being murdered, not by a crowd in a passion but systematically and on the order of the government, the Committee.[^src-de-19150805 para17] The consul at Aleppo reported on 27 July that bodies had floated past Rumkaleh, Biredjik and Djerablus on the Euphrates “for a total of 25 days”, bound together “two by two, back to back”, which he read as killing by the authorities and not by bands.[^src-gust-19150727-en para5][^src-de-19150727 para5][^src-de-19150727 secdate]

A woman from the village of Kheiban, one of the deported of the Moush district, told her story to an Armenian of Moush then in hiding, who wrote it down on 25 July 1915 for the Armenian journal Van-Tosp, from which the British compilation reprinted it: the women and children of village after village were gathered into “a company of 8,000 to 10,000 people”, the old and the weak “were killed”, the guards “Very often they violated eight or ten-year-old girls”, and “Our company moved on slowly, leaving heaps of corpses behind”.[^src-bryce-1916 p92] By the boundary of the Ginj district, she said, “our number was diminished by half”; at a river the guards ordered them to cross, and many obeyed.[^src-bryce-1916 p93] Aurora Mardiganian's memoir describes the first dawn of her convoy, when Kurdish riders came down on the camp, gathered the animals and walked among the women to pick out the young ones.[^src-mardiganian-1918 p81-82] On 11 August the American ambassador wrote that the people of Ada Bazar, Izmid and Bardizag, “some 50 miles from Constantinople”, would be “herded like cattle into the arid and hostile wastes of the interior, where the greater number, possibly excepting those who in desperation embrace Mohammedanism, will doubtless perish by murder or slow starvation”.[^src-frus-d1406 whole] On 3 September he telegraphed that “Destruction of Armenian race in Turkey is progressing rapidly”.[^src-frus-d1410 whole]

::: analysis an-words-for-the-aim

### 3. The state's own case

The Ottoman government's pamphlet of 1916 holds that the commander of the Imperial Army, finding the Armenians making common cause with the enemy, was forced to order the transfer southward of the Armenian element in localities considered military zones, to secure the rear of his troops.[^src-verite-1916 sectitle][^src-verite-1916 p11] It describes the measures as preventive rather than repressive, taken after the dissolution of the Armenian committees, and states that during their application the Armenians were at times the victims of regrettable abuses and violence, which it calls inevitable given the indignation of the Muslim population.[^src-verite-1916 p13][^src-verite-1916 p14] Ahmed Rüstem Bey, formerly ambassador at Washington, argued in his book that the Entente's charge of extermination was absurd because so many Ottoman Armenians survived.[^src-rustem-1918 p88-89] The Republic's foreign ministry holds the same line: that the government relocated the Armenian population in or near the war zone, that some Armenians away from the front were included, and that no authentic evidence of a premeditated plan to kill Armenians exists.[^src-mfa-tr para6][^src-mfa-tr para7]

The ally's file tests the first of those claims and contradicts it: the extension to Trebizond, Mamuret-ul-Aziz and Sivas was reported by the German ambassador on 7 July as reaching provinces threatened by no invasion, and the consul at Samsun on the Black Sea reported the expulsion order as “apparently valid for all Anatolian Vilayets”.[^src-gust-19150707-en para2][^src-gust-19150716-en para13][^src-de-19150707 para2][^src-de-19150707 secdate] The second claim, that no authentic evidence of a plan exists, is tested here against the interior minister's own words as the German embassy recorded them and against the two ambassadors' independent readings, and no further: the Ottoman archives and the court-martial verdict of 1919 could not be read free, and a verdict on documents this record does not hold would be a guess.[^src-gust-19150617-en para4][^src-de-19150617 para4][^src-anumyan-2025 p38] The interior minister's own report is held, and it counts.{i}

::: exhibit ex-talat-summary

The notes to its opening summary page give the 1914 census as 1,187,818 Gregorian and 63,967 Catholic Armenians, 1,256,403 together, and add that because the data were incomplete the true figure should be around 1,500,000; the Armenians counted in 1917 as natives and outsiders, Constantinople included, were 284,157, a figure the note would raise by 30 percent.[^src-talat-1917 p20][^src-talat-1917 p21] The two figures the note gives do not make the total it prints, and the editor marks that total sic.[^src-talat-1917 p20] The summary page's own column for 1914 totals 1,112,614, not the 1,256,403 of the notes, and against it the 284,157 of 1917 is about a quarter.[^src-talat-1917 p21][^src-talat-1917 p20] The editor notes that provinces with Armenian populations, Edirne, Chatalja, Kale-i Sultaniye, Menteshe, Teke, Janik and Jerusalem, are missing from the report.[^src-talat-1917 p20]

::: analysis an-ministry-ledger

### 4. The counts

Toynbee wrote in the British compilation that the first step was the number of Armenians in the Ottoman frontiers when the deportations began, and that “there are no independent foreign estimates of this on record”: the Patriarchate's enquiry of 1912 placed it as high as 2,100,000 and the Ottoman government's latest returns at 1,100,000 and no more.[^src-bryce-1916 p648-649] He took 1,600,000 provisionally and wrote that the true figure lay between that and 2,000,000.[^src-bryce-1916 p648-649] Tatoyan, from the Yerevan museum, sets the two official series side by side, 1,294,851 in the Ottoman figures of 1914 against 2,026,700 in the Patriarchate's, and finds the refugee censuses of the Russian Caucasus about 70% above the Ottoman figures for the same districts.[^src-tatoyan-2020 p56][^src-tatoyan-2020 p55]

::: analysis an-prewar-counts

For the dead, the American Committee's bulletin of May 1916 computed “between 450,000 and 850,000” and called 600,000 safe, and Toynbee summed up that “about an equal number of Armenians in Turkey seem to have escaped, to have perished, and to have survived deportation in 1915”, each category in round numbers 600,000.[^src-bryce-1916 p664][^src-bryce-1916 p651] Toynbee's own convoy figures run from 18,000 at Malatia to 150 at Aleppo, and from 5,000 to 213 on the road from Harput.[^src-bryce-1916 p650] By December 1916 the German administrator who had watched Erzurum emptied reported that his fear “that the evacuation of the Armenians would be tantamount to their annihilation” had come true and that the Turkish Armenians, “with the exception of several hundred thousand living in Constantinople and other larger cities, have been practically wiped out”.[^src-gust-19161204-en para22][^src-de-19161204 para22][^src-de-19161204 secdate] The later counts, the Bundestag's, the White House's and the scholarship's spread, are set out under the disagreements below with the producers of each.{i}

### 5. The word and the law

::: exhibit ex-frus-1398

The Allied declaration of 24 May did not use the word; it spoke of “crimes of Turkey against humanity and civilization”.[^src-frus-d1398 whole] The Treaty of Sèvres of 10 August 1920 made Turkey recognise Armenia as a free and independent state, refused to recognise conversions to Islam made since 1 November 1914 in view of the “terrorist régime”, and bound the Turkish government to hand over “the persons whose surrender may be required by the latter as being responsible for the massacres”.[^src-sevres-1920 sectitle][^src-sevres-1920 art88][^src-sevres-1920 art142][^src-sevres-1920 art230] The Convention of 9 December 1948 defines genocide as acts “committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group, as such”, and names among them “Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part”.[^src-un-convention-1948 sectitle][^src-un-convention-1948 art2] A prosecution document at Nuremberg, headed as Hitler's talk to his commanders of 22-8-39, ends a passage on killing the Polish population with the line “Who still talks nowadays of the extermination of the Armenians ?”, as the volume prints it.[^src-nca-vii-1946 p752-753]

On 18 June 1987 the European Parliament resolved that the events of 1915-1917 “constitute genocide within the meaning of the convention”, and in the same paragraph that “neither political nor legal or material claims against present-day Turkey can be derived from the recognition”.[^src-ep-1987 p119][^src-ep-1987 p120] Its preamble records that the Turkish state “rejects the charge of genocide as unfounded”.[^src-ep-1987 p119] The Bundestag's printed paper 18/8613, a motion as tabled, states that the planned expulsion and destruction of the Armenians began on 24 April 1915, and that the German Reich, the principal military ally, despite unambiguous information from its own diplomats and missionaries, did not try to stop it.[^src-bundestag-2016 p1] The President of the United States said on 24 April 2021 that “one and a half million Armenians were deported, massacred, or marched to their deaths in a campaign of extermination”.[^src-biden-2021 para1] The Republic of Armenia's declaration of independence, in its 11th article, stands in support of the international recognition of the 1915 genocide of the Armenians in Ottoman Turkey and Western Armenia.[^src-armenia-decl-1990 item11] The record holds no judgment of any court on the word, and the thesis does not supply one.{i}

## The historiography

The state's position is held here in its own words, from its pamphlet of 1916 to its foreign ministry's page, which calls the Armenian narrative one that “selects the Armenian suffering, embellishes it in several ways and presents it as a genocide” and holds that “greater numbers of Turks died or were killed in the years leading to and during the War”.[^src-mfa-tr para2][^src-mfa-tr para4][^src-verite-1916 sectitle] Its strongest line is Rüstem's, that the survival of hundreds of thousands of Ottoman Armenians proves there was no extermination, and the German record answers it in its own terms: the officer who had reported from Erzurum wrote in December 1916 that the Turkish Armenians, “with the exception of several hundred thousand living in Constantinople and other larger cities, have been practically wiped out”.[^src-rustem-1918 p88-89][^src-rustem-1918 sectitle][^src-gust-19161204-en para22][^src-de-19161204 secdate] The witnesses' position is the memoir's, that the deportation was “a new method of massacre” and the ministers “made no particular attempt to conceal the fact”, and the memoir's Talaat says “we have already disposed of three quarters of the” Armenians; the editor of the minister's own report prints the diary's version, “they had already disposed of three quarters of them”.[^src-morgenthau-1918 p309][^src-morgenthau-1918 p337-338][^src-talat-1917 p5] The scholarship held here calls mass murder, deportation and forced assimilation “three faces of the same process of destruction organized by the Young Turk regime”, describes conversion as a bureaucratic process from Talat Pasha's own directives, and gives the spread of estimates of the dead as running upward from 800,000.[^src-kurt-2016 para1][^src-kurt-2016 para5][^src-wevelsiep-2015 para2] Kévorkian's survey, in French, records that the Interior Ministry's fonds opened in 2005 show the administrative planning of the deportations and a will to transform the composition of Asia Minor, and that survivor testimony was long denied the standing of the state's own documents.[^src-kevorkian-2013 para3][^src-kevorkian-2013 para6] The Armenian scholarship held here reports the court-martial's death sentences of 5 July 1919 on Talât, Enver, Cemal and Nazım, 20 death sentences in 63 cases and 3 carried out, and the law's position is the French parliament's bill of 18 January 2001 and the parliaments' resolutions above.[^src-anumyan-2025 p38][^src-masseret-2001 p113]

## What the record omits

- No source in this record is an Ottoman order in Ottoman Turkish: the state's case is held in a French pamphlet, an English edition of the interior minister's tally and a foreign ministry web page. {against=ottoman-turkish-state}
- No source in this record counts the Muslim dead of the eastern provinces, on which the Turkish state's comparison rests. {against=ottoman-turkish-state}
- No source in this record comes from an Armenian archive's own collection of testimony: the narrative from Moush was recorded by an Armenian for an Armenian journal and is held here in a British ministry's reprint, and the memoir was written up for American readers. {against=armenian-account}
- No source in this record is the court-martial verdict of 1919; it is known here as one Armenian scholar summarises the gazette. {against=armenian-account}
- No source in this record is by a Kurdish participant, whom the German consuls and the deported woman both name as the killers on the road. {against=international-witness}
- No source in this record is a monograph of the last 20 years read past its title page; the scholarship is held through open articles and a preprint. {against=genocide-scholarship}
- No source in this record is a judgment of a court on the word genocide; the Strasbourg judgment that touched it answered a challenge page. {against=law-and-recognition}
