## The question

::: episode chapter=0

The Act that ended British rule set the appointed day as “the fifteenth day of August, nineteen hundred and forty-seven”, and set up two Dominions, India and Pakistan.[^src-iia-1947 sec1] The line between them in the Punjab was signed at New Delhi on 12 August 1947 by a chairman who did not attend the public sittings in person.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para12][^src-radcliffe-punjab para5] The Commons was told that December that the refugees who had moved between the two Punjabs numbered 8,500,000 or thereabouts, and the economists put the displaced at “14.5 million people” within four years.[^src-hansard-19471218 p1845][^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p5]

This page tells the whole event first, from the League's demand of 1940 to the treaty that divided the rivers, and every fact in that telling carries a note to a document this record holds. It then argues the questions the documents leave open, one at a time, and tests each account of the partition against them. It makes three claims.

## The event

### 1. The demand, 1940 to 1946

At Lahore on 23 March 1940 the All-India Muslim League resolved that no constitutional plan would be acceptable to the Muslims unless the areas in which they were numerically in a majority, “as in the North-Western and Eastern zones of India”, were grouped to constitute “Independent States”.[^src-lahore-resolution-1940 p1] The Sikh All Parties Committee's note to Cripps described the two western divisions of the Punjab as predominantly Muslim, the two eastern divisions as predominantly non-Muslim, and the three central districts of Amritsar, Gurdaspur and Lahore as balanced.[^src-sikh-note-1942 p6-7]

::: exhibit ex-lahore-1940

The Cabinet Mission's statement, read to the Lords on 16 May 1946, examined the League's claim to “a separate and fully independent sovereign state of Pakistan” in two areas, the provinces of the north-west and those of Bengal and Assam.[^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p271][^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p273] It recorded that the League regarded a smaller Pakistan of the Muslim-majority areas alone as quite impracticable, because it would exclude the whole of the Ambala and Jullundur divisions, all of Assam but Sylhet, and a large part of western Bengal with Calcutta, and it judged that any radical partition of the Punjab and Bengal would be contrary to the wishes and interests of a very large proportion of their inhabitants.[^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p274] It added that any division of the Punjab would of necessity divide the Sikhs.[^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p274] It could not advise that power be handed to “two entirely separate sovereign states”, and proposed instead a Union of India for foreign affairs, defence and communications, with every other subject left to the provinces.[^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p275][^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p276] For electing their representatives the provinces were grouped into three sections, and the sections were to settle the provincial constitutions.[^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p278] Section B was the Punjab, the North West Frontier Province and Sind, with 22 Muslim members of 35, and Section C was Bengal and Assam, with 36 Muslim members of 70.[^src-hansard-lords-19460516 p279]

::: exhibit ex-cabinet-mission-11

### 2. Direct action, 1946

The British government told the Commons on 12 December 1946 where the parties had divided over the plan: the Mission, the government and the League held that the sections decided by simple majority whether a province stayed in its group, and the Congress held that the plan's statement of aims overrode its procedure.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1346][^src-hansard-19461212 p1353] On 29 July 1946 the League had withdrawn its acceptance of the Mission's scheme and resolved that “the time has come for the Muslim nation to resort to direct action to achieve Pakistan”.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1346][^src-hansard-19461212 p1348] Jinnah declared, as the Indian fact-finding body later quoted him, that the League bid “good-bye to constitutional methods”.[^src-khosla-1949 p41]

The League set aside 16 August as Direct Action Day, and in Calcutta the riots that followed resulted, Cripps told the Commons, in some 4,000 deaths and 10,000 people injured.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1350-1351] Interviewed in Bombay in August 1946, Jinnah condemned the violence, said he did not know who was responsible, and said that those who had used it had acted “contrary to expressed instructions”.[^src-jinnah-papers-xiv p642] The Indian fact-finding body prints a table of 3,173 bodies collected and disposed of in the city by three organisations, which it says “does not represent the total number of deaths”, and reports the estimate that “more than five thousand persons were killed and more than fifteen thousand injured”.[^src-khosla-1949 p65][^src-khosla-1949 p66] It also records that the League's leaders and press blamed the supporters of the Congress for starting the riot.[^src-khosla-1949 p66] Those figures and that charge are argued under the contested questions below.{i}

The violence moved east and then west.{i} From 10 October, the government told the Commons, “gangs of roving Muslim hooligans” in Eastern Bengal drove out 50,000 people and killed 200 or more, with abductions, rape and forced conversions.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1351] Mahbub and Saba, from Dhaka, date the eastern side's violence from the Kolkata and Noakhali riots of 1946 and call it more protracted and more episodic than the Punjab's.[^src-mahbub-saba-2018 p109] For Bihar, Cripps said “It is not possible to give the figures of dead and wounded in Bihar with any accuracy”, and put the dead, practically all of them Muslims, at 5,000 in what he called “probably not an exaggerated estimate”; the same account gave 445 deaths in the United Provinces since September and 622 in Bombay by 18 November.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1351] Jinnah said that November: “I know that the Musalmans have suffered heavily and are suffering, but the tragedy of Bihar has eclipsed what are mere specks elsewhere.”[^src-jinnah-papers-xiv p643]

### 3. The deadline and the plan, 1947

On 20 February 1947 the government declared its intention to transfer power into responsible Indian hands “by a date not later than June, 1948”.[^src-hansard-lords-19470220 p835][^src-hansard-lords-19470220 p836] If no fully representative assembly had framed a constitution by then, it would consider handing over the powers of the central government whether as a whole, or “in some areas to the existing Provincial Governments”.[^src-hansard-lords-19470220 p837] The same statement named Mountbatten to succeed Lord Wavell, with the task of transferring to Indian hands the responsibility for the government of British India.[^src-hansard-lords-19470220 p839]

In March 1947 the Congress Working Committee, naming Amritsar and Multan as scenes of horror and devastation, resolved that the Punjab be divided into two provinces “so that the predominantly Muslim part may be separated from the predominantly non-Muslim part”.[^src-cwc-19470308 p23] That month the Governor of the Punjab wrote to the new Viceroy that in the Rawalpindi Division there had been “a concerted attempt by the Muslim villagers to exterminate the non-Muslims or to force them to leave the area”.[^src-jenkins-19470326 p31] The Viceroy told the press in June that it was mainly at the request of the Sikh community that the Congress had put forward its resolution on the partition of the Punjab.[^src-top-xi item59]

The statement read to the Commons when the plan was announced told the assemblies of Bengal and the Punjab to meet in two parts, and named the count they would be divided by: “For the purpose of determining the population of districts, the 1941 census figures will be taken as authoritative.”[^src-hansard-19470603 p36] Sardar Patel wrote to the Viceroy that the League Council's resolution on the plan was contradictory: at one place it said the League could not agree to the partition of Bengal and the Punjab, at another that it accepted the fundamental principles of the plan “as a compromise”.[^src-top-xi item130] The All-India Congress Committee accepted the proposals on 15 June for two reasons it gave: the League's refusal to accept the plan of May 16 and to take part in the Constituent Assembly, and the Congress's policy that it could not compel the people of any territorial unit to remain in an Indian Union against their declared and established will; and it accepted that they were likely to lead to “the secession of some parts of the country from India”.[^src-aicc-19470615 p108][^src-aicc-19470615 p109] On June 14 the Panthic bodies of the Sikhs resolved that the Boundary Commission be directed to recommend “the transfer of Hindu and Sikh population” and property from the western Punjab to the eastern.[^src-panthic-19470614 p108]

The members of the Bengal assembly from its western districts then voted 58 to 21 that the province be partitioned, and the members from its eastern districts voted by 106 votes that it should not, as the Governor of Bengal reported to the Viceroy.[^src-top-xi item278] The Prime Minister told the Commons that the time given to the Indian Independence Bill was not commensurate with its importance, “But we are really constrained by the urgency of the matter”, and the Secretary of State told the Lords that the House was being asked for a passage “so rapid as to be almost unprecedented”, because a Cabinet still unitary in form was “in fact divided by sharp differences of view”.[^src-hansard-19470710 p2447][^src-hansard-lords-19470716 p802] Mountbatten wrote that June that he was “trying to keep up the momentum of our good start”.[^src-top-xi item155]

### 4. The awards and independence, August 1947

The Punjab commission was constituted by the Governor-General's announcement of 30 June 1947, with Din Muhammad, Muhammad Munir, Mehr Chand Mahajan and Teja Singh as members and Radcliffe appointed chairman afterwards.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para2] It sat in public at Lahore from 21 July to 31 July 1947, and was asked to decide before 15 August.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para5][^src-radcliffe-punjab para3] On 8 August 1947 Sir George Abell, the Viceroy's private secretary, sent Mr. S. Abbott a map “showing roughly the boundary which Sir Cyril Radcliffe proposes to demarcate in his award”, and wrote that there would not be any great changes from it, “but it will have to be accurately defined with reference to village and zail boundaries in Lahore district”.[^src-abell-19470808 p456] Radcliffe signed the Punjab award at New Delhi on 12 August 1947.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para12] The Governor of the Punjab later recalled that about 10 or 11 August he was still expecting the award on 13 August at latest, and that on 12 or 13 August he was told it would not be announced until after the transfer of power.[^src-jenkins-19480407 p707]

::: episode chapter=2

Jinnah told Pakistan's Constituent Assembly that in his judgment “there was no other solution”, and that its people were “free to go to your temples”.[^src-jinnah-19470811 whole] Nehru moved the pledge in India's Constituent Assembly at the midnight session, saying it was redeemed “not wholly or in full measure, but very substantially”, and, in the Hindustani speech the Debates translate, that “Not far from Delhi, big cities are ablaze and its heat is reaching us here”.[^src-cad-19470814 sec53612][^src-cad-19470814 sec53610]

In Bengal the award left Murshidabad west of the line and turned south along the Khulna and 24-Parganas boundary to the sea, so Khulna went east.[^src-radcliffe-bengal item5][^src-radcliffe-bengal item8] Gandhi, at a prayer meeting in Calcutta after the award, deplored that “Khulna was to celebrate” its entry into Pakistan and said the award must be regarded as final “except to the extent that the ministers of the two Dominions agreed to vary it”.[^src-gandhi-19470824 para6] In Sylhet, 8 of 55 thanas had non-Muslim majorities, and the chairman found that a division on majorities alone would leave the six non-Muslim thanas divorced from the rest of Assam and sever the railway, so he exchanged territory.[^src-radcliffe-sylhet para10][^src-radcliffe-sylhet para12][^src-radcliffe-sylhet para13]

### 5. The Punjab, August to December 1947

Before the award, the Governor of the Punjab had reported the Boundary Force's strength as “say 7500 effective rifles to control 12 districts with a population of no less than 12 million”.[^src-top-xii item382] On 27 August 1947 India's High Commissioner in Lahore telegraphed Nehru that the attitude of the provincial government and of the Boundary Force was “most unhelpful”, and that a conference at Government House that morning had been attended by Lady Mountbatten, Amrit Kaur, the provincial ministers, Ghazanfar Ali and Governor Mudie.[^src-hc-lahore-19470827 p502] He wrote that “transfer of population is inevitable and absolutely essential if major disasters are to be avoided”, that the conference had felt it should take place, and that Chaman Lal feared that 40,000 lives were in danger during the next 48 hours.[^src-hc-lahore-19470827 p502-503] On 29 August 1947 the Joint Defence Council met at Lahore with the Governors-General and the Prime Ministers of both Dominions present.[^src-jdc-19470829 p503] It approved the Supreme Commander's proposals, under which the Punjab Boundary Force could be disbanded from midnight on 31st August.[^src-jdc-19470829 p504]

::: exhibit ex-jdc-29aug

Within a week after 15 August, as the officer who later ran the resettlement of East Punjab recorded, about 11 lakh Hindu and Sikh refugees had crossed from West to East Punjab, and in the following week another 25 lakhs had collected in “concentration camps” in West Punjab.[^src-randhawa-1954 secix] By the joint plan of the two Dominions' evacuation organisations, dated 20 October 1947, an estimated 21 lakhs of Muslims had moved into West Punjab since the first of August and 20 lakhs of non-Muslims had left for East Punjab.[^src-joint-evacuation-plan-19471020 p548] The balance it gave as still to move, about 32 lakhs of Muslims and 18 lakhs of non-Muslims, is the whole minority population of 1941 on each side, 53 lakhs and 38 lakhs, less those already moved.[^src-joint-evacuation-plan-19471020 p548] By the end of November 1947 the total camp population had reached 7,20,000, and the one big camp planned at Kurukshetra had been meant for up to five lakhs.[^src-randhawa-1954 p30] The Commons was told in December that the refugees who had moved between East and West Punjab, both ways, numbered “8,500,000 or thereabouts”, and the economists put all the displaced at “14.5 million people” within four years.[^src-hansard-19471218 p1845][^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p5]

::: exhibit ex-refugee-train

Khushi Muhammad Jut, born at Aloona Tola in the district of Ludhiana and seven years old when Pakistan was created, recalled his elders saying “For generations we have been living here, how can we leave our homes?”, and then Sikh migrants arriving to press their brethren to push the Muslims out, who “even planned to attack the village”.[^src-pa-khushi-muhammad para1][^src-pa-khushi-muhammad para8] A friend of his father's from Jandali sent a horseman to tip them off to leave the village, and his father hid the family in a field near Nasrali on the land of Shadi Singh.[^src-pa-khushi-muhammad para8] Niamat Ali Malak, born at the village of Lasoi in the princely state of Patiala, recalled the announcement that Pakistan had been made and people changing sides, with both communities attacking each other when they found the chance.[^src-pa-niamat-ali para1][^src-pa-niamat-ali para8] Bhagwant Singh Jawanda, born in 1924 in his father's village of Shanker in Ludhiana district, recalled Jinnah at Bradlaugh Hall in March 1947 telling the crowd “this is March, so march on, march on, march on!”, and Master Tara Singh advising the students at his hostel to walk in groups of 2 or 3.[^src-pa-bhagwant-singh para1][^src-pa-bhagwant-singh para5]

Sarla Dutta grew up in Mirpur, then under Jammu, which she says was badly affected by the riots of 1947; the people walked out together in a kafila.[^src-pa-sarla-dutta para4] Her father was killed on the way, and she and two or three other girls were abducted; she was converted, renamed Anwar, and kept in the house of her abductor, a father of three.[^src-pa-sarla-dutta para5]

::: exhibit ex-waris-shah

The poem calls to Waris Shah to speak from the grave and turn a new page of the book of love, because one daughter of Punjab had wept and lakhs were weeping, and the Chenab was full of blood.[^src-amrita-pritam-1949 sec1]

### 6. Kashmir, 1947 to 1972

At the end of October the Commons was told, “According to the information I have received”, that some 2,000 armed raiders had entered Kashmir on or about 22 October, and that “It is alleged that they came in reprisal for attacks on Moslems in some parts of Kashmir reported by refugees”.[^src-hansard-19471030 p1086] The Maharaja had appealed to India on 26 October and asked leave to accede, and on 27 October his accession was accepted “subject to a reference to the people of Kashmir, when law and order had been restored”.[^src-hansard-19471030 p1086] On 20 January 1948 the Security Council set up a commission of representatives of three member states, one selected by India, one by Pakistan and the third by those two.[^src-un-sres39 op1] Its resolution 47 of 21 April 1948 noted that both governments desired the accession “decided through the democratic method of a free and impartial plebiscite” and recommended measures to bring it about, beginning with the withdrawal of tribesmen and Pakistani nationals.[^src-un-sres47 pre1][^src-un-sres47 pre3][^src-un-sres47 op1]

India and Pakistan agreed a ceasefire to take place at one minute before midnight on December 31, 1948, Schofield writes, and the Commission adopted another resolution on January 5, 1949.[^src-schofield-2022 p23] Schofield writes that the Karachi Agreement of July 27, 1949 set a ceasefire line, 830 km long, roughly dividing the state between the two countries, and stipulated that a United Nations observer group would observe and report on complaints of its violation.[^src-schofield-2022 p24] In its resolution 91 of 30 March 1951 the Security Council reminded the Governments and authorities concerned of the principle that the final disposition of the State would be made “through the democratic method of a free and impartial plebiscite conducted under the auspices of the United Nations”.[^src-un-sres91 pre1][^src-un-sres91 pre4] It affirmed that a constituent assembly convened in the State, and anything that assembly did to decide its future, would not constitute a disposition of the State in accordance with that principle.[^src-un-sres91 pre5] In 1972, Schofield writes, following Pakistan's loss of its eastern wing, which became Bangladesh, Indira Gandhi and Zulfikar Ali Bhutto signed the Simla agreement.[^src-schofield-2022 p30] Under it, she writes, the ceasefire line, renamed the line of control, was to be respected “without prejudice to the respective positions of each side”.[^src-schofield-2022 p31] Schofield's article records that the plebiscite had not been held, the state remaining divided in fact between India and Pakistan.[^src-schofield-2022 p17]

### 7. Resettlement and citizenship

By the resettlement officer's own figures, the refugee landholders from the west were settled on less land than they had left: 67 lakh acres abandoned by Hindu and Sikh landholders in West Pakistan against 47 lakh acres available in East Punjab and Pepsu, a gap of 20 lakh acres which he gives as 38 per cent of the area abandoned, measured in standard acres, and which “precluded possibilities of full compensation”.[^src-randhawa-1954 p93] Of irrigated land, he records, the Hindu and Sikh landholders had left 43 lakh acres in the west, and the Muslims 13 lakh acres in the east.[^src-randhawa-1954 p93] In Pakistan, the census of 1951, as Abbas, Gillani and Ahmad cite it, counted “7.15 Million people migrated from India to Pakistan out of which 4.8 million in the province of Punjab alone”.[^src-abbas-2020 p164] They write that the Thal Development Authority settled “more than thirtyone thousand families of refugees in the Thal desert”.[^src-abbas-2020 abstract] India's census of 1951 enumerated “7.3 million displaced persons”, of whom “4.7 and 2.55 million had come from West and East Pakistan, respectively”.[^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p40]

::: analysis an-two-censuses

The Constitution of India of 1950 deemed a migrant from Pakistan a citizen if he, a parent or a grandparent had been born in India as the Government of India Act, 1935 defined it, and he had come “before the nineteenth day of July, 1948” and lived in India since, or, coming later, had been registered as a citizen on an application made before the Constitution began, after at least six months' residence.[^src-coi-art6 art6] A person who had migrated to Pakistan “after the first day of March, 1947” was not deemed a citizen of India, unless he had returned under a permit for resettlement or permanent return.[^src-coi-art7 art7] The two governments decided in December 1947, as the editor of the Punjab documents summarises it, that the conversion of persons abducted after 1 March 1947 was not to be recognised and that all such persons were to be restored to their Dominions “even against the wishes of persons concerned”.[^src-kirpal-intro-recovery secxxxi] India's Chief Liaison Officer in Lahore wrote to the Chief Secretary of East Punjab at the end of April 1948 that in Sheikhupura about 100 abducted women were still to be recovered, that the District Liaison Officer had clues to 47 of them, and that from the start of April to 25 April only 16 women and 10 children had been recovered; in Sargodha about 100 women were still to be recovered, 28 of them reported to be with Pathans.[^src-clo-19480429 p610-611] In the east the crossing ran on: Chatterji writes that between 1947 and 1967 “at least 6 million Hindu refugees from East Bengal crossed into West Bengal”.[^src-chatterji-2007 p1-2]

### 8. What the partition left

The rivers the award had cut were divided by treaty.{i} The Indus Waters Treaty was signed at Karachi on 19 September 1960 by Nehru for India and Ayub Khan for Pakistan, with the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development a party.[^src-indus-waters-treaty-1960 p126] It defined the eastern rivers as the Sutlej, the Beas and the Ravi, and the western rivers as the Indus, the Jhelum and the Chenab.[^src-indus-waters-treaty-1960 art1] All the waters of the eastern rivers were to be available for India's unrestricted use, “except as otherwise expressly provided in this Article”, and Pakistan was to receive for unrestricted use all the waters of the western rivers that India was obliged to let flow.[^src-indus-waters-treaty-1960 art2][^src-indus-waters-treaty-1960 art3] India agreed to pay 62,060,000 pounds sterling towards works to replace the supplies of canals in Pakistan that had depended on the eastern rivers on 15th August 1947.[^src-indus-waters-treaty-1960 art5] Gilmartin reads the award as the work of a chairman who saw water as the most important of the other factors he was allowed to weigh.[^src-gilmartin-2024 p85-86]

On the eastern border, Rabbani, from the Asiatic Society of Bangladesh, quotes the Protocol of 2011 under which “111 Indian enclaves in Bangladesh” and 51 Bangladeshi enclaves in India were to be exchanged.[^src-rabbani-2024 p101-102][^src-rabbani-2024 p102] The Land Boundary Agreement of 2015 transferred them from the midnight of July 31, 2015.[^src-rabbani-2024 p102] In Kashmir the line of 1949 became the line of control of 1972, and the plebiscite the Security Council called for had not been held when Schofield wrote.[^src-schofield-2022 p24][^src-schofield-2022 p30][^src-schofield-2022 p31][^src-schofield-2022 p17]

## The contested questions

### 1. The census and the notional line

The plan's own instrument was the notional division: the assemblies of Bengal and the Punjab would sit in two parts according to Muslim-majority and non-Muslim-majority districts, and until a commission's report was put into effect “the provisional boundaries indicated in the Appendix to the White Paper will be used”.[^src-hansard-19470603 p37] The appendix lists the Muslim-majority districts by the 1941 census: in Bengal the listed districts of the Chittagong, Dacca, Presidency and Rajshahi divisions; in the Punjab the listed districts of the Lahore, Rawalpindi and Multan divisions, Gurdaspur among them.[^src-top-xi item45]

::: exhibit ex-appendix-3june

The commissions did not work from surveys of their own.{i} Answering the commission's secretaries on 23 July 1947, Sir George Abell “did not attach the map but suggested that any map if read with the population statistics in the 1941 census would suffice for the purpose”.[^src-top-xii item196] The parties argued from that census, and against it.{i} The League-nominated judge wrote that the Muslims of Gurdaspur were 51.14 per cent of its people against 48.86 non-Muslim, and printed the four tehsils' communities from the 1941 census.[^src-dinmohammad-1947 p389-390][^src-dinmohammad-1947 p383-385] The Congress-nominated judge took the same district total of 1,153,511, with 589,923 Muslims and 563,488 non-Muslims, and set against it an affidavit on an arithmetical error in the Shakargarh register that would reduce the Muslim majority from 26,000 to 20,000.[^src-mahajan-1947 p327-328] The event record's claim that the maps Radcliffe worked from had not been resurveyed since the census before last is carried by no document this record holds, and the episode's line that he worked from the last census is.{i}

### 2. The commission and the line

Radcliffe did not attend the public sittings in person, because the Bengal commission was sitting at the same time, but “made arrangements to study daily the record of the proceedings”.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para5] After the sittings the commission adjourned to Simla, where the divergence between the members proved so wide that no agreed line was possible, and they assented to his giving his own decision.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para6] The Prime Minister had told the Commons that Radcliffe “has accepted the request made to him by all parties in the Government of India to be chairman of both Commissions”.[^src-hansard-19470710 p2448]

::: exhibit ex-award-punjab-9

The award names its own reasons.{i} Radcliffe hesitated “long over those not inconsiderable areas east of the Sutlej River and in the angle of the Beas and Sutlej Rivers in which Muslim majorities are found”, and left them to the East Punjab, in the true interests of neither State and because of “the disruption of railway communications and water systems”.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para10] He could not preserve the Upper Bari Doab Canal undivided, or the Mandi hydro-electric scheme, and hoped for joint control by agreement.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para11] He wrote that the award could not satisfy “sentiments and aspirations deeply held on either side” and that the means to gratify them lay in political arrangements with which he was not concerned.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para12]

::: analysis an-notional-vs-award

In Bengal the report says the province offers few satisfactory natural boundaries and that its development “has been on lines that do not well accord with a division by contiguous majority areas”.[^src-radcliffe-bengal para7] Its questions were whether Calcutta could be divided, what territory the city needed, whether the river line could displace the Muslim majorities of Jessore and Nadia, whether Khulna could be held apart from Jessore, and to which State Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri and the Chittagong Hill Tracts should go, the last with a Muslim population of only 3 per cent.[^src-radcliffe-bengal para8] The colleagues could not agree on any of these, and the chairman decided.[^src-radcliffe-bengal para9]

### 3. Gurdaspur

Every count of the district's people in the record puts the Muslim share a little over half, and the disagreement is over which count should rule.{i} The Sikh committee's note to Cripps tabled the district at 50.8 per cent Muslim by the 1931 census and called the three central districts balanced.[^src-sikh-note-1942 p7][^src-sikh-note-1942 p6-7] The Governor of the Punjab wrote, in the note he sent Lord Wavell, the Viceroy, that March, that Gurdaspur had 590,000 Muslims and 560,000 non-Muslims, that the Pathankot tehsil was predominantly non-Muslim, and that the other three tehsils had small Muslim majorities.[^src-jenkins-punjab-problem-1947 p19-20] Asked on 4 June 1947 why the final boundaries would almost certainly differ from the provisional ones, the Viceroy answered that Gurdaspur's population was “50.4 per cent Muslims”, adding that he thought so, and that with a difference of 0.8 per cent it was unlikely the commission would throw the whole district into the Muslim majority areas.[^src-mountbatten-press-19470604 p99-100] The League's memorandum proposed a line on which every tehsil of Gurdaspur but Pathankot stayed in the West Punjab and the Ajnala tehsil of Amritsar joined it.[^src-league-memo-1947 p184-185] The League's judge put the Muslim share at 51.14 per cent and the tehsils at 52.1, 55.06, 51.3 and 38.8.[^src-dinmohammad-1947 p389-390][^src-dinmohammad-1947 p383-385] The Congress's judge answered with the electoral rolls, 74,790 Muslim voters against 81,525 non-Muslim, and asked why the plebiscite test used in Sylhet and the Frontier should not apply to a district whose population majority was 1 per cent or a little more.[^src-mahajan-1947 p328-329]

::: analysis an-gurdaspur-counts

The schedule follows the Ujh river down the western boundary of the Pathankot tahsil to the point where the Pathankot, Shakargarh and Gurdaspur tahsils meet, and then the Ravi along the boundary between the Gurdaspur and Shakargarh tahsils and between Batala and Shakargarh, so Shakargarh went west and the other three tahsils east.[^src-radcliffe-punjab item1][^src-radcliffe-punjab item2] Lord Ismay received a message from Liaquat Ali Khan whose gist, as he restated it, was that Gurdaspur or a large portion of it had been given to East Punjab, that the report was that this was “a political decision, and not a judicial one”, and that if so it was a grave injustice amounting to a breach of faith.[^src-top-xii item428] Ismay answered that the Viceroy had from the outset made it clear he must have nothing to do with the commissions and that the Indian leaders had themselves selected them, drafted their terms and undertaken to implement the award.[^src-top-xii item428]

The Viceroy's own record of an interview eight days before the award was signed has him telling the Nawab of Bhopal that Kashmir “was so placed geographically that it could join either Dominion, provided part of Gurdaspur were put into East Punjab by the Boundary Commission”.[^src-top-xii item335] That sentence is the only document in this record that ties Gurdaspur to Kashmir before the award, and it is the Viceroy's, not Radcliffe's.{i} The award's reasons do not mention Kashmir, and the schedule names the State of Kashmir only as the place where the west branch of the Ujh enters the Punjab.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para9][^src-radcliffe-punjab para11][^src-radcliffe-punjab item1] The two stated grounds that name Gurdaspur or its tahsils are the Upper Bari Doab Canal from Madhopur in Pathankot, which the chairman could not preserve undivided, and the Mandi hydro-electric scheme, which supplies Gurdaspur among the districts it names and which he could not keep under one jurisdiction; the passage on the Sutlej and the Ferozepore headworks concerns Ferozepore, not Gurdaspur.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para11][^src-radcliffe-punjab para10] Those grounds are what the Pakistani account's charge of a political decision is set against, and the award says no more of them than that.{i} The record therefore shows a motive on the Viceroy's side and no document that carries it to the chairman, and this thesis says no more than that.{i}

### 4. The Ferozepur salient

The Pakistani state's edition of Jinnah's papers holds that Mountbatten and Radcliffe acted in league with the Congress leadership, and that the Punjab award was finalised by Radcliffe about a week before partition and then gerrymandered in favour of India.[^src-jinnah-papers-v-intro secxiii] That is the edition's claim, and no document this record holds tests the collusion it asserts.{i} The documents this record holds on the change are all British.{i} Abell's letter of 8 August 1947 enclosed a map “showing roughly the boundary which Sir Cyril Radcliffe proposes to demarcate in his award” and said there would not be any great changes from it.[^src-abell-19470808 p456] In a letter Lord Ismay telegraphed to Mountbatten in April 1948, Jenkins described what the enclosures showed.[^src-jenkins-19480407 p706] They showed, he wrote, a boundary which included in Pakistan “a sharp salient in the Ferozepur District”, enclosing the whole of the Ferozepur and Zira tehsils.[^src-jenkins-19480407 p706-707] About 10 or 11 August, he wrote, he received a message from Viceroy's House containing the words “Eliminate salient”, and those told of the expected boundary were “appraised of this change”.[^src-jenkins-19480407 p707] The change caused some surprise, he wrote, “not because the Ferozepur salienthad been regarded as inevitable or even probable, but because it seemed odd that any advance information had been given by the Commission if the award was not substantially complete”.[^src-jenkins-19480407 p707] Mountbatten recorded that Liaquat Ali Khan told the Joint Defence Council that the documents showed it had been intended to allot the Ferozepur and Zira tehsils to Pakistan.[^src-mountbatten-19480319 p703] The award ran the line down the Sutlej on the boundary between the districts of Lahore and Ferozepore, which left Ferozepore in East Punjab, and its chairman wrote that he had hesitated long over the Muslim-majority areas east of the Sutlej before deciding that to extend the West Punjab to a strip on the far side of the river would be in the true interests of neither State.[^src-radcliffe-punjab item4][^src-radcliffe-punjab para10] The record therefore holds a forecast line, a message from Viceroy's House after which a change was made known, and an award that gives a reason for the line without the salient; no document here says who decided the change or why.{i} The change it records is in Ferozepur; the Pakistani charge of a political decision in Gurdaspur, argued above, rests on other documents.{i}

### 5. The force

::: episode chapter=5

In the first of two reports on the Force, the Governor of the Punjab told the Viceroy that its Commander put his 5 brigade groups at an average of 1500 effective rifles, “say 7500 effective rifles to control 12 districts with a population of no less than 12 million”, and that static troops and training centres would add another 1500.[^src-top-xii item382] In the second, a letter written the day after the award was signed, he wrote that the population of the districts was distributed over 17,932 inhabited towns and villages, that he had estimated the need at a minimum of about 20,000 effective fighting men, and gave the effective strength as about 7,500, or about 9,000 with static troops.[^src-top-xii item459] The episode's count of the Force is not one this record holds.{i}

::: exhibit ex-jenkins-13aug

::: analysis an-rifles-per-head

The same telegram said that rural raiding in areas where the communities were inextricably mixed could not be checked except by a display and use of force on a massive scale, and said interception of raiders was vital.[^src-top-xii item382] The Joint Defence Council, with both Governors-General present, ended the Force from midnight on 31st August, and the Supreme Commander told it that mixed units could by then be split to a very great extent.[^src-jdc-19470829 p503][^src-jdc-19470829 p504] What the Governor described is a force that had been asked for and counted before the line existed, and found short the day after the line was signed.{i}

### 6. Calcutta, 1946

The counts of the Calcutta dead disagree, and so does the record on who began the killing.{i} The British government told the Commons of some 4,000 deaths and 10,000 injured; the Indian fact-finding body gave 570 dead in the hospital figures and reports an estimate of “more than five thousand persons”.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1350-1351][^src-khosla-1949 p66] The same body reports the Statesman's running total rising from “over 170 persons” killed on 18 August to 3,468 bodies accounted for on 28 August.[^src-khosla-1949 p78]

::: analysis an-calcutta-counts

The fact-finding body wrote that if the hospital figures were accepted as a basis for the ratio, “the Muslims fared almost as badly the Hindus”, and wrote that when this came to light the League's leaders and press said the riot had been started by the supporters of the Congress.[^src-khosla-1949 p66] Jinnah's own words in Bombay that August were that he did not know who was responsible, and that those who had used violence had played “into the hands of the enemies of the Muslim League”.[^src-jinnah-papers-xiv p642] The words of the League's resolution of 29 July held here, as Cripps read them to the Commons, resolve on direct action to achieve Pakistan and do not describe the action.[^src-hansard-19461212 p1348] The Indian fact-finding body wrote that its meaning was not left in doubt: it meant the “forging of a pistol” and using it.[^src-khosla-1949 p41] No document in this record investigates the first day of the riot, and each account of who began it is a party's.{i}

### 7. The count

The Indian fact-finding body examined “nearly fifteen thousand witnesses” who bore testimony to the murder of “fifty thousand non-Muslims”, and said the incidents deposed to represented only a fraction of the devastation.[^src-khosla-1949 p298] Taking that into account it estimated the loss of non-Muslim life “at a figure between 200,000 and 250,000”, and believed “an equal number of Muslims perished in the course of the riots in India”.[^src-khosla-1949 p299] The economists write that “By many accounts” the partition was a disaster “involving nearly a million deaths due to the riots”, and of “a mass migration and population transfer of nearly 17 million people”, and put the displaced at “14.5 million people” within four years of partition.[^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p1][^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p5] Printed side by side, and not added: the fact-finding body's 200,000 to 250,000 is of non-Muslim dead, with an equal number of Muslims it believed perished in India, while Abbas, Gillani and Ahmad, from Bahawalpur, write of the partition's “death of 0.5 Million” in all, citing a work of 1953.[^src-khosla-1949 p299][^src-abbas-2020 p164] The figures are set beside each other in the disagreements below, and this thesis states no single number.{i}

### 8. The women

The question the record leaves open is whether recovery rescued the women it found or took them a second time.{i} When an army search came looking for abducted girls, Sarla Dutta's abductor put her inside the seed drum, and she slid her hand through an opening and screamed; she was 13.[^src-pa-sarla-dutta para6] The rule the two governments made in December 1947, as the editor of the Punjab documents summarises it, restored such persons to their Dominions “even against the wishes of persons concerned”, and the passage of the liaison officer's letter held here counts the recovered without recording what any of them wished.[^src-kirpal-intro-recovery secxxxi][^src-clo-19480429 p610-611]

::: exhibit ex-sarla-dutta

Dey reads Sidhwa's novel of abduction and recovery as showing a patriarchal ideology that turned women into mute signs, and language itself failing to articulate what was done to them.[^src-dey-2018 p28] Sherman, Gould and Ansari name refugee rehabilitation and the recovery of abducted women as the low-level state programmes through which recent work has bridged the study of high and low politics in the two new states.[^src-sherman-gould-ansari-2010 p2]

Jinnah's address to the Constituent Assembly, given four days before the appointed day, is held here whole, and it is where the Pakistani position states itself.{i} He told the Assembly that the division of India and the partition of the Punjab and Bengal had been accepted and that it was now the duty of everyone to abide by it, that in his judgment “there was no other solution”, and that “You are free; you are free to go to your temples, you are free to go to your mosques or to any other places of worship in this State of Pakistan”.[^src-jinnah-19470811 whole] Nehru's motion of the same week is held in the same way.{i} He said the pledge was being redeemed “not wholly or in full measure, but very substantially”, and, in the Hindustani speech the Debates translate, that “Not far from Delhi, big cities are ablaze and its heat is reaching us here”.[^src-cad-19470814 sec53612][^src-cad-19470814 sec53610] The Sikh judge gave the Sikhs of the British Punjab by the 1941 census as 3,767,401, of whom 1,683,855 lived in the western districts of the notional division and 2,073,546 in the east; the two parts add to less than the total he gives.[^src-tejasingh-1947 p353-354] Of those in the west, he counted 1,368,962 in the six districts that would border the new line.[^src-tejasingh-1947 p353-354] The Sikh compilation writes of the uprooting “of over twelve millions, and the butchering of about a million”.[^src-talib-1950 p43] Hanif records that at the Punjab Assembly's meeting of 23 June 1947 the vote was 88 for remaining with India and 91 for joining Pakistan, and that the majority came from the votes of the three Christian members, Singha, Gibbon and Fazal Elahi, with Singha's additional vote as Speaker.[^src-hanif-2020 p844][^src-hanif-2020 p845-846] Gibbon, Hanif adds, was summoned before the commission between 21 and 31 July.[^src-hanif-2020 p845-846]

## What the record omits

- No source in this record is by Radcliffe other than the awards themselves; his reasons are known here only as the awards state them. {against=pakistani-nationalist}
- No source in this record says who decided the change to the Ferozepur salient, or why; the message from Viceroy's House and the change made known after it are known here only as the Governor of the Punjab recalled them in April 1948. {against=british-administrative}
- No source in this record is the Government of Bengal's report on the Calcutta killings, or the League's own count of its dead; the League's case is held as its president's statements. {against=pakistani-nationalist}
- No source in this record is a Muslim woman's own account of abduction or recovery; the one first-person account of abduction is by a girl who was converted and renamed, and the Muslim survivors are known here through an archive's summaries. {against=women-and-recovery}
- No source in this record is a district file, a police diary or a railway log of the Punjab in August 1947; the killing is known here through a Governor's telegrams, a High Commissioner's telegram, a fact-finding body and survivors. {against=british-administrative}
- No source in this record is the Punjab volume of the census of 1941 with its tables by community; the all-India volume held gives district totals, not communities, and the community figures are known here as the commission's members and the Governor reproduce them. {against=indian-nationalist}
- No source in this record is a Sikh survivor's first-person testimony; the one Sikh voice on the violence written in the first person is a poem, and the one Sikh testimony is an archive's summary. {against=sikh-panthic}
- No source in this record counts the dead of Bengal in 1947, or the Muslims who moved east; the eastern count is of Hindus leaving. {against=bengal-long-partition}
- No source in this record counts the land and houses Muslim evacuees left in East Punjab or received in West Punjab, except through the East Punjab administration's own figures. {against=the-displaced}
- No source in this record is a Bengali survivor's testimony; of the four testimonies held, three are of the Punjab and one of Mirpur, and the eastern partition is known here through scholarship, the award and the Governor's report of the vote. {against=bengal-long-partition}
- No source in this record is a Dalit voice; the one work on caste inside the migration is paywalled, and no testimony held names caste. {against=women-and-recovery}
- No source in this record is in Urdu, Hindi or Bengali, and the one Punjabi source is a poem; every argument here is made from documents written in English. {against=the-counters}
