## The question

::: episode chapter=0

The statement read to the House of Commons when the plan was announced told the two provincial assemblies to meet in two parts, and it named the count they would be divided by: “For the purpose of determining the population of districts, the 1941 census figures will be taken as authoritative.”[^src-hansard-19470603 p36] The Boundary Commissions were to be instructed “to demarcate the boundaries of the two parts of the Punjab on the basis of ascertaining the contiguous majority areas of Muslims and non-Muslims”, and to take into account other factors.[^src-hansard-19470603 p37] The Punjab commission sat in public at Lahore from 21 July to 31 July 1947, and was asked to decide before 15 August.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para5][^src-radcliffe-punjab para3] Its chairman signed the award at New Delhi on 12 August 1947.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para12]

This thesis asks what the commissions worked from, why Gurdaspur went to India, and what the free record settles about the force that was to hold the Punjab, the dead and the displaced. It makes three claims. First, that the notional line and the terms of reference are in the record, that the census figures the commissions argued from are in it as the members reproduce them, and that every count of Gurdaspur's people in it puts the district within 2 points of half. Second, that the award placed 4 districts across the notional line, and that the only document tying Gurdaspur to Kashmir before the award is the Viceroy's own, with nothing to show it reached Radcliffe. Third, that the Boundary Force had, the police apart, one effective rifle for at least 1,600 people by its Governor's count, and that the dead and the displaced are unlike counts, which this thesis sets side by side and does not reduce to one number.

## The record

The primary documents this thesis holds were fetched from the UK legislation and Hansard services, from the digitised Constituent Assembly Debates, from the National Assembly of Pakistan, from the United Nations document system and from scans on the Internet Archive, and every passage below was sliced from that copy by a script rather than retyped.{i} The two awards and the Sylhet report are held from the Government of India's own reprint, paragraph by paragraph and schedule paragraph by schedule paragraph.{i} Three of the four reports of the Punjab commission's members, the Muslim League's memorandum, the Governor of the Punjab's note to the Viceroy of that March, the Viceroy's press conference and the liaison agency's recovery letter are held from Kirpal Singh's documentary collection, and the pages given are the collection's.{i} The Transfer of Power volumes are Crown copyright, so they are held only as at most five passages each, and Mountbatten's own last report is among what the cap leaves out.{i} The scholarship is held only as the passages cited, and one source is in Punjabi, with a rendering marked as this thesis's own.{i} What the record does not hold is listed under the gaps below, by name, with what was tried.{i} The largest are the Punjab volume of the census the commission argued from, which is known here only as the commission's members reproduce it; the report of the second League-nominated judge, which the collection carries and this pass did not slice; the two governments' submissions to the Security Council, which were fetched but whose text layers are unreadable; the one peer-reviewed demographic count of the Punjab's dead, which is paywalled; and every Urdu, Hindi and Bengali source that could be found, none of which could be read as text.{i} The Indian fact-finding body's own statement of the limits of counting comes first.{i} It wrote that the census returns of 1941 “were not accurate even at the time they were compiled”, that they became “hopelessly wrong, at the end of six years”, and that its own witnesses covered 2,094 of the 19,914 villages of West Punjab.[^src-khosla-1949 p298]

## The argument

### 1. The date and the census

The Act set the appointed day as “the fifteenth day of August, nineteen hundred and forty-seven”, and set up two Dominions, India and Pakistan.[^src-iia-1947 sec1] From that day the suzerainty of the Crown over the Indian States lapsed, and with it every treaty and agreement between the Crown and their rulers.[^src-iia-1947 sec7] The Prime Minister told the Commons that the time given to the Bill was not commensurate with its importance and that the Government was constrained by the urgency of the matter.[^src-hansard-19470710 p2447] In the Lords the Secretary of State said the House was being asked for a passage “so rapid as to be almost unprecedented” and that it might appear “a degree of haste that is almost unseemly”.[^src-hansard-lords-19470716 p802]

The plan's own instrument was the notional division: the assemblies of Bengal and the Punjab would sit in two parts according to Muslim-majority and non-Muslim-majority districts, and until a commission's report was put into effect “the provisional boundaries indicated in the Appendix to the White Paper will be used”.[^src-hansard-19470603 p37] The appendix lists the Muslim-majority districts by the 1941 census: in Bengal the listed districts of the Chittagong, Dacca, Presidency and Rajshahi divisions; in the Punjab the listed districts of the Lahore, Rawalpindi and Multan divisions, Gurdaspur among them.[^src-top-xi item45]

::: exhibit ex-appendix-3june

The commissions did not work from surveys of their own.{i} Answering the commission's secretaries on 23 July 1947, Sir George Abell “did not attach the map but suggested that any map if read with the population statistics in the 1941 census would suffice for the purpose”.[^src-top-xii item196] The parties argued from that census, and against it.{i} The League-nominated judge wrote that the Muslims of Gurdaspur were 51.14 per cent of its people against 48.86 non-Muslim, and printed the four tehsils' communities from the 1941 census.[^src-dinmohammad-1947 p389-390][^src-dinmohammad-1947 p383-385] The Congress-nominated judge took the same district total of 1,153,511, with 589,923 Muslims and 563,488 non-Muslims, and set against it an affidavit on an arithmetical error in the Shakargarh register that would reduce the Muslim majority from 26,000 to 20,000.[^src-mahajan-1947 p327-328] The event record's claim that the maps Radcliffe worked from had not been resurveyed since the census before last is carried by no document this record holds, and the episode's line that he worked from the last census is.{i}

### 2. The commission and the line

The Punjab commission was constituted by the Governor-General's announcement of 30 June 1947, with Din Muhammad, Muhammad Munir, Mehr Chand Mahajan and Teja Singh as members and Radcliffe appointed chairman afterwards.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para2] Radcliffe did not attend the public sittings in person, because the Bengal commission was sitting at the same time, and had the daily record sent to him.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para5] After the sittings the commission adjourned to Simla, where the divergence between the members proved so wide that no agreed line was possible, and they assented to his giving his own decision.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para6] The Prime Minister had told the Commons that Radcliffe “has accepted the request made to him by all parties in the Government of India to be chairman of both Commissions”.[^src-hansard-19470710 p2448]

::: exhibit ex-award-punjab-9

The award names its own reasons.{i} Radcliffe hesitated “long over those not inconsiderable areas east of the Sutlej River and in the angle of the Beas and Sutlej Rivers in which Muslim majorities are found”, and left them to the East Punjab, in the true interests of neither State and because of “the disruption of railway communications and water systems”.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para10] He could not preserve the Upper Bari Doab Canal undivided, or the Mandi hydro-electric scheme, and hoped for joint control by agreement.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para11] He wrote that the award could not satisfy “sentiments and aspirations deeply held on either side” and that the means to gratify them lay in political arrangements with which he was not concerned.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para12] Gilmartin reads the same paragraphs as a chairman who saw water as the most important of the other factors.[^src-gilmartin-2024 p85-86]

::: analysis an-notional-vs-award

In Bengal the report says the province offers few satisfactory natural boundaries and that its development “has been on lines that do not well accord with a division by contiguous majority areas”.[^src-radcliffe-bengal para7] Its questions were whether Calcutta could be divided, what territory the city needed, whether the river line could displace the Muslim majorities of Jessore and Nadia, whether Khulna could be held apart from Jessore, and to which State Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri and the Chittagong Hill Tracts should go, the last with a Muslim population of only 3 per cent.[^src-radcliffe-bengal para8] The colleagues could not agree on any of these, and the chairman decided.[^src-radcliffe-bengal para9] The schedule leaves Murshidabad west of the line and turns south along the Khulna and 24-Parganas boundary to the sea, so Khulna went east.[^src-radcliffe-bengal item5][^src-radcliffe-bengal item8] Gandhi, at a prayer meeting in Calcutta after the award, deplored that “Khulna was to celebrate” its entry into Pakistan and said the award must be regarded as final “except to the extent that the ministers of the two Dominions agreed to vary it”.[^src-gandhi-19470824 para6] In Sylhet, 8 of 55 thanas had non-Muslim majorities, and the chairman found that a division on majorities alone would leave the six non-Muslim thanas divorced from the rest of Assam and sever the railway, so he exchanged territory.[^src-radcliffe-sylhet para10][^src-radcliffe-sylhet para12][^src-radcliffe-sylhet para13]

### 3. Gurdaspur

Every count of the district's people in the record puts the Muslim share a little over half, and the disagreement is over which count should rule.{i} The Sikh committee's note to Cripps tabled the district at 50.8 per cent Muslim by the 1931 census and called the three central districts balanced.[^src-sikh-note-1942 p7][^src-sikh-note-1942 p6-7] The Governor of the Punjab wrote, in the note he sent Lord Wavell, the Viceroy, that March, that Gurdaspur had 590,000 Muslims and 560,000 non-Muslims, that the Pathankot tehsil was predominantly non-Muslim, and that the other three tehsils had small Muslim majorities.[^src-jenkins-punjab-problem-1947 p19-20] Asked on 4 June 1947 why the final boundaries would almost certainly differ from the provisional ones, the Viceroy answered that Gurdaspur's population was “50.4 per cent Muslims”, and that with a difference of 0.8 per cent it was unlikely the commission would throw the whole district into the Muslim majority areas.[^src-mountbatten-press-19470604 p99-100] The League's memorandum proposed a line on which every tehsil of Gurdaspur but Pathankot stayed in the West Punjab and the Ajnala tehsil of Amritsar joined it.[^src-league-memo-1947 p184-185] The League's judge put the Muslim share at 51.14 per cent and the tehsils at 52.1, 55.06, 51.3 and 38.8.[^src-dinmohammad-1947 p389-390][^src-dinmohammad-1947 p383-385] The Congress's judge answered with the electoral rolls, 74,790 Muslim voters against 81,525 non-Muslim, and asked why the plebiscite test used in Sylhet and the Frontier should not apply to a district whose population majority was 1 per cent or a little more.[^src-mahajan-1947 p328-329]

::: analysis an-gurdaspur-counts

The schedule follows the Ujh river down the western boundary of the Pathankot tahsil to the point where the Pathankot, Shakargarh and Gurdaspur tahsils meet, and then the Ravi along the boundary between the Gurdaspur and Shakargarh tahsils and between Batala and Shakargarh, so Shakargarh went west and the other three tahsils east.[^src-radcliffe-punjab item1][^src-radcliffe-punjab item2] Lord Ismay received a message from Liaquat Ali Khan whose gist, as he restated it, was that Gurdaspur or a large portion of it had been given to East Punjab, that the report was that this was “a political decision, and not a judicial one”, and that if so it was a grave injustice amounting to a breach of faith.[^src-top-xii item428] Ismay answered that the Viceroy had from the outset made it clear he must have nothing to do with the commissions and that the Indian leaders had themselves selected them, drafted their terms and undertaken to implement the award.[^src-top-xii item428]

The Viceroy's own record of an interview eight days before the award was signed has him telling the Nawab of Bhopal that Kashmir “was so placed geographically that it could join either Dominion, provided part of Gurdaspur were put into East Punjab by the Boundary Commission”.[^src-top-xii item335] That sentence is the only document in this record that ties Gurdaspur to Kashmir before the award, and it is the Viceroy's, not Radcliffe's.{i} The award's reasons do not mention Kashmir, and the schedule names the State of Kashmir only as the place where the west branch of the Ujh enters the Punjab.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para9][^src-radcliffe-punjab para11][^src-radcliffe-punjab item1] The two stated grounds that name Gurdaspur or its tahsils are the Upper Bari Doab Canal from Madhopur in Pathankot, which the chairman could not preserve undivided, and the Mandi hydro-electric scheme, which supplies Gurdaspur among the districts it names and which he could not keep under one jurisdiction; the passage on the Sutlej and the Ferozepore headworks concerns Ferozepore, not Gurdaspur.[^src-radcliffe-punjab para11][^src-radcliffe-punjab para10] Those grounds are what the Pakistani account's charge of a political decision is set against, and the award says no more of them than that.{i} The record therefore shows a motive on the Viceroy's side and no document that carries it to the chairman, and this thesis says no more than that.{i} At the end of October the Commons was told that some 2,000 armed raiders had entered Kashmir on or about 22 October, that the Maharaja had appealed to India on 26 October and asked leave to accede, and that on 27 October his accession was accepted “subject to a reference to the people of Kashmir”.[^src-hansard-19471030 p1086] The Security Council's resolution 47 of 21 April 1948 noted that both governments desired the accession “decided through the democratic method of a free and impartial plebiscite” and recommended measures to bring it about, beginning with the withdrawal of tribesmen and Pakistani nationals.[^src-un-sres47 pre1][^src-un-sres47 pre3][^src-un-sres47 op1] Schofield's reading is that the plebiscite has never been held, the state remaining divided in fact between the two countries, and her paper examines why it was not held.[^src-schofield-2022 p17]

### 4. The force

::: episode chapter=5

In the first of two reports on the Force, the Governor of the Punjab told the Viceroy that its Commander put his 5 brigade groups at an average of 1500 effective rifles, “say 7500 effective rifles to control 12 districts with a population of no less than 12 million”, and that static troops and training centres would add another 1500.[^src-top-xii item382] In the second, a letter written the day after the award was signed, he wrote that the population of the districts was distributed over 17,932 inhabited towns and villages, that he had estimated the need at a minimum of about 20,000 effective fighting men, and gave the effective strength as about 7,500, or about 9,000 with static troops.[^src-top-xii item459] The episode's count of the Force is not one this record holds.{i}

::: exhibit ex-jenkins-13aug

::: analysis an-rifles-per-head

The same telegram said that rural raiding in areas where the communities were inextricably mixed could not be checked except by a display and use of force on a massive scale, and said interception of raiders was vital.[^src-top-xii item382] What the Governor described is a force that had been asked for and counted before the line existed, and found short the day after the line was signed.{i}

### 5. The count

The Indian fact-finding body examined “nearly fifteen thousand witnesses” who bore testimony to the murder of “fifty thousand non-Muslims”, and said the incidents deposed to represented only a fraction of the devastation.[^src-khosla-1949 p298] Taking that into account it estimated the loss of non-Muslim life “at a figure between 200,000 and 250,000”, and believed “an equal number of Muslims perished in the course of the riots in India”.[^src-khosla-1949 p299] The economists write of “nearly a million deaths due to the riots” and of “a mass migration and population transfer of nearly 17 million people”, and put the displaced at “14.5 million people” within four years of partition.[^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p1][^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p5] The figures are set beside each other in the disagreements below, and this thesis states no single number.{i}

::: analysis an-migration-counts

The Commons was told in December that the number of refugees who had moved between East Punjab and West Punjab, both ways, “is 8,500,000 or thereabouts”, and the economists put the displaced at “14.5 million people” within four years.[^src-hansard-19471218 p1845][^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p5] They cite the 1951 census of India, which enumerated “7.3 million displaced persons”, of whom “4.7 and 2.55 million had come from West and East Pakistan, respectively”.[^src-bharadwaj-mirza-2019 p40] In the east the count runs on: Chatterji writes that between 1947 and 1967 “at least 6 million Hindu refugees from East Bengal crossed into West Bengal”.[^src-chatterji-2007 p1-2] Mahbub and Saba, from Dhaka, write that the violence on the eastern side was more protracted and more episodic, beginning with the Kolkata and Noakhali riots of 1946, while the west saw massacres from 1947 to 1950.[^src-mahbub-saba-2018 p109]

### 6. The women

Sarla Dutta was abducted on the road with two or three other girls, converted and renamed Anwar, and kept in the house of a man with three children.[^src-pa-sarla-dutta para5] When an army search came looking for abducted girls, her abductor put her inside the seed drum, and she slid her hand through an opening and screamed; she was 13.[^src-pa-sarla-dutta para6] The two governments had decided in December 1947, as the editor of the Punjab documents summarises it, that the conversion of persons abducted after 1 March 1947 was not to be recognised and that all such persons were to be restored to their Dominions “even against the wishes of persons concerned”.[^src-kirpal-intro-recovery secxxxi]

::: exhibit ex-sarla-dutta

The Chief Liaison Officer wrote to the Chief Secretary of East Punjab at the end of April 1948 that in Sheikhupura about 100 abducted women were still to be recovered, that the District Liaison Officer had clues to 47 of them, and that from the start of April to 25 April only 16 women and 10 children had been recovered; in Sargodha about 100 women were still to be recovered, 28 of them reported to be with Pathans.[^src-clo-19480429 p610-611] Dey reads Sidhwa's novel of abduction and recovery as showing a patriarchal ideology that turned women into mute signs, and language itself failing to articulate what was done to them.[^src-dey-2018 p28] Sherman, Gould and Ansari name refugee rehabilitation and the recovery of abducted women as the low-level state programmes through which recent work has bridged the study of high and low politics in the two new states.[^src-sherman-gould-ansari-2010 p2]

### 7. The villages

Khushi Muhammad Jut, born at Aloona Tola in the district of Ludhiana and seven years old when Pakistan was created, recalled his elders saying “For generations we have been living here, how can we leave our homes?”, and then Sikh migrants arriving to press their brethren to push the Muslims out.[^src-pa-khushi-muhammad para1][^src-pa-khushi-muhammad para8] A friend of his father's from Jandali sent a horseman to warn them, and his father hid the family in a field near Nasrali on the land of Shadi Singh, before the attack they had been warned of.[^src-pa-khushi-muhammad para8] Niamat Ali Malak, from a village in Patiala State, recalled the announcement that Pakistan had been made and people changing sides, with both communities attacking each other when they found the chance.[^src-pa-niamat-ali para8] Bhagwant Singh Jawanda, born in 1924 in his father's village of Shanker in Ludhiana district, recalled Jinnah at Bradlaugh Hall in March 1947 telling the crowd “this is March, so march on, march on, march on!”, and Master Tara Singh advising the students at his hostel to walk in groups of 2 or 3.[^src-pa-bhagwant-singh para1][^src-pa-bhagwant-singh para5]

The poem calls to Waris Shah to speak from the grave and turn a new page of the book of love, because one daughter of Punjab had wept and lakhs were weeping, and the Chenab was full of blood.[^src-amrita-pritam-1949 sec1]

Jinnah's address to the Constituent Assembly, given three days before the transfer, is held here whole, and it is where the Pakistani position states itself.{i} He told the Assembly that the division of India and the partition of the Punjab and Bengal had been accepted and that it was now the duty of everyone to abide by it, that in his judgment “there was no other solution”, and that “You are free; you are free to go to your temples, you are free to go to your mosques or to any other places of worship in this State of Pakistan”.[^src-jinnah-19470811 whole] Nehru's motion of the same week is held in the same way.{i} He said the pledge was being redeemed “not wholly or in full measure, but very substantially”, and, in the Hindustani speech the Debates translate, that “Not far from Delhi, big cities are ablaze and its heat is reaching us here”.[^src-cad-19470814 sec53612][^src-cad-19470814 sec53610] The Sikh judge counted 3,767,401 Sikhs in the British Punjab by the 1941 census, 1,683,855 of them in the western districts of the notional division and 2,073,546 in the east, and 1,368,962 in the border districts.[^src-tejasingh-1947 p353-354] The Sikh compilation writes of the uprooting “of over twelve millions, and the butchering of about a million”.[^src-talib-1950 p43] Hanif records that Gibbon was one of the three Punjab Assembly legislators who voted for Pakistan on 23 June 1947, and that he was summoned before the commission between 21 and 31 July.[^src-hanif-2020 p845-846] Rabbani, from the Asiatic Society of Bangladesh, quotes the Protocol of 2011 under which “111 Indian enclaves in Bangladesh” and 51 Bangladeshi enclaves in India were exchanged.[^src-rabbani-2024 p101-102][^src-rabbani-2024 p102]

## What the record omits

- No source in this record is by Radcliffe other than the awards themselves; his reasons are known here only as the awards state them. {against=pakistani-nationalist}
- No source in this record is a Muslim woman's own account of abduction or recovery; the one first-person account of abduction is by a girl who was converted and renamed, and the Muslim survivors are known here through an archive's summaries. {against=women-and-recovery}
- No source in this record is a district file, a police diary or a railway log of the Punjab in August 1947; the killing is known here through a Governor's telegrams, a fact-finding body and survivors. {against=british-administrative}
- No source in this record is the Punjab census of 1941 itself; its figures are known here as the commission's members and the Governor reproduce them. {against=indian-nationalist}
- No source in this record is a Sikh survivor's first-person testimony; the one Sikh voice in its own words is a poem, and the one Sikh testimony is an archive's summary. {against=sikh-panthic}
- No source in this record counts the dead of Bengal, or the Muslims who moved east; the eastern count is of Hindus leaving. {against=bengal-long-partition}
- No source in this record is a Bengali survivor's testimony; the four testimonies held are all of the Punjab, and the eastern partition is known here through scholarship and the award. {against=bengal-long-partition}
- No source in this record is a Dalit voice; the one work on caste inside the migration is paywalled, and no testimony held names caste. {against=women-and-recovery}
- No source in this record is in Urdu, Hindi or Bengali, and the one Punjabi source is a poem; every argument here is made from documents written in English. {against=the-counters}
