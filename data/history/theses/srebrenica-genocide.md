## The question

::: episode chapter=0

On 16 April 1993 the Security Council demanded that Srebrenica and its surroundings be treated as a safe area “free from any armed attack or any other hostile act”.[^src-un-sres-819 whole] The peacekeepers who held it were lightly armed and at any one time numbered no more than 600 men.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para20] The force around them was put at between 1,000 and 2,000 soldiers with tanks, armoured vehicles, artillery and mortars.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para21] The Trial Chamber that tried the first of the commanders found that the total number of men executed after the town fell is likely to lie within the range of 7,000 to 8,000.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para84]

This thesis asks whether the safe area was lost on 11 July 1995 or was never held, and what the free record settles about the men who were taken from it. It makes three claims. First, that the resolution and the force sent under it were never matched, and that the tribunal and the Secretariat agree on the numbers. Second, that the executions were organised, dated and located in the tribunal's own record, and that the forensic minimum in that record already exceeds the ceiling the denial position sets. Third, that the count of the dead is a range, and that the page states it as a range.

## The record

Every primary document this thesis holds was fetched from the tribunal, from the United Nations document system or from a court, and every passage below was sliced from that copy rather than retyped.{i} The judgments and the resolutions are held as extracts with a paragraph or an operative number; the scholarship is held only as the passages cited, and the Serbian passages are held in Serbian with a rendering that is marked as this thesis's own.{i} What the record does not hold is listed under the gaps below, by name, with what was tried; the largest gap is the judgment on state responsibility, which this thesis reaches only through the General Assembly's recital of it.{i} The tribunal's own statement of the limits of its count is the honest place to start.{i} It found it impossible to determine with precision the number of Bosnian Muslim men killed after the take-over, because the corpses had been broken up and intermingled when they were moved from primary to secondary graves; it recorded that the minimum number of bodies in the graves exhumed was 2028, against a total it put within the range of 7,000 to 8,000 men.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para80][^src-icty-krstic-tj para84]

## The argument

### 1. The safe area

The resolution of 16 April 1993 demanded that all parties treat Srebrenica as a safe area, demanded the immediate withdrawal of Bosnian Serb paramilitary units from the areas around it, and demanded that the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia cease supplying them.[^src-un-sres-819 whole] It requested the Secretary-General to increase the presence of UNPROFOR in Srebrenica, with a view to monitoring the humanitarian situation.[^src-un-sres-819 whole] A resolution that monitors is not a resolution that defends, and the Secretariat's own review says so.{i}

::: exhibit ex-res-819

The first UNPROFOR troops arrived on 18 April 1993, were rotated about every six months, and were “lightly armed and at any one time numbered no more than 600 men (a much smaller force than had been originally requested)”.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para20] They “manned thirteen observation posts” on the perimeter, and in January 1995 a battalion from the Netherlands rotated in.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para20] Around them, “Between 1,000 and 2,000 soldiers from three Drina Corps Brigades were deployed around the enclave”, equipped with tanks, armoured vehicles, artillery and mortars.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para21] The Secretariat put the same besieging force at 1,000 to 2,000 well-equipped soldiers from three brigades, and the enclave's own defenders as numerically superior, “3,000 to 4,000 men in the Twenty-eighth Division”, with no heavy weapons.[^src-un-a54549 para230]

The tribunal also heard that from the outset both parties violated the safe area agreement: a deliberate Bosnian Serb strategy limited the aid convoys, the Dutch commander testified that his personnel, equipment and ammunition were kept out, and some Bosnian Muslims in Srebrenica complained of attacks by Bosnian Serb soldiers.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para22] The Secretariat's conclusion, written four years after the fall, is that the safe areas “were established by the Security Council without the consent of the parties and without the provision of any credible military deterrent”.[^src-un-a54549 para499]

### 2. The fall

The offensive began in earnest on 6 July 1995, and in the following days the observation posts in the south of the enclave fell one by one.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para31] By the evening of 9 July the Drina Corps had pressed four kilometres into the enclave and halted one kilometre short of the town; late that night President Karadžić authorised the corps to capture it.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para33] On the morning of 10 July Colonel Karremans sent urgent requests for NATO air support, and no assistance came until around 1430 hours on 11 July 1995, when NATO bombed VRS tanks advancing towards the town.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para34] The Secretariat's account has the Serb flag hoisted above a bakery at the southern end of the town at 1407 hours, the residents fleeing north at approximately 1430, and “at least three (but possibly up to five) requests for air support by Dutchbat” turned down before that at various levels of the chain of command.[^src-un-a54549 para304] In the same account “Dutchbat had also not fired a single shot directly at the advancing Serb forces”.[^src-un-a54549 para304]

::: analysis an-clock-11-july

At approximately 1440 hours two NATO aircraft dropped a total of two bombs on what were thought to be Serb vehicles.[^src-un-a54549 para305] The Bosnian Serb army then radioed a threat to shell the town and the compound and to kill the Dutch soldiers it held, the Netherlands Minister of Defence telephoned the Special Representative to ask that the air action stop, and it was halted.[^src-un-a54549 para306] The tribunal records the same abandonment of the air strikes after threats to kill the Dutch troops in VRS custody and to shell the compound where 20,000 to 30,000 civilians had fled.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para34] Late in the afternoon General Mladić walked through the empty streets of the town with General Živanović and General Krstić, and the walk was filmed by a Serbian journalist.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para36]

By the evening of 11 July 1995 approximately 20,000 to 25,000 refugees were gathered at Potočari, and “Several thousand had pressed inside the UN compound itself”; witnesses estimated at least 300 men inside its perimeter and between 600 and 900 men in the crowd outside.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para37] The Dutch commander's message after meeting Mladić described “more than 15,000 people within one square kilometre, including the battalion, in an extreme vulnerable position”.[^src-un-a54549 para315] The Secretariat's own verdict on the battalion is that “the 150 fighting men of Dutchbat were lightly armed and in indefensible positions, and were faced with 2,000 Serbs advancing with the support of armour and artillery”.[^src-un-a54549 para472]

::: analysis an-crowd-counts

### 3. The executions

::: episode chapter=3

The men who did not go to Potočari left Šušnjari shortly after midnight on 12 July in a column that may have numbered up to 15,000, perhaps a third of them armed, and the last units left more than 12 hours after the first.[^src-un-a54549 para316] The tribunal's own table of contents lists the mass execution sites in the order the Drina Corps reached them, and dates them.[^src-icty-krstic-tj toc] Between 1,000 and 1,500 men captured from the column were bussed or marched to the Kravica Warehouse on the afternoon of 13 July 1995, and at around 18.00 hours, when the warehouse was full, the soldiers began throwing grenades and shooting into the men packed inside.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para205]

::: analysis an-execution-sites

At the Branjevo Military Farm the first buses arrived at 10 o’clock in the morning, the men were made to “get off in groups of ten” and lined up with their backs to the firing squad, and the executions continued until about 3 o’clock in the afternoon; Erdemović estimated about 20 buses of approximately 60 men and boys each, and he “believes that he personally killed about seventy people”.[^src-icty-erdemovic-sj para78] As a prosecution witness in the Krstić trial he said of the men in front of him: “When those men turned their backs to us, we shot at them. We were given orders to shoot.”[^src-icty-krstic-tj para234] At his own sentencing he told the chamber what he had been told when he refused: “If you’re sorry for them, stand up, line up with them and we will kill you too.”[^src-icty-erdemovic-sj para10]

### 4. The graves and the count

Commencing in 1996 the prosecution exhumed 21 gravesites; 14 were primary graves where bodies had been put directly after the killing, of which eight were later disturbed and the bodies reburied elsewhere, and seven of the exhumed sites were secondary burial sites.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para71] The reburials took place over several weeks in September and early October 1995, and the chamber read them as “a concerted campaign to conceal the bodies of the men in these primary gravesites”, prompted by international scrutiny.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para78]

::: analysis an-graves-disturbed

Investigators found “at least 448 blindfolds on or with the bodies uncovered during the exhumations at ten separate sites” and 423 ligatures at 13 separate sites, most of the ligatures made of wire, and the chamber held that “These ligatures and blindfolds are inconsistent with combat casualties”.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para75] The same chamber did not rule out that a percentage of the bodies in the graves examined were of men killed in combat.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para77] The minimum number of bodies in the graves exhumed was 2028, against a total the chamber put within the range of 7,000 to 8,000 men.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para80][^src-icty-krstic-tj para84]

::: exhibit ex-krstic-84

On 23 May 2024 the General Assembly designated 11 July an international day of commemoration and noted that at least 8,372 lives were lost, a figure above the tribunal's range of 7,000 to 8,000 and drawn from the identifications that continued after the tribunal's exhumations.[^src-un-ares78282 whole][^src-icty-krstic-tj para84] The resolution condemns without reservation any denial of the genocide as a historical event.[^src-un-ares78282 whole] The two counts are set beside the denial position's figure in the disagreements below, and this thesis states no single number.{i}

::: exhibit ex-memorial-2009

### 5. The verdicts

The Trial Chamber concluded that the intent to kill all the Bosnian Muslim men of military age in Srebrenica constitutes an intent to destroy the group in part and “therefore must be qualified as a genocide”.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para598] It found the prosecution had proven beyond all reasonable doubt that genocide, crimes against humanity and violations of the laws or customs of war were perpetrated against the Bosnian Muslims at Srebrenica in July 1995.[^src-icty-krstic-tj para599] The Appeals Chamber held that the forces “targeted for extinction the forty thousand Bosnian Muslims living in Srebrenica” and that the law “calls the massacre at Srebrenica by its proper name: genocide”.[^src-icty-krstic-aj para37] It set aside Krstić's conviction as a participant in a joint criminal enterprise to commit genocide, found him guilty of aiding and abetting genocide, and sentenced him to 35 years.[^src-icty-krstic-aj dispositif]

The chamber that tried Mladić found him not guilty of Count 1, genocide, guilty of Count 2, genocide, and guilty of persecution, extermination, murder, deportation, forcible transfer, terror, unlawful attacks on civilians and the taking of hostages, and sentenced him to life imprisonment.[^src-icty-mladic-tj para5214] The General Assembly's resolution recites eight tribunal judgments with guilty verdicts for genocide at Srebrenica and the judgment of the International Court of Justice of 26 February 2007 “in which the Court determined that the acts committed in Srebrenica constituted acts of genocide”.[^src-un-ares78282 whole] The Secretariat's report reproduces the words of the judge who confirmed the first indictment on 16 November 1995: “These are truly scenes from hell, written on the darkest pages of human history.”[^src-un-a54549 para2]

### 6. The reckoning

The Secretariat wrote that “Through error, misjudgement and an inability to recognize the scope of the evil confronting us, we failed to do our part to help save the people of Srebrenica from the Serb campaign of mass murder”.[^src-un-a54549 para503] Its cardinal lesson is that “a deliberate and systematic attempt to terrorize, expel or murder an entire people must be met decisively with all necessary means”.[^src-un-a54549 para502] Its judgment on the battalion is that it cannot be said with any certainty that stronger action by Dutchbat would have saved lives, and that such efforts could have done more harm than good.[^src-un-a54549 para473]

::: exhibit ex-a54549-503

The Supreme Court of the Netherlands held that Dutchbat's acts until 23:00 on 11 July 1995 were under the command and control of the United Nations and cannot be attributed to the State, that the State cannot be held liable for the capture of Srebrenica, and that it was wrongful not to offer the male refugees inside the compound the choice of staying there at the end of the afternoon of 13 July 1995.[^src-nl-hr-2019 sec5] It estimated the chance that those men would have escaped the Bosnian Serbs at 10%, and limited the State's liability to 10% of the damage to their surviving relatives.[^src-nl-hr-2019 sec5] In doing so it set aside the judgment of the Court of Appeal of The Hague of 27 June 2017.[^src-nl-hr-2019 sec6]

The Strasbourg court found that operations under Chapter VII are fundamental to the mission of the United Nations and that the Convention cannot subject the Security Council's acts and omissions to domestic jurisdiction without the accord of the United Nations.[^src-echr-mothers-2013 para154] It noted that the only instrument on which individuals could base a remedy against the United Nations for UNPROFOR's acts is the status agreement of 15 May 1993, Article 48 of which requires a claims commission, and that it would appear this has not been done.[^src-echr-mothers-2013 para162] It found the grant of immunity served a legitimate purpose and was not disproportionate, and declared the application inadmissible.[^src-echr-mothers-2013 para169][^src-echr-mothers-2013 dispositif]

The Dutch debate is held here only at its opening.{i} The parliamentary inquiry commission of January 2003 confirmed the motion's judgment ‘dat het NIOD een gedegen en evenwichtig onderzoek heeft verricht’.[^src-brouwer-2003 p1] In English, that the institute had carried out a thorough and balanced inquiry.{i} The institute's authors replied that the review articles differed sharply in tone and standpoint, and quoted the objection that the report was too long and came too late.[^src-blom-2003 p1] The denial position's own English summary says that the Army of Republika Srpska passed through the Muslim villages without leaving a killed civilian behind it, that it never meant to capture the town, and that the killings were prepared by the American, Bosnian and Croatian leaderships as the price of NATO bombing.[^src-atlagic-2021 p16] Its Serbian abstract puts the real number of Muslim victims at a fraction of the tribunal's range and calls the tribunal's figure exaggerated and politically constructed; the figures are set out in the disagreements below.[^src-atlagic-2021 abstract][^src-atlagic-2021 p1] The Serbian critical account names three official reports, of 2002, 2004 and 2021, and describes their conclusions as conflicting.[^src-pintar-2024 p37] The Bosniak scholarship counts 51 imams in the Srebrenica area in the Islamic Community's report of May 1995 and treats their killing from 1992 to 1995 as one segment of the genocide.[^src-karcic-2022 p2]

## What the record omits

- No source in this record was written by a soldier of the Netherlands battalion or by its commander, other than the messages the Secretariat's report quotes. {against=united-nations}
- No source in this record is an order, a log or an intercept of the Bosnian Serb army in its own words; the army is known here only as the tribunal describes it. {against=tribunal-record}
- No source in this record dates the order to move the bodies from the primary graves, only the weeks in which it was carried out. {against=tribunal-record}
- No source in this record is by a survivor of the column or of the execution sites in their own words; the survivors are known here through the chambers that heard them. {against=bosniak-scholarship}
- No source in this record is the text of either Republika Srpska report on Srebrenica; both are known here as one Serbian scholar summarises them. {against=serbian-critical}
- No source in this record is the text of the Dutch institute's report; it is known here through the Dutch courts' findings of fact and the reviews that answered it. {against=dutch-inquiry-debate}
- No source in this record is the judgment of the court that ruled on state responsibility in 2007; the General Assembly's recital of it is the only copy the free record reached. {against=united-nations}
