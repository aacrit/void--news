## TURN
H: Start with what the two columns share, because it is nearly everything. Both report the same ten thousand cases. Both cite the same official, Jean Kaseya, saying the same thing about funding. Both carry Uganda's insistence that its outbreak is contained, and both carry the cases in Kenya and Nigeria that put that insistence under scrutiny. The facts are not in dispute. The conclusions are.
H: The left column takes Kaseya's warning as its whole argument: the illness spreads like Covid unless more funding is secured. It does not say who should pay, how much, or to whom. A warning about scarcity that names no sum is an alarm, not a plan.
H: The right column rests on sovereignty: strict control of territory, regulated travel, no open-ended funding appeals. It names its own strongest objection, that infectious diseases recognize no borders, and answers it with a duty rather than a mechanism. The column does not say how a border is closed to a pathogen whose cases are already in Kenya and Nigeria.
H: Neither column names a border crossing, a checkpoint or a flight. Neither quotes anyone in Kenya or Nigeria.

## SECOND | Madrid to Seville
B: On Wednesday, rescue workers in Spain scoured the wreckage at the crash scene in Toledo province, where at least sixty-four people were killed when two high-speed trains collided. The high-speed service travelling from Madrid to Seville ran into the back of another train that had stopped, knocking two carriages off the track. Both trains were travelling in the same direction, on the line from the Spanish capital in the centre to Seville in the south-west of the country. It was the second deadly railway accident in the country this year.
A: The leader of the central region of Castilla-La Mancha, Emiliano Garcia-Page, told reporters at the scene that the death toll stood at approximately sixty-four, but warned that the number was probably going to grow even more. That raised a previous toll of thirty-one.
B: Evacuated passengers streamed along a road near the scene. Some were splattered with blood or had their wounds bandaged, while others wheeled luggage away from the derailed cars. By Thursday, outlets were updating their tracking of the collision. One hundred and seven sources carried the story on Wednesday, thirty on Thursday.
A: By Friday, when the Australian government announced that two of its citizens were among those affected, the count stood at sixty-nine dead and almost two hundred injured. The crash happened near the town of Urda, one hundred and thirty kilometres south of Madrid.
