# Feed QA, 2026-09-10: findings that are reports rather than patches

Three items here are analysis, not code. P0-C because four fixes have not
held and the CEO asked for the mechanism before a fifth. P1.1 and P1.2
because they were commissioned as written findings.

Everything below was measured against the 2026-09-10 production run, its
GitHub Actions log, the 1,377-story printed archive, and HTML fetched from
https://news.voidvision.org.

## P0-C: the pronoun scrubber alters what named people said

### What shipped

Served from production, in the Colombia gun-permit card:

> Rubio stressed the need for security in the region, claiming that the same
> networks, the same criminal groups that, although they commit their crimes
> here, they also commit crimes in the U.S., and they damage **their** people.

"Our people" became "their people". Prior instances: "never smoked a
cigarette in their life" (should be *her*), "regaining their independence"
(should be *his*), "keep their wife's name the hell out of your mouth"
(should be *my*).

### The current logic, in full

`_convert_first_person_outside_quotes` is step **3k**, the last content step
of `_apply_summary_postchecks` in `pipeline/summarizer/cluster_summarizer.py`.
It does three things:

1. Splits the summary on `_QUOTED_SPAN_RE`, which matches a **balanced** pair
   of double quotes, straight or curly.
2. Leaves every quoted span byte for byte.
3. On the text between spans, applies `_FIRST_PERSON_SUBS`: `we`/`we're`/
   `we've`/`we'll`/`we'd` to *they*, `our`/`ours`/`ourselves` to *their*,
   lowercase `us` to *them* (lowercase only, so the country "US" is safe), and
   `my` to *their*.
4. Since rev 65, a segment containing an unconvertible first person (`I`,
   `I'm`, `me`) is left entirely alone, so a sentence is never half converted.

Its premise is stated in the code: "After 3i (`_repair_orphan_quotes`) quotes
are balanced, so these spans are well-formed and pronouns inside them are
preserved."

### Why that premise is false

The three steps run back to back:

```
s = _drop_orphan_fragments(s)                  # 3h
s = _repair_orphan_quotes(s)                   # 3i
s = _convert_first_person_outside_quotes(s)    # 3k
```

3i does not balance an unbalanced quotation by adding the missing mark. It
balances it by **deleting the mark that is there**. Its own docstring says so:
"pair quotes left to right and REMOVE the unpaired ones (an orphan closer, or
an unclosed opener)".

So a quotation the generator left unclosed, which is the ordinary truncation
shape, has its opening quote deleted one line before the scrubber runs. What
reaches 3k is a verbatim quotation with no quotation marks, and 3k cannot tell
it from indirect speech. Reproduced directly:

| input | 3i leaves quotes | 3k output |
|---|---|---|
| `...they damage our people."` (balanced) | yes | **our people** survives |
| `...they damage our people.` (opening quote, no closer) | no | **their people** |

The second row is the string that shipped.

### Why four fixes have not held

All four addressed the substitution table: which pronouns it converts, and
what to do when it cannot convert one. None addressed the step that destroys
the evidence the table depends on. The scrubber is not failing to apply its
rule. It is applying its rule correctly to text that was a quotation until one
line earlier.

### Recommendation

The CEO's own framing is the right one: if the scrubber cannot reliably tell
quoted from unquoted text, it should not be rewriting people's words. It
cannot, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of tuning.

Two options, and I recommend the second.

**Option A, minimal.** Treat text following an unpaired opening quote as
quoted, and run 3k before 3i so the marks are still present. This keeps the
scrubber and closes the specific hole. It does not close the general one: any
future step that touches quotation marks re-opens it, and the failure is
silent, because after conversion there is no first person left for E-03 to
catch.

**Option B, recommended.** Retire the scrubber and handle first person at
generation time. E-03 is already ENFORCED, so a summary carrying a bare first
person fails validation at 8d.3 and is regenerated once with the rule named,
which is the loud path rather than the silent one. The cost is one extra flash
call on the cards that trip it. The benefit is that Void stops editing
attributed speech, which is the category of error that a discerning reader
treats as dishonesty rather than as a bug.

This is Block 5a, and it now has a named victim: the scrubber turned Ted
Cruz's "a traumatic experience for all of us" into "for all of them" on the
09-09 feed, and Rubio's "our people" into "their people" on 09-10.

**One change worth making either way:** 3i currently deletes quotation marks
silently, so the frequency of this cannot be measured after the fact. It
should log every removal. Today the only evidence a quotation was destroyed is
the corrupted output.

## P1.1: a national angle led an international story

> **Aleem Khan Represents Pakistan at Norway's King Harald V Funeral**, 23
> sources

Exactly **one of the cluster's 28 member headlines** carries that angle, from
The Nation (Pakistan). The rest are the funeral: Prince William, three Nordic
monarchs, Zelenskyy, the burial at Akershus.

### How the headline is chosen

Not by the summarizer. `_generate_cluster_title` in
`pipeline/clustering/story_cluster.py` picks one member's headline verbatim and
scores the candidates on length, a small set of penalties, and one substantive
term: `+2.0` for each of the cluster's top entities the headline contains.

Those top entities are counted over **`articles[:20]`**, the first twenty
members in list order, and only entities appearing at least twice qualify.

### The mechanism, demonstrated

On the identical 28 members, changing nothing but the order:

| article order | headline chosen |
|---|---|
| as stored | Thousands Line Streets For Royal Funeral of Norway's King Harald V |
| Aleem Khan article moved first | Aleem Khan represents Pakistan at funeral of Norway's King Harald V |

Moving one article to the front of the list changes the front page. With the
Pakistani article inside the first twenty, `pakistan` enters the top-entity set
and adds `+2.0` to that headline; outside it, it does not. The function's
docstring says it picks "the most representative headline", but nothing in it
measures how many members share the angle.

The same flip happens at every small cluster size, which is the likelier real
path: the cluster was titled while it was still four to thirteen articles, and
Phase 2 and 3 merges regenerate the title from the merged set, so whichever
window the Pakistani article landed inside decided it.

### What I tested and rejected

Four deterministic ways to prefer the international angle, all measured on
this cluster:

| approach | result |
|---|---|
| share of other headlines sharing any distinctive stem | Aleem 1.00, funeral 0.93. Fails: the shared words dilute it |
| weakest-stem support | Aleem 0.04, "Thousands Line Streets" 0.04. Fails: every headline has a rare word |
| entities counted over ALL members | removes the order dependence, but adds `pakistan` to the top set in both orders, so it makes the wrong answer *stable* |
| entity term weighted by share of members naming it | Aleem 3.22, funeral 3.15. Fails: all candidates carry the same high-frequency entities |

The words that make the Aleem headline wrong (`aleem`, `khan`, `pakistan`) and
the words that make "Thousands Line Streets" merely vivid (`line`, `street`)
are lexically indistinguishable. One names the subject, the other is colour.
Telling them apart needs syntax or meaning.

### Recommendation

Two changes, in this order.

1. **Count entities over all members, not `articles[:20]`.** This does not fix
   the Aleem card, and I would not claim it does. It fixes something worse: the
   front page currently depends on list order, so the same cluster can be
   headlined two ways on two runs with identical membership. Determinism first.

2. **Put the angle judgment where judgment lives.** The critique pass already
   asks the model L-07, whether a place named in the headline is contradicted
   by the summary. The natural sibling is: *the headline's subject is the
   story's subject, not one country's delegation to it*. That is a sentence a
   model answers reliably and a regex does not, and the pass already reads the
   card against its articles.

I would not tune the scorer further. Four variants, none separating, is enough
evidence that the signal is not in the words.

## P1.2: two cards on one Israel thread

> **#13 Nations Impose Sanctions on Israeli Settlements; US Considers Talks**,
> 21 sources
> **#17 Israel Orders UK to Close East Jerusalem Consulate in 30 Days**,
> 22 sources

### Did the gate evaluate the pair, and which branch did it fail

It did. Both were on the 35-cluster bench, so `should_merge` saw them, and it
rejected them at the **first conjunct**: fewer than the required two shared
specific stems. It never reached the temporal window or the anchor test.

It shared **zero**. That is a lower number than it should be, and chasing it
turned up a separate defect, now fixed on `claude/p0a-merge-specificity`:
"Israeli" reaches the stem lookup as `isra` while "Israel" reaches it as
`israel`, and `_DEMONYM` was keyed on the spelling `israeli`, so the mapping
never fired on any Israel pair. Repaired, the two headlines share exactly one
stem, `israel`, and the pair still fails the same conjunct.

So the gate's verdict is right, and it was right for a slightly wrong reason.

### My judgment: they should not merge

Sanctions imposed by seven governments on settlement goods, and Israel
expelling a British consulate, are two actions by different actors in
different places. They are causally linked and a day apart, which is a
thread, not an event.

The merge module's doctrine is that "a development and its direct consequence
are ONE event". I read the consequence here as having outgrown that: it has 22
sources of its own, more than the action it answers, and its own set of
actors. A merged card would have to be headlined with a semicolon joining two
subjects, and P0-A establishes that this shape is the signature of exactly the
contamination we are trying to stop. Merging these would manufacture, by
editorial choice, the thing the top card did by accident.

The CEO's real objection stands though: 10% of a 20-story feed on one thread
is too much, and #13's body already reports the consulate closure.

### Recommendation

Do not merge. Show the relationship instead. `printed_stories` already carries
`story_thread_id` and `continues_printed_id`, and nothing on the page uses
them. Two cards on one thread is defensible when the page says they are one
thread and lets the reader collapse them; it reads as duplication only when
the page is silent about it.

Failing that, the cheaper lever is the ranking, not the merge: the same-event
cap already demotes a second angle on one event, and a thread-aware version of
it would push #17 down without destroying it. Merging destroys a story;
demotion costs it a slot.
