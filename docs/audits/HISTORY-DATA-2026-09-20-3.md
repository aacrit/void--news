# History data audit, 2026-09-20, part 3 of 3

One of three parallel audits comparing every event's `data/history/events/*.yaml`
against its validated documentary script in `data/history/scripts/`. The scripts
are gate-checked and sourced, so where the two disagree the YAML is the likelier
error, and the page renders the YAML.

Moved here from a session scratchpad, which does not survive the session. The
evidence is the point: several findings in these tables were REFUTED on checking,
and a refutation is only reusable if the evidence behind the original claim
survives alongside it.

Findings acted on, and findings refuted, are recorded in the git history of
`data/history/events/` and in `docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md`.

The five P0s and the opium-wars/ottoman-empire fabricated-quote pattern were found by that method and by deep reads of a subset of events; a second pass targeting only `key_figures` wikipedia links (a `grep` for disambiguation parentheses) across the other 26 events, and only `notable_quotes.speaker` fields for non-name text, would be the highest-value next step if more auditing time is available.

## Summary

Audited 30 events. 17 are clean. Of the 13 with findings: **5 P0**, **7 P1**, **3 P2** (one event, peloponnesian-war, carries both a P0 and a P2; opium-wars and ottoman-empire also carry one P0/P1 each on top of the ones already counted — see table for exact per-event counts). The single worst finding is the pattern of **wrong-identity key-figure links**: `trail-of-tears`' "John Ross" and `treaty-of-waitangi`'s "Henry Williams" are both linked to, and dated from, entirely different historical people who happen to share the name — in the Ross case the file even contradicts its own media caption ("served... until 1866" vs. "died: 1856"). Combined with `opium-wars`' Lord Elgin (dead in 1841, credited with an act in 1860) and `peloponnesian-war`'s Lysander (born after his own recorded campaigns), this is a four-event pattern of key-figure identity/date corruption that a validator checking only quote-sourcing would never catch, since none of these are quotes — they're the birth/death fields on the page's key-figures cards.
